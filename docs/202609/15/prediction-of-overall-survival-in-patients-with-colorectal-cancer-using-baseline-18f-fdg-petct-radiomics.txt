# Prediction of overall survival in patients with colorectal 

# cancer using baseline  18 F-FDG PET/CT radiomics 

## Na Wang · Huanxin Zhu · Meng Dai · Yunuan Liu · Fenglian Jing · Yuhan Sun · 

## Jingmian Zhang · Jianfang Wang · Xinming Zhao  · Zhaoqi Zhang 

Received: 27 April 2026 / Accepted: 4 September 2026 

© The Author(s) 2026 

Abstract 

To predict 3-year overall survival (OS) in patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) using baseline  18 F-FDG PET/ 

CT radiomics. This retrospective study entrolled of 166 patients with colorectal cancer (CRC) patients, who 

were randomly assigned into a training cohort ( n = 133) and a validation cohort ( n = 33). The optimum subset 

was screened out from 2446 radiomic features via least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) 

Cox regression. Significant clinical characteristics identified by Cox analysis were combined with radiomic 

score (Radscore) to construct a combined prediction model. The Akaike information criterion (AIC), Harrell’s 

concordance index (C-index) and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) were applied 

to evaluate model performance. The complex model had the lowest AIC, highest C-index with optimal efficacy 

across both cohorts. In the training cohort, its AUC was superior to the single radiomics (0.877 vs. 0.784, 

p = 0.027) and clinical model (0.877 vs. 0.774,  p < 0.001), with no significant difference between the two alone 

(0.784 vs. 0.774,  p = 0.878). In the validation cohort, its AUC differed significantly from the clinical model 

(0.896 vs. 0.822,  p = 0.012). By contrast, no significant differences were found between the radiomics model 

and the complex model (0.707 vs. 0.896,  p = 0.094), nor between the radiomics model and the clinical model 

(0.707 vs. 0.822,  p = 0.400). Baseline PET/CT radiomics enables effective prediction of 3-year OS in patients 

with CRC. The complex model could further improve the predictive efficacy of the clinical model alone. 

Keywords  Colorectal cancer  · FDG  · PET/CT  · Radiomics  · Overall survival 

## Introduction 

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common cancers of the digestive tract, ranking the second leading 

cause of cancer-related death in the United States 1 and the fifth leading cause death in China 2, with a gradually 

younger age of onset 1–2. Earlier detection and new treatment strategies improve the overall survival (OS) of 

patients with CRC 3,4, but its mortality rate is still high, especially for metastatic CRC, whose 3-year OS rate is 

less than 40% 5. Therefore, more pretreatment features are urgently needed to predict the prognosis of patients 

with CRC and guide the clinical development of personalized treatment strategies. Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-70862-2 A major obstacle to the treatment of CRC is intratumoral heterogeneity, which represents the core challenge in 

cancer phenotype classification, recurrence, metastasis and treatment failure 6–9. As a novel medical image analy -

sis technique, radiomics can extract and analyze high-throughput quantitative features from medical images and 

has demonstrated great potential and application value in disease diagnosis, treatment assessment, and prognosis 

prediction 10 –12 . Furthermore, radiomics can characterize intratumoral heterogeneity and help clinicians identify 

high-risk patients early in the disease course, enabling the implementation of more aggressive treatment strate -

gies to, improve patient outcomes, Prolong the overall survival (OS), and enhance quality of life-a goal that is 

among the most important determinants of of patient prognosis 13 ,14 . Therefore, the development of robust sur -

vival prediction tools is of great clinical significance. 

Existing radiomics studies for CRC prognosis prediction mainly depend on single anatomical CT or MRI 

sequences. Single-modality CT/MRI radiomics can only capture morphological and structural tumor characteris -

tics but cannot reflect the glycolytic metabolic heterogeneity inside lesions, which is a key marker of malignant 

biological behavior of colorectal tumors 15 . In comparison, dual-modality ¹⁸F-FDG PET/CT perfectly integrates 

high-precision anatomical positioning information from CT and in vivo tumor metabolic activity signals from 

PET, which can provide more comprehensive multi-dimensional tumor phenotypic information and overcome 

the information limitation of single anatomical imaging 16 ,17 . However, few studies have thus far applied  18 F-FDG 

PET/CT radiomics to predict prognosis in CRC patients 16 ,18 , warranting further investigation and validation . 

The purpose of this study was to use pre-treatment  18 F-FDG PET/CT radiomics to predict 3-year OS in patients 

with CRC, stratify patients into high- and low-risk groups, and guide clinicians to formulate personalized treat -

ment plans. 

## Materials and methods 

Patients 

This retrospective study has been approved by the ethics committee of our institution (no. 2021KY292), which 

waived the requirement for patient informed consent due to the retrospective nature of this study, and therefore 

has been performed in accordance with the ethical standards laid down in the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and 

its later amendments. 

We retrospectively collected clinical and PET/CT imaging data from patients with stage I–IV colorectal adeno -

carcinoma diagnosed by histopathology between February 2014 and December 2021 who underwent  18 F-FDG 

PET/CT prior to treatment in our hospital. Patients who were found to have other primary malignancies, incom -

plete clinical data, or were lost to follow-up were excluded from the study. 

A dataset of 166 patients with colorectal adenocarcinoma was enrolled in the study, including 104 males and 62 

females (average age: [60.19  ± 12.09] years; age range: 26–86 years), who were randomly divided into the train -

ing cohort ( n = 133) and validation cohort ( n = 33) in a ratio of 8:2. The flowchart for patient selection is shown 

in Fig.  1.

All patients underwent re-evaluation after treatment, and follow-up was continued until September 16, 2024. 

All patients were followed up at least 33 months or until death. The primary endpoint of the study was 3-year OS. 

OS was defined as the time between the initial diagnosis of CRC and the patient’s death from any cause. 

PET/CT protocol and acquisition parameters 

All  18 F-FDG PET/CT examinations were performed on either a Philips Gemini GXL16 (143 patients, 86.1%) 

or a Philips Vereos (23 patients, 13.9%). Whole-body PET/CT imaging was performed at least 6  h after fasting 

and 60  min after intravenous injection of  18 F-FDG (3.7–5.5 MBq/kg). Supplementary Table  S1  online provides 

specific parameters of PET/CT image acquisition. Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-70862-2 Image segmentation and radiomics features extraction 

Two nuclear medicine physicians with 10 years of medical experience manually segmented lesion contours using 

the segmentation tool of 3D Slicer (version: 5.6.2,  https://www.slicer.org ). Original PET/CT raw acquisition 

and reconstruction parameters extracted from DICOM tags for the two scanners are detailed in Supplementary 

Table  1. Uniform harmonization and batch-effect correction were performed on all images to eliminate inter-

scanner bias caused by differences in crystal materials, PET reconstruction algorithms, matrix sizes and CT slice 

thickness between PHILIPS Gemini GXL16 and Vereos systems. Before segmentation and feature extraction, 

standardized preprocessing pipelines were performed in 3D Slicer 5.6.2: all PET and CT images were resampled 

to an isotropic 1  × 1 × 1 mm uniform voxel size via the Resample Scalar Volume module; B-spline interpolation 

was applied for PET/CT functional/anatomical images, while nearest-neighbor interpolation was used for tumor 

segmentation masks. PET images were fully normalized to standardized uptake value (SUV) via the built-in PET 

SUV Correction module relying on DICOM metadata including patient weight, injected ¹⁸F-FDG activity and 

post-injection scan delay time. Rigid multi-modal co-registration between CT (fixed volume) and PET (moving 

volume) was completed via the Elastix module with mutual information selected as the similarity metric. During 

tumor delineation, unified segmentation rules were followed: tumor boundaries were drawn combining CT ana -

tomical scope and PET hypermetabolic areas, and low-metabolic necrotic regions inside solid tumors were manu -

ally erased and excluded from all VOIs. Any inconsistent segmentation regions generated by the two physicians 

were jointly rechecked; unresolved discrepancies were adjudicated by a senior chief nuclear medicine physician. 

Sixty randomly selected cases were used to calculate intra-observer and inter-observer intraclass correlation coef -

ficients (ICCs) to quantify segmentation reproducibility. 

SlicerRadiomics was used to extract 2446 radiomics features (1223 from PET and 1223 from CT, respectively). 

Filtering parameters were preset within the SlicerRadiomics extension, including Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG, 

σ = 1 mm, 2  mm, 3  mm, 4  mm, 5  mm) and multi-scale wavelet transforms. Fixed bin-width gray-level discretiza -

tion (bin width  = 25) was adopted for PET SUV images before texture feature calculation. Radiomics features 

included shape and shape2D features extracted from the original images, firstorder features, texture features, 

which included gray level co-occurrence (GLCM), gray level dependence (GLDM), gray level run length matrix 

(GLRLM), gray level size zone matrix (GLSZM), and neighborhood gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM) 

extracted from the original, log-transformed or wavelet-filtered images. To eliminate the impact of dimensional 

differences and facilitate data analysis and comparison, all radiomics features were normalized by Z-scores.

Features selection and model establishment 

In the training cohort, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression analysis was 

employed to screen radiomics features associated with 3-year OS using lambda.min to retain predictive radiomics 

features, and their corresponding non-zero coefficients were used to calculate the radiomics score (Radscore) and 

establish radiomics model 19 .

Clinical characteristics (age, sex, tumor location, tumor node metastasis [TNM] stage, carcinoembryonic anti -

gen, [CEA], carbohydrate antigen 199, [CA199], carbohydrate antigen 724, [CA724]) were included in univari -

ate and Multivariate Cox regression analysis to select the optimal clinical variables associated with 3-year OS. 

In our study, TNM stage was coded as an ordinal numerical variable (stage I  = 1, stage II  = 2, stage III  = 3, stage 

IV  = 4), CEA, CA19-9 and CA72-4 were measured by chemiluminescence immunoassay. Tumor markers were 

dichotomized according to uniform reference thresholds for all participants: CEA  ≤ 5.0 ng/mL versus >  5.0 ng/ 

mL, CA19-9  ≤ 27.0 U/mL versus >  27.0 U/mL, and CA72-4  ≤ 6.90 U/mL versus >  6.90 U/mL. These cut-off val -

ues remained consistent throughout the study period. The selected variables were used to calculate the clinical 

score to build clinical model. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to calculate the complex score 

and establish the complex model based on the Radscore and the optimal clinical variables. 

Stratified Kaplan–Meier survival analyses based on individual TNM stages were performed in both cohorts to 

compare inter-stage survival differences, compensate for the limitations of ordinal TNM coding, and validate the 

progressive hazard trend across advancing tumor stages. 

Several common prognostic factors were not included in the baseline model for rigorous methodological rea -

sons. Molecular biomarkers ([Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog, KRAS], [Neuroblastoma RAS viral 

oncogene homolog, NRAS], [B-Raf proto-oncogene, serine/threonine kinase, BRAF], [Microsatellite Instability/ 

Mismatch Repair, MSI/MMR]), pathological features ([Lymphovascular Invasion, LVI], [Perineural invasion, 

PNI]), and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) scores were incompletely documented throughout the 

cohort. In addition, treatment regimens and postoperative status represent time-dependent variables after baseline 

PET/CT examination and are inappropriate for pretreatment modeling. Moreover, incorporating metastatic sites 

and resection status would induce severe multicollinearity with TNM staging. Limited sample size also prohib -

ited further stratified subgroup modeling to avoid overfitting. 

The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of the three models were drawn, and the patients were 

divided into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the optimal thresholds (Radscore, Clinical score, com -

plex score) determined by the Youden index of the ROC curves. 

Models evaluation and validation 

The Akaike information criterion (AIC) 20  and Harrell’s concordance index (C-index) 21  were employed to assess 

the goodness of fit and prediction performance of the three models in both the training and validation cohorts. A 

lower AIC value indicated a superior model fit. 

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was conducted to calculate the area under the curve (AUC), 

sensitivity, specificity and accuracy for predicting 3-year OS of three models. 

The DeLong non-parametric test (MedCalc19.0) was used to assess the significance of differences in area 

under the ROC curve (AUC) values among the three models in both the training and validation cohorts, thereby 

comparing their predictive performance. Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-70862-2 Nomogram construction, calibration assessment and decision curve analysis 

Based on multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis, a nomogram incorporating TNM stage 

and PET/CT Radscore was established to predict the 3-year overall survival (OS) of patients with colorectal 

adenocarcinoma. 

Calibration curves, calibration slope, calibration intercept and integrated Brier score (IBS, 0–36 months) were 

used to assess model calibration in both training and validation cohorts. A well-calibrated model presents a slope 

close to 1 and intercept close to 0; lower IBS represents smaller predictive error. 

Decision curve analysis (DCA) was further performed to evaluate the clinical net benefit of the nomogram. 

The net benefit of the model was compared with two extreme strategies (treat all patients/treat no patients). If the 

model curve lies above the two reference lines within a reasonable threshold range, the model possesses clinical 

value to guide individualized management. 

Statistical analysis 

IBM SPSS 26.0 and R 4.3.3 software ( http://www.R-project.org ) were used for statistical analyses. Normally 

distributed continuous variables were compared via independent sample t-test, and non-normal continuous vari -

ables were assessed using the Mann–Whitney U test. The Chi-square test was used for categorical variables (sex, 

TNM stage, CEA/CA199 status), with Fisher’s exact test adopted for limited sample sizes. The Kaplan–Meier 

(K–M) method and log-rank test were used to compare 3-year OS. A  p-value  < 0.05 was considered statistically 

significant.

## Results 

Patient characteristics 

The median OS of CRC patients in the training and validation cohorts was 50.3 and 50.5 months, respectively. 

At the last follow-up, 36 and 15 patients in the training ( n = 133) and validation cohorts ( n = 33) had died within 

3 years, respectively, yielding 3-year OS rates of 72.9% (97/133) and 54.5% (18/33), respectively. The clinical 

characteristics of all enrolled CRC patients are summarized in Table  1. Comparisons between the training and 

validation cohorts revealed no statistically significant differences in age, sex, tumor location, TNM stage, CEA, 

CA199 and CA724 (all  P > 0.05), suggesting balanced baseline characteristics. 

Intra-observer and inter-observer reproducibility of tumor segmentation 

The reliability analysis of tumor segmentation demonstrated excellent consistency. The intra-observer ICC 

reached 0.978, and the inter-observer ICC was 0.925. These results confirmed favorable repeatability of manual 

lesion delineation in this study. Article in Press Scientific Reports

Models evaluation and validation 

In the training cohort, the AIC values of the radiomics, clinical and complex models were 318.756, 311.123 

and 293.585, respectively, while in the validation cohort, their AIC values were 96.892, 87.611 and 83.288,

The primary objective of this single-center retrospective study was to use a PET/CT radiomics model, a clinical 

model, and a complex model that incorporates the PET/CT Radscore with the key clinical variable (TNM stage) 

to estimate 3-year OS in patients with CRC. The results showed that the PET/CT radiomics model achieved 

high accuracy in predicting 3-year OS probability in CRC patients, while the complex model exhibited the best 

predictive efficiency. In the training cohort, the complex model enhanced the predictive performance of both 

the radiomics model and the clinical model. However, in the validation cohort, it only improved the predictive 

performance of the clinical model. 

Of the 11 PET/CT radiomics features associated with 3-year OS, seven were wavelet signatures, one was origi -

nal_shape feature, one was an original_gray level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) feature, and two were LoG-

sigma features. The wavelet transform can decompose images into various frequency bands and analyze them 

at different scales. It is usually used in combination with traditional radiomics features such as GLCM and gray 

level run length matrix (GLRLM) to predict biological characteristics and prognosis of patients with lung cancer 

and CRC, demonstrating broad application prospects and important clinical value 5,22 . Tumor shape features can 

be reflected by original morphology, which helps deepen our understanding of internal tumor structure and char -

acteristics, thereby identifying biological properties such as heterogeneity and aiding in the prediction of patient 

prognosis 23 . GLCM plays an important role in radiomics: it can reveal image texture features, explore the spatial 

distribution of image pixels, reflect the internal heterogeneity, and is significantly associated with in rectal cancer 

patients 24 . Meanwhile, GLCM shows good reproducibility and robustness 25 . Laplacian of gaussian (LoG)-sigma 

features can quantify the complexity and contrast of image textures, thereby improving the characterization of 

key information metabolic activity information within lesions. They are often combined with shape features and 

GLCM, et al., assisting clinicians in making more accurate disease diagnoses and treatment plans 26 .

Our study showed that the Radscore could be used as an independent predictor of 3-year OS, and that it could 

indentify high-risk CRC patients with poor 3-year survival of CRC and assist in individualized treatment for 

these patients. We found that Radscore was negatively associated with 3-year OS in patients with CRC: survival 

was poorer when Radscore was higher than 0.023, which was consistent with previous studies 27 ,28 . Notably, this 

dual-modality PET/CT-based Radscore has obvious incremental advantages over conventional single CT or MRI 

radiomic signatures reported in prior literature. Single CT/MRI radiomics merely extracts structural texture infor -

mation and fails to quantify tumor glycolytic metabolism; these models are prone to underestimate intratumoral 

malignant heterogeneity and show limited predictive stability across all tumor stages 29 . By fusing anatomical and 

metabolic imaging features simultaneously, our PET/CT radiomic model captures dual-layer tumor phenotypic 

characteristics, thereby achieving more stable and powerful prognostic discrimination performance for stage I–IV 

CRC patients, which greatly complements the defects of single anatomical imaging radiomics in survival predic -

tion 30 . The established combined model yielded excellent predictive performance (C-index = 0.827), superior 

to a previous model relying on separate PET and CT signatures 16 . Our standalone PET/CT radiomic model also 

maintained stable discrimination in both training and validation cohorts. 

Complete disclosure of model equations greatly facilitates external validation by other research teams and 

accelerates the clinical translation of our radiomic predictive system. 

This study has inherent limitations due to its retrospective single-center design. Several pathological and 

molecular markers were not included owing to incomplete clinical records and inconsistent clinical testing. Nev -

ertheless, the Radscore remained an independent prognostic factor after adjusting for all available baseline vari -

ables, confirming that PET/CT radiomics captures unique metabolic and morphological heterogeneity beyond 

conventional clinical indicators. Future multicenter prospective studies with complete multi-dimensional clinical 

data are required to optimize and externally validate the model. 

It is widely recognized that TNM staging is closely associated with survival outcomes in patients with CRC. 

Patients with early-stage disease usually have a more favorable prognosis and longer survival, whereas those Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-70862-2 with late-stage disease have a poorer prognosis and shorter survival 31 . However, CRC exhibits significant hetero -

geneity; even among patients at the same stage, survival times can vary widely. Of the seven clinical variables 

evaluated in our study, only TNM stage was an independent predictor of 3-year OS in CRC patients. By contrast, 

some studies have indentified CA199, lymph node status, TNM stage and CEA as independent predictors of 

overall survival in patients with CRC. A clinical prediction model incorporating these four factors achieved a 

C-index of 0.716 for predicting OS in stage I–IV CRC 16 . However, CA199, lymph node status, and CEA were 

not validated as independent prognostic factors in the present study, which may be attributable to the characteris -

tics of the included patient cohort. In our study, a predictive model was constructed using TNM stage as the sole 

clinical variable. This model demonstrated high prognostic performance for predicting overall survival in stage 

I-IV CRC, both in the training cohort and the validation cohort, with accuracy comparable to that of the clinical 

model in the aforementioned study 16 . Several earlier radiomic studies have attempted to predict overall survival 

among colorectal cancer patients. Similar to these reports, our PET/CT radiomics-based nomogram demonstrated 

favourable prognostic performance 16 . Variations in imaging parameters, population characteristics and analyti -

cal pipelines complicate direct head-to-head performance comparisons across different works. Compared with 

existing studies, the present work combined radiomic signatures with TNM stage, performed both internal and 

external validation, and assessed clinical net benefit through DCA. Complete disclosure of model formulas fur -

ther improves the reproducibility of our prognostic tool. 

We have fully disclosed the scoring formulas of all prediction models in the manuscript. Complete formula 

reporting facilitates external validation by other researchers and promotes further clinical translation of this prog -

nostic tool. Our study optimized clinical risk stratification for CRC patients using a baseline PET/CT radiomics 

model or a complex model. These approaches can supplement and refine prognostic information, enabling a more 

precise characterization and improve of survival outcomes prediction in patientst with CRC. 

Some studies have shown that PET/CT radiomics can effectively stratify stage II and III CRC patients into 

high-risk and low-risk groups based on recurrence risk 18 . The present study demonstrated that whether the PET/ 

CT radiomics model, the clinical model, or the complex model was used, all three models successfully stratified 

stage I-IV CRC patients into high-risk and low-risk categories using K–M survival curves, clearly delineating 

patient survival status and predicting survival outcomes. This information is critical for guiding individualized 

treatment strategies and disease monitoring. 

Our calibration curve exhibited favourable calibration in the training cohort, whereas mild miscalibration was 

noted in the validation cohort. Even so, the IBS values remained acceptable, indicating limited predictive error. 

DCA demonstrated that the model yielded greater net clinical benefit than universal treatment or no intervention 

across wide threshold ranges in both cohorts. These results suggest that this PET/CT radiomics-based nomo -

gram could facilitate individualized risk stratification and clinical decision-making for patients with colorectal 

adenocarcinoma. 

The nomogram integrating TNM stage and PET/CT radiomic Radscore provides an accessible tool for individ -

ualized risk assessment in colorectal adenocarcinoma. Complementary to traditional TNM staging, the radiomic 

signature reflects tumour metabolic heterogeneity and enables more precise survival stratification. Satisfactory 

calibration and favourable net clinical benefit verified by DCA indicate that the model can guide personalized 

surveillance and treatment planning. Full disclosure of model formulas improves research reproducibility and 

supports further external validation. Future multicentre prospective investigations are essential to accelerate the 

clinical translation of this predictive model. 

Single-step LASSO regression was independently utilized for feature dimensionality reduction in the pres -

ent study. However, this approach has inherent drawbacks. LASSO tends to randomly select a single feature 

from highly correlated radiomic features, which may exclude potentially predictive imaging biomarkers and 

lead to unstable feature selection outcomes. Conventional multi-stage feature screening pipelines widely used 

in radiomics include univariate analysis, correlation filtering, dimensionality reduction algorithms and regres -

sion modeling. We compared their advantages and applicable scenarios with our single LASSO strategy. In this Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-70862-2 work, we adopted LASSO alone to simplify modeling procedures. Multi-stage screening will be implemented in 

follow-up research to enhance the stability of feature selection. 

This retrospective study had several limitations. First, All patients in this retrospective research were recruited 

from a single medical center, and no external independent cohort was included to verify the predictive perfor -

mance of our combined radiomics-clinical model. Single-center data may bring inherent selection bias, and the 

unified PET/CT scanning equipment, laboratory detection kits and clinical diagnostic standards in our institution 

may restrict the generalization ability of the established nomogram to other hospitals with different imaging plat -

forms and patient populations. Correspondingly, we supplemented the relevant future research perspective that 

multi-center external validation cohorts should be collected in subsequent studies to further confirm the robust -

ness and clinical transferability of our model. Our studies have shown that high PET radiomics scores are gener -

ally associated with larger tumor size and higher tumor metabolic load 27 . Meanwhile, several investigators have 

confirmed that metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) serve as important prognostic 

indicators for CRC 32 . We therefore propose integrating these conventional PET parameters into future multicen -

tre research. In the present study, we did not take into account basic PET metabolic parameters such as MTV and 

TLG. Therefore, whether combining PET/CT radiomics features with these metabolic parameters could improve 

the predictive efficacy of OS needs to be further explored. Second, numerous studies have reported that lymphatic 

infiltration, perineural invasion (PNI), KRAS mutation and other pathological factors are key factors associated 

with the poor prognosis in patients with CRC 33 –35 . Additionally, TNM stage was entered as an ordinal variable in 

the Cox regression, assuming uniform risk increases across successive stages. While stratified survival analyses 

supported progressive poor prognosis with higher stages, this statistical assumption might not hold. Dummy vari -

able coding will be considered in subsequent larger-scale research. However, these pathological factors were not 

included in this study and may be considered for inclusion in the future studies. 

## Conclusions 

Tumor analysis using baseline PET/CT radiomics techniques could effectively predict 3-year OS in patients with 

CRC. The complex model combining the Radscore and clinical characteristics improved the predictive efficacy 

of the clinical model and might provide clinical guidance for the development of personalized treatment for CRC 

patients.
