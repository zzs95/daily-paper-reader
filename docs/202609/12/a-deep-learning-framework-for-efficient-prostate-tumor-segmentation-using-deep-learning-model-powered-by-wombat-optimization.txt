Abstract 

Medical image segmentation plays a vital role in the early and precise detection of prostate cancer, which con -

tinues to be a major cause of morbidity among men. The research proposes a strong framework for prostate 

cancer segmentation that achieves high precision by combining deep learning and optimization methods. In 

order to improve data diversity and quality, the system first gathers and prepares prostate MRI datasets. Next, 

it performs sophisticated preprocessing operations as rotation, reshaping, Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram 

Equalization (CLAHE), and image flipping. For efficient learning and assessment, the pre-processed data is 

divided into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets. A new deep learning model designated as SwinDense-

GAM-UNet  + + is used for segmentation. It combines the advantages of Swin Transformer module for capturing 

long-range dependencies and enhance contextual understanding. Gradient Attention Modules (GAM) for real -

istic segmentation learning, dense connections for deeper gradient flow, and UNet  + + for hierarchical feature 

extraction. Furthermore, to enhance training efficiency and optimize model hyperparameters, the Wombat Opti -

mization Algorithm (WOA) is utilized. Python software is used to implement the proposed system for image 

processing, model training, and evaluation. The model’s performance is evaluated using common measures like 

as recall, accuracy, and precision. The proposed structure offers a potential alternative for clinical diagnostic 

support, as evidenced by the results, which show that it performs noticeably better than current models in 

prostate cancer segmentation tasks.

## Introduction 

In the male reproductive system, the prostate is a tubulo-alveolar gland made up of stromal and epithelial cells. 

In adults, it is divided into four zones with varying amounts of glandular tissue. The central zone contains about 

25% epithelium, whereas the peripheral zone exceeds 70%. By contrast, the transitional and periurethral zones 

have few secretory glands but progressively undergo hyperplastic enlargement with age. Adenocarcinoma, the Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69388-4 predominant form of prostate cancer, arises within this gland and typically exhibits slow initial growth, although 

more aggressive variants can metastasize rapidly 1. Accurately distinguishing indolent tumours from high-risk 

lesions is therefore critical to avoid both overtreatment and undertreatment 2. As the second most common disease 

diagnosed globally and the fifth largest cause of cancer-related deaths among men, prostate cancer poses a serious 

threat to global health. The need for efficient detection and localisation methods is important due to its insidious 

development and asymptomatic early presentation 3,4. To visualise prostate anatomy and tumour areas, Magnetic 

Resonance Imaging (MRI) provides unmatched soft tissue contrast and multi-parametric capabilities, nonethe -

less, manual delineation is still time-consuming and susceptible to inter-observer variability. Consequently, the 

development of automated, precise and effective segmentation models is essential for directing biopsy and treat -

ment planning, increasing diagnostic consistency and eventually improving patient outcomes 5,6. Hand-crafted 

features and traditional image-processing pipelines were the mainstays of early computer approaches to prostate 

segmentation. While region-growing and thresholding procedures were simple, they were fragile when con -

fronted with anatomical heterogeneity and image noise 7. To increase resilience, active shape and active contour 

models used prior shape information, nonetheless, they had trouble with weak borders and intensity inhomoge -

neities 8. Atlas-based registration transferred labels by aligning patient pictures with template anatomies, but it 

was time-consuming and deformable, and it failed when there was a lot of inter-patient variability 9. As Machine 

Learning (ML) advanced, Support Vector Machine (SVM) 10  and Random Forest (RF) 11  trained on texture and 

intensity histograms offered small improvements, but they still mainly relied on feature engineering. 

The introduction of Deep Learning (DL) transformed the segmentation of medical images by facilitating end-

to-end feature learning. The symmetric encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections that U-Net offered 

sparked a wave of CNN-based techniques that produced state-of-the-art outcomes on the PROMISE12 and NCI-

ISBI challenges 12 . Subsequent works enriched this backbone with multi-scale context aggregation via atrous 

convolutions 13 , dense connectivity to promote gradient flow 14  and attention gates to highlight salient prostate 

regions 15 . Nevertheless, CNNs are limited in their capacity to simulate long-range relationships that are neces -

sary for capturing global prostate morphology because of their intrinsic focus on small receptive fields. Over, 

the past five years, several transformative trends have emerged. Initially created for natural language process -

ing, transformer-based models have been modified for use in medical imaging. Self-attention layers are used in 

place of convolutional blocks in Vision Transformer (ViT) and its variations, providing a global receptive field 

at a high computational cost 16 . To mitigate this, hierarchical versions such as Swin Transformer employ shifted 

window self‐attention for linear complexity while preserving cross‐window interactions 17 . These designs suc -

cessfully model both local texture and global shape signals, resulting in higher performance in whole-gland and 

zonal segmentation challenges 18 . Techniques for domain generalisation and adaptation deal with cross-site and 

cross-scanner variability, which is a major obstacle to clinical translation. Adversarial style transfer aligns feature 

distributions across domains, while causal inference frameworks disentangle anatomical content from imaging 

style factors 19 ,20 . Initialisation parameters that quickly adjust to new domains with little fine-tuning are learnt via 

meta-learning techniques 21 . Together, these tactics lessen performance deterioration when models are applied to 

unknown datasets, improving generalisability. 

Self-supervised and semi-supervised learning lessens the need for expensively curated huge annotated data -

sets. Pseudo-labelling and consistency regularization incorporate unlabelled data to refine decision boundaries, 

while contrastive learning pretrains encoders on proxy tasks such as patch discrimination 22 ,23 . These models are 

as accurate as their fully supervised counterparts when adjusted with sparse annotations. Additionally, trans -

former backbones pretrained with self-supervised objectives display strong feature representations that translate 

effectively to segmentation functions 24 .Hybrid architectures integrate CNN and transformer modules to com -

bine the strengths of both. CNN encoders, for instance, record fine-grained texture information, whereas trans -

former decoders compile global context 25 . GAM further refine spatial and channel attention by using edge-aware 

cues, enhancing boundary delineation in low-contrast regions 26 . Dense UNet + + structures reduce the semantic 

gap between encoder and decoder by gradually fusing multi-scale data through nested skip connectivity 27 . To Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69388-4 optimise complex model configurations, advanced hyperparameter and architectural search techniques have been 

implemented. Bayesian optimization and reinforcement learning facilitate automated selection of layer depths, 

filter sizes and learning rates 28 . Bio-inspired metaheuristics such as the Wombat Optimization Algorithm (WOA) 

simulate animal foraging and predator-escape behaviours to balance global exploration and local exploitation in 

high-dimensional search spaces 29 . These optimisers identify robust configurations that are more generalisable 

across datasets and speed up convergence. 

Thereby, a novel end-to-end DL framework, SwinDense-GAM-UNet  + + powered by the Wombat Optimiza -

tion Algorithm, is introduced for precise prostate tumour segmentation in this work. A hierarchical Swin Trans -

former encoder captures both local textures and global context via shifted-window self-attention, while densely 

nested UNet  + + decoder blocks fuse multi-scale features. Gradient Attention Modules use gradient-enhanced 

cues to sharpen lesion boundaries and WOA systematically fine-tunes hyperparameters by emulating wombat 

foraging and escape strategies to achieve an optimal balance between global exploration and local refinement. 

The proposed SwinDense-GAM-UNet  + + framework is designed by integrating complementary modules 

that address different challenges in prostate cancer segmentation. The Swin Transformer effectively captures 

long-range spatial dependencies and global contextual information, which are essential for identifying complex 

tumor structures in MRI images. UNet  + + provides robust encoder-decoder architecture with nested skip connec -

tions that facilitate multi-scale feature fusion and accurate localization of lesion boundaries. The GAM further 

enhances segmentation performance by emphasizing diagnostically relevant regions and suppressing redun -

dant background information, thereby improving feature discrimination. Dense connections are incorporated 

to strengthen feature reuse and gradient propagation, reducing the risk of information loss during deep network 

training. The novelty of the proposed framework lies in the unified integration of Swin Transformer, GAM, dense 

connectivity and UNet  + + within a single architecture, enabling simultaneous exploitation of global context, local 

details, attention-guided learning and efficient feature transfer, which has not been extensively explored for pros -

tate cancer segmentation. Furthermore, the WOA is employed to optimize critical hyperparameters because of 

its strong balance between exploration and exploitation, adaptive search capability and reduced tendency to 

become trapped in local optima. Compared with conventional optimizers and many metaheuristic algorithms,

## System modelling 

Dataset description 

The Medical Segmentation Decathlon (MSD), a benchmark project that seeks to promote research in the field of 

generalisable medical picture segmentation across numerous anatomical sites and imaging modalities, provides 

the publicly available Task05_Prostate dataset, which is used in the proposed work. Task05_Prostate specifically 

focusses on multi-region segmentation of the prostate gland from T2-weighted MRI, which is a common imag -

ing technique that is especially useful for seeing the structure of the prostate due to its strong soft tissue contrast. 

The dataset includes 48 MRI volumes that have been collected from several institutions, guaranteeing variation 

in imaging quality and acquisition methods, which in turn enhances the generalisability and robustness of the 

model. For supervised training, 32 of the total subjects have manually annotated ground truth labels, whereas 16 

cases serve as test data without annotations in accordance with the blinded evaluation methodology of the chal -

lenge. Each image volume is provided in NIfTI (.nii.gz) format, which supports 3D volumetric data storage along 

with essential metadata such as voxel dimensions and spatial orientation. The primary segmentation task is to 

delineate two anatomically distinct regions within the prostate gland: 

● Class 1: Peripheral Zone (PZ). 

● Class 2: Transition Zone (TZ). 

These zones are important from a clinical standpoint because prostate cancer frequently starts in the periphery 

and spread to the transition zone. As a reflection of scanner variability in the real world, the pictures range in 

resolution and voxel spacing, usually having in-plane diameters of 0.5–0.6  mm and slice thicknesses of 3–4  mm. 

Although additional augmentation and normalisation steps are frequently used during model training, the data is 

pre-aligned and resampled during preprocessing in the MSD challenge. 

● PROMISE12 dataset elaborated as PROstate MR Image Segmentation 2012 is also adopted in this work. The 

task is whole prostrate gland segmentation and the imaging modality is T2-weighted MRI. The total number of 

classes included is 80 among which 50 is used for training and 30 is used for testing. 

Pre-processing 

The MRI volumes from the Task05_Prostate dataset are subjected to a multi-stage pre-processing procedure in 

order to increase input quality and model generalisation. Each 3D MRI scan in NIfTI format is first divided into 

two-dimensional axial slices. Because patient scans vary in spatial dimensions, bicubic interpolation is used 

to reformat each slice to a consistent resolution of 256  × 256 pixels. The compatibility with the input layer of 

the SwinDense-GAM-UNet  + + network is guaranteed by this resizing procedure.Geometric augmentation tech -

niques including random rotation, horizontal image flipping and random cropping are used to artificially increase 

the training data and provide diversity. Particularly in situations where the prostate gland’s position and anatomy 

vary, these augmentations allow the model to acquire invariant properties and lessen overfitting. CLAHE is used 

to standardise the image intensity after spatial normalisation.In T2-weighted MRI slices, where soft tissue bor -

ders appear faint or hazy, CLAHE is very useful at boosting local contrast. 

The input image and parameter selection are the first steps in the CLAHE process, as seen in Fig.  2. Local 

control modification is applied to the input, and each contextual region is then processed to provide grey-level 

mappings. The improved output image is then created by interpolating these mappings, which suppresses noise Article in Press Scientific Reports 

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-69388-4 amplification while maintaining edge information. The voxel intensity values are scaled to the [0, 1] range using 

min-max normalisation, which promotes steady convergence throughout model training. After that, the dataset is 

split into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets, ensuring that the Peripheral Zone (PZ) and Transition Zone (TZ), 

two labelled prostate regions, are equally represented. By ensuring that the input data is clean, contrast-enhanced, 

geographically standardised, and robustly augmented, this meticulously designed preprocessing pipeline makes 

it possible for the proposed model to extract discriminative features more effectively. 

The proposed preprocessing pipeline significantly enhances segmentation performance by improving image 

quality, increasing data diversity and reducing variations in MRI acquisition conditions. Resizing all slices to 

a uniform resolution of 256  × 256 pixels ensures consistent spatial representation and facilitates stable feature 

learning across the dataset. Data augmentation techniques such as random rotation, horizontal flipping and ran -

dom cropping increase the effective size of the training dataset, thereby reducing overfitting and improving model 

generalization. Furthermore, CLAHE enhances local contrast and emphasizes prostate boundaries, enabling the 

network to better distinguish the PZ, TZ and surrounding tissues. Min-max normalization standardizes intensity 

distributions and accelerates convergence during training. Quantitatively, the inclusion of the preprocessing stage 

resulted in noticeable improvements across all evaluation metrics.

The Swin Transformer serves as the main feature extraction backbone of the proposed SwinDense-GAM-UNet++, 

which captures long-range spatial dependencies while maintaining computational efficiency. By implementing a 

hierarchical attention-based mechanism that permits both global context modelling and fine-grained local feature 

extraction, the Swin Transformer overcomes significant drawbacks of conventional CNNs. This is essential for 

intricate medical image segmentation tasks like multi-region prostate gland delineation. Figure  3 depicts the Swin 

Transformer block’s fundamental architecture. Window-based Multi-head Self-Attention (W-MSA) and Shifted 

Window Multi-head Self-Attention (SW-MSA) are the two successive components that make up each block. 

Both components are set up with residual connections to allow for deeper model training and steady gradient 

flow. To stabilise the input distribution, the block starts with a Layer Normalisation (LN) step. After that, it goes 

through a W-MSA or SW-MSA module, which determines attention across shifted windows or inside local win -

dow regions, respectively. The attended features are then refined using a feed-forward Multi-Layer Perceptron 

(MLP) module and another LN. Because it facilitates cross-window information flow, the second block’s usage 

of shifted windows is especially crucial for improving spatial continuity and simulating non-local interactions 

between distant anatomical features in the image. 

Figure  4 shows the complete hierarchical structure of the Swin Transformer. A patch partitioning method is 

used to first divide the input image into fixed-size patches. After being flattened, each patch is projected into a 

high-dimensional space appropriate for attention processing using a linear embedding layer.Each of the four 

stages that make up the model architecture consists of a collection of patch merging layers and Swin Transformer 

blocks. Patch merging gradually increases channel dimensions while decreasing spatial resolution. Multi-scale

The hierarchical Swin Transformer encoder effectively addresses several limitations of previous conventional 

segmentation models. Traditional architectures rely on local convolution operations with limited receptive fields, 

making it difficult to capture long-range dependencies and global anatomical relationships that are essential for 

accurate prostate cancer segmentation. As a result, these models may struggle to distinguish tumor boundar -

ies when lesions exhibit heterogeneous appearance, low contrast or complex spatial structures. The proposed 

hierarchical transformer encoder overcomes this limitation through its self-attention mechanism, which enables 

the model to learn interactions between distant image regions and capture rich global contextual information. 

Furthermore, the multi-stage hierarchical design generates feature representations at different spatial resolutions, 

allowing the network to simultaneously preserve fine-grained boundary details and high-level semantic infor -

mation. The shifted-window attention strategy further facilitates cross-window communication, preventing fea -

ture isolation and improving spatial continuity across the image. Consequently, the Swin Transformer encoder 

enhances feature representation, improves lesion localization and provides greater robustness under challenging 

imaging conditions compared with conventional segmentation frameworks.

The dense nested decoder of UNet  + + effectively addresses one of the major limitations of previous encoder-

decoder segmentation models, namely the semantic gap between low-level encoder features and high-level 

decoder representations. In conventional U-Net architectures, direct skip connections transfer encoder features 

to the decoder without sufficient feature alignment, which can lead to inaccurate fusion of spatial details and

maps compared with traditional U-Net and other conventional encoder–decoder architectures.

pared with manually tuned or conventionally optimized architectures.
