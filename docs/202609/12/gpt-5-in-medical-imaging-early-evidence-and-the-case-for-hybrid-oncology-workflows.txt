## Abstract

GPT-5 introduces enhanced multimodal reasoning capabilities, with potential relevance to medical imaging. This commentary interprets early evidence from seven evaluations of GPT-5 across oncologic and diagnostic imaging and clinical reasoning tasks and argues for a hybrid deployment workflow. On reasoning-intensive and structured question-answering tasks, GPT-5 improves over GPT-4o, with the largest gains in multimodal reasoning, calculation, and examination-style benchmarks. However, performance on fine-grained perceptual tasks remains limited. In mammography, zero-shot GPT-5 substantially trails human readers and domain-specific models, and in brain tumor MRI, it reaches only moderate accuracy, with no model clearly outperforming the others. Confident hallucinations, prompt sensitivity, and three-dimensional spatial reasoning constraints persist as critical failure modes, and gains observed in examination-style benchmarks do not consistently translate to applied clinical scenarios. The evidence supports a hybrid workflow in which specialized tools perform detection, segmentation, and quantification while GPT-5 acts as an integrative reasoning layer over structured findings and clinical context. This keeps its reasoning grounded in tool-derived inputs rather than acting as an autonomous diagnostic agent that can generate unreliable findings. In oncology, the most plausible applications are response assessment, surveillance, and treatment sequencing over tool-derived inputs, where errors such as a misclassified response or an unsupported plan make uncertainty signaling and clinician oversight prerequisites. Educational use is a lower-risk entry point, while clinical decision support requires prospective validation and regulatory scrutiny before integration into patient care.
