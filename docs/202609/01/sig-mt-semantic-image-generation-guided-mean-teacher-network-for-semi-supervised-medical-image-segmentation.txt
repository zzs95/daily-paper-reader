## Abstract

Deep learning has advanced medical image segmentation but depends on expensive, expert-annotated data. While semi-supervised learning reduces this need, its current methods risk misleading the model with limited or low-quality labels, failing to fully exploit the valuable information in unlabeled data. In this paper, a novel Semantic Image Generation-guided Mean Teacher network (SIG-MT) for semi-supervised medical image segmentation was proposed. First, a Semantic Image Generation based Consistency Strategy (SIGCS) was adopted based on the Mean Teacher (MT) framework, which constrains the segmentation model by constructing a semantic image generation based consistency loss by using the generated results. Second, to better support semantic image generation in medical images, we design a novel SIG model as the backbone of the generation network, which improves feature fusion and produces high-quality reconstructed images. Third, experiments on the LA and 3D Vessel datasets show that SIG-MT achieves competitive or superior performance compared with existing semi-supervised methods, especially under limited labeled-data settings. Additional plug-in experiments suggest that SIGCS can provide complementary gains for several semi-supervised frameworks, while its effectiveness is method-dependent, especially for sparse vessel segmentation.

## Introduction

Medical image segmentation can segment important organs or lesions from medical images, and plays an indispensable role in computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. In recent years, many encoder-decoder based networks have achieved great success in the field of medical image segmentation [6], [7], [8]. However, the success of these methods relies heavily on a large amount of high precision pixel-level labeled data, while in practice, labelling medical images is expensive and time-consuming, rendering it almost impossible to construct large medical datasets with corresponding labels. One of the reasons for this is the poor quality of the visual effects often exhibited by medical images, which is a consequence of low contrast and noise interference. In contrast, semi-supervised learning utilizes a small amount of labeled data and a large amount of unlabeled data to achieve joint training, which can also achieve good results [9], [10]. Obviously, in medical image segmentation, semi-supervised learning is more important than supervised learning, and more in line with the requirements of actual clinical scenarios.

The main semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods can be roughly classified as consistency regularization [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16] and pseudo-labeling [17], [18]. Pseudo labeling based methods mainly follow the self-training pipeline, which are built upon cluster assumption, i.e., the cluster of each class should be compact and thus of low entropy [19]. In general, these methods first predict pseudo labels on unlabeled data and then blend them with labeled data to retrain the model [20]. Consistency regularization based methods primarily rely on the smoothness assumption, i.e., small perturbations of an input should not produce the obvious corresponding deviations [21]. In this paper, we mainly consider consistency regularization. One of the most representative methods is the Mean Teacher Network (MT) [11], which utilizes the perturbation-based consistency loss of the teacher model and the student model on unlabeled data as well as the supervised loss on labeled data to jointly train the model. The subsequent improvements focus on selecting different data perturbations and feature perturbations to achieve performance gains [13], [22], [23].

However, using a small amount of labeled data in semi-supervised image segmentation leads to the fact that these networks cannot fully utilize unlabeled data merely by using consistency regularization, the application of semi-supervised learning in the case with insufficient labeling and low data quality may potentially mislead the model. To address this issue, many works have added different training strategies to enhance the feature extraction ability of the model, such as multi-task learning strategies [24], [25], adversarial learning strategies [26], [27], [28] and contrast learning strategies [29], [30]. Recently, Chen et al. [31] proposed a generative consistency for semi-supervised (GCS) model, which utilizes a generation network to reconstruct images from segmentation probability maps and applies consistency constraints to improve feature representation. The GCS model demonstrates promising results in cerebrovascular segmentation from TOF-MRA data. However, this approach is specifically designed for a single modality and anatomical structure (cerebrovascular), which may limit its generalizability to other medical segmentation tasks. Additionally, the GCS model does not incorporate a dedicated semantic image generation network or integrate with modern semi-supervised frameworks, potentially restricting its ability to fully leverage unlabeled data in complex scenarios.

In this paper, we propose a novel Semantic Image Generation-guided Mean Teacher network (SIG-MT) for semi-supervised medical image segmentation. The core of SIG-MT is a newly designed Semantic Image Generation based Consistency Strategy (SIGCS), which integrates a dedicated generation model into the robust Mean Teacher (MT) framework. The MT framework enhances training stability and prediction consistency by maintaining a teacher model through exponential moving average of the student model weights, effectively leveraging unlabeled data under different perturbations. Building upon this, SIGCS further exploits the intrinsic prior relationships within unlabeled data by constraining the segmentation network through a generative consistency loss between the original image and the reconstructed output from the generation model. To enable high-fidelity image reconstruction, we introduce a novel Semantic Image Generation (SIG) model as the backbone of the generation network. The SIG model is specifically designed to preserve and fuse semantic information during upsampling, thereby producing high-quality images that reinforce the segmentation network’s feature extraction capability.

The contributions of this study are as follows:

*   •
We propose a novel Semantic Image Generation-guided Mean Teacher network (SIG-MT), which adopts a Semantic Image Generation based Consistency Strategy (SIGCS) built upon the Mean Teacher (MT) framework. SIGCS introduces an auxiliary generative network that reconstructs the input image from the segmentation probability map. By minimizing the mean squared error between the original and reconstructed images, SIGCS leverages the intrinsic consistency within unlabeled data, which not only reduces the model's susceptibility to noisy pseudo-labels but also encourages the segmentation network to yield higher-quality predictions.

*   •
We design a new Semantic Image Generation model (SIG), which enhances contextual feature fusion and preserves semantic information during image reconstruction. The SIG model introduces the spatially-adaptive denormalization (SPADE) block to avoid the loss of semantic information during upsampling. The SIG model effectively improves the feature extraction capability of the SIGCS and avoids network overfitting.

*   •
We validate SIG-MT on two public 3D medical image segmentation datasets and compare it with representative MT-style and generative consistency-based methods. Additional plug-in experiments are provided to examine both the extensibility and limitations of SIGCS when integrated into other semi-supervised frameworks, showing that its effectiveness depends on the target structure and the original semi-supervised strategy.
