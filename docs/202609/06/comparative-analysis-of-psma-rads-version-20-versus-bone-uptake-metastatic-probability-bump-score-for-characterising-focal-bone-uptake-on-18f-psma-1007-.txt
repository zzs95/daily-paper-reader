Title: Comparative analysis of PSMA-RADS Version 2.0 versus bone uptake metastatic probability (BUMP) score for characterising focal bone uptake on [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT in prostate cancer – a multicentre evaluation

URL Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0

Markdown Content:
## Abstract

### Purpose

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET/CT is widely used in prostate cancer management, but unspecific bone uptake (UBU) remains a diagnostic challenge particularly with [18F]PSMA-1007. Bone uptake metastatic probability (BUMP) score - a composite imaging-clinical model, was compared with PSMA Reporting and Data System (PSMA-RADS, Version 2) for predicting skeletal metastases in UBU.

### Methods

A real-world cohort of [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT scans performed at three tertiary cancer centres was retrospectively analysed. Bone lesions with focal PSMA uptake were evaluated. SUV max, CT-derived mean Hounsfield unit and androgen deprivation therapy status were recorded to calculate BUMP score for each lesion. Lesional PSMA-RADS scores were assigned by experienced radiologists. Ground truth was established using follow-up imaging, prostate-specific antigen (PSA) kinetics and multidisciplinary consensus. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses assessed diagnostic performance.

### Results

130 patients (median age 70 years) with 412 bone lesions were analysed; 60.9% (_n_ = 251) of lesions were metastatic. Median SUV max was higher in malignant than benign lesions: 7.54 (Interquartile range (IQR) 10.3) versus 3.53 (IQR 1.51). BUMP achieved an AUROC of 0.812 (95% CI: 0.636—0.931), the optimal threshold was 0.134 yielding an F1 score of 0.800. PSMA-RADS v2.0 outperformed BUMP with higher sensitivity and specificity (98% and 96%, versus 74% and 80%). In equivocal PSMA-RADS Score 3 lesions (_n_ = 44), BUMP showed reduced discrimination (AUROC 0.61, 95% CI 0.457–0.781).

### Conclusion

BUMP had good predictive ability for classifying bone metastases but underperformed compared with human-led PSMA-RADS assessment. For equivocal lesions BUMP offered limited added value, highlighting the importance of expert interpretation.

## Introduction

Prostate cancer is the second commonest cancer in men and the fourth most common cancer overall worldwide. There were an estimated 1,467,854 new cases diagnosed and 397,430 deaths globally in 2022 [[1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR1 "Schafer EJ, Laversanne M, Sung H, Soerjomataram I, Briganti A, Dahut W, et al. Recent patterns and trends in global prostate cancer incidence and mortality: an update. Eur Urol. 2025;87:302–13. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2024.11.013
                
              .")]. Incidence is projected to increase dramatically with new cases expected to rise from 1.4 million in 2020 to 2.9 million by 2040, driven primarily by ageing populations and improved life expectancy [[2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR2 "James ND, Tannock I, N’Dow J, Feng F, Gillessen S, Ali SA, et al. The Lancet Commission on prostate cancer: planning for the surge in cases. Lancet. 2024;403:1683–722. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(24)00651-2
                
              .")]. Accurate staging at diagnosis and biochemical recurrence is critical for guiding treatment decisions, monitoring response and estimating prognosis. The introduction of prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PSMA PET/CT) has significantly improved detection of primary and metastatic disease, offering superior sensitivity and specificity compared with conventional imaging modalities such as bone scintigraphy and CT alone [[3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR3 "Daniels H, Gilbert R, Bonin L. The diagnostic accuracy of 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT versus 99mTc-MDP bone scintigraphy for identifying bone metastases in persons with prostate cancer: A systematic review. J Med Imaging Radiat Sci. 2023;54:545–55. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmir.2023.04.005
                
              .")]. In addition, PSMA PET/CT has shown the potential to significantly reduce radiation exposure with fewer equivocal results when compared to conventional imaging with CT and bone scintigraphy. Among available radiotracers [18F]PSMA-1007 has gained increasing clinical adoption, demonstrating excellent biodistribution characteristics enabling assessment of the prostate and pelvic region without urinary interference. The tracer demonstrates high tumour-to-background ratio with higher spatial resolution and a longer half-life compared to 68 Ga-labelled PSMA tracers [[4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR4 "Mazzone E, Thomson A, Chen DC, Cannoletta D, Quarta L, Pellegrino A, et al. The role of prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography for assessment of local recurrence and distant metastases in patients with biochemical recurrence of prostate cancer after definitive treatment: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Eur Urol. 2025;88:129–41. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2025.05.006
                
              .")].

Despite these advantages, unspecific bone uptake (UBU) on [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT is more prevalent with this radiopharmaceutical and represents a persistent and clinically significant diagnostic challenge. UBU is defined as focal bone radiotracer uptake without definitive morphological correlate on CT, or uptake patterns insufficient to confidently distinguish between benign processes and true skeletal metastases. The clinical prevalence of UBU on PSMA PET/CT is substantial, with studies reporting rates of between 19.8–36.7% of patients having at least one indeterminate lesion [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              ."), [6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR6 "Phelps TE, Harmon SA, Mena E, Lindenberg L, Shih JH, Citrin DE, et al. Predicting outcomes of indeterminate bone lesions on 18F-DCFPyL PSMA PET/CT scans in the setting of high-risk primary or recurrent prostate cancer. J Nucl Med. 2023;64:395–401. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.122.264334
                
              .")]. Clinical consequences of this uncertainty are multifaceted. Equivocal findings frequently necessitate additional imaging studies, prolonged follow-up protocols and can result in delayed treatment initiation, increased healthcare costs and patient anxiety. More critically, false-positive misinterpretation of UBU can lead to inappropriate upstaging resulting in unnecessary systemic therapy or exclusion from potentially curative treatments. False-negative interpretations may delay necessary interventions for truly metastatic disease. In one study, PSMA PET/CT findings were shown to influence treatment decisions in up to 28% of cases, emphasising the critical importance of accurate lesion characterisation [[7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR7 "Hofman MS, Lawrentschuk N, Francis RJ, Tang C, Vela I, Thomas P, et al. Prostate-specific membrane antigen PET-CT in patients with high-risk prostate cancer before curative-intent surgery or radiotherapy (proPSMA): a prospective, randomised, multicentre study. Lancet. 2020;395:1208–16. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)30314-7
                
              .")].

To address interpretation variability and standardise reporting, several interpretation systems have been developed, notably the PSMA Reporting and Data System (PSMA-RADS) Version 2.0 [[8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR8 "Werner RA, Hartrampf PE, Fendler WP, Serfling SE, Derlin T, Higuchi T, et al. Prostate-specific membrane antigen reporting and data system version 2.0. Eur Urol. 2023;84:491–502. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2023.06.008
                
              ."), [9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR9 "Almalki YE, Basha MAA, Ali SA, Donkol RH, Hamed MAG, Metwally MI, et al. Comparison of prostate-specific membrane antigen reporting and data system version 1.0 versus 2.0 for prostate cancer assessment. Radiol Imaging Cancer. 2025;7:e240390. 
                https://doi.org/10.1148/rycan.240390
                
              .")]. PSMA-RADS employs a 5-point ordinal scale ranging from certainly benign (PSMA-RADS 1) to highly suspicious for malignancy (PSMA-RADS 5), with indeterminate findings described as PSMA-RADS 3. The use of PSMA-RADS v2.0 has demonstrated excellent accuracy of 97.4%, 94.7% and 95.7% in new diagnoses, follow-up and biochemical recurrence cases respectively [[9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR9 "Almalki YE, Basha MAA, Ali SA, Donkol RH, Hamed MAG, Metwally MI, et al. Comparison of prostate-specific membrane antigen reporting and data system version 1.0 versus 2.0 for prostate cancer assessment. Radiol Imaging Cancer. 2025;7:e240390. 
                https://doi.org/10.1148/rycan.240390
                
              .")]. While PSMA-RADS demonstrates excellent interobserver agreement [[10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR10 "Bundschuh RA, Lütje S, Bundschuh L, Lapa C, Higuchi T, Hartrampf PE, et al. High interobserver agreement on PSMA PET/CT even in the absence of clinical data. Clin Nucl Med. 2023;48:207–12. 
                https://doi.org/10.1097/RLU.0000000000004524
                
              .")], indeterminate lesions classified as RADS-3 continue to present diagnostic uncertainty.

To address the limitations of visual assessment and structured reporting systems, quantitative methods incorporating imaging and clinical data have been explored as promising solutions. The bone uptake metastatic probability (BUMP) score represents a composite model designed to distinguish UBU from true bone metastases on [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")]. BUMP score integrates PET-derived maximum standardised uptake values (SUV max), CT based mean Hounsfield units (HU mean) as a measure of lesion density and androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) status at the time of imaging to generate a composite prediction score for malignancy. BUMP score demonstrated robust diagnostic performance in internal validation, achieving an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.871 (_n_ = 448 bone uptake, 42% metastatic), with external validation AUROC of 0.925 (_n_ = 89 bone uptake, 51% metastatic) [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")]. The continuous nature enables threshold-based decision making tailored to the specific clinical priority and patient-specific factors.

Despite initial promise, external validation remains limited. There is a paucity of evidence demonstrating whether BUMP score can effectively reduce diagnostic ambiguity specifically in equivocal lesions which represents the clinical scenario where quantitative tools could provide maximum clinical benefit with direct impact on treatment decision making. In addition, there is limited evidence comparing the performance of BUMP score against structured reporting systems such as PSMA-RADS.

Therefore, the present study aimed to further externally validate diagnostic performance of BUMP score for prediction of skeletal metastases in bone lesions detected on PSMA PET/CT across a multi-centre patient cohort. The purpose was also to compare the performance of BUMP score with PSMA-RADS version 2.0 and assess the utility of BUMP score for further stratifying indeterminate PSMA-RADS lesions.

## Methods

### Study design and patient cohort

This was a retrospective, multi-centre study including patients with prostate cancer undergoing [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT scans as part of routine clinical care at three tertiary cancer centres in the UK between October 2021 and March 2023.

PET/CT imaging was performed across the three centres using GE Healthcare Discovery 690 and 710 PET/CT scanners. Standardised radiotracer injection protocols and image acquisition parameters followed European Association of Nuclear Medicine (EANM) guidelines [[11](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR11 "Fendler WP, Eiber M, Beheshti M, Bomanji J, Calais J, Ceci F, et al. PSMA PET/CT: joint EANM procedure guideline/SNMMI procedure standard for prostate cancer imaging 2.0. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2023;50:1466–86. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-022-06089-w
                
              .")]. Proprietary ordered subset expectation maximisation (OSEM) time-of-flight reconstructions were performed at each site by default (Vue Point FX, VPFX, GE Healthcare) and used for imaging evaluation.

### Ethical approval

This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was considered by each participating institution as an observational service evaluation project with no impact on patient care. Formal ethics committee approval and informed patient consent was waived.

### Imaging analysis and lesion selection

[18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT scans were retrospectively reviewed to identify bone lesions demonstrating focal PSMA uptake. Lesions were included if they exhibited discrete uptake above local background activity and were not definitively physiological uptake. Lesions were segmented by a panel of experienced nuclear medicine and radiology reporters, all of whom were blinded to the adjudicated clinical outcomes. Segmentation was undertaken using a paired-review process in which one reporter delineated the lesion on standard PET/CT visualisation software (XD; Mirada Medical, Oxford, UK) and a second reporter checked and confirmed the contour. Any discrepancies were resolved by consensus. Image review and lesion delineation were undertaken by appropriately credentialed radionuclide radiologists, including dual-certified radiologists/nuclear medicine physicians, with experience in PSMA PET/CT reporting ranging from 1 to more than 5 years. A detailed description of the image-review and annotation procedure and a blank copy of the patient- and lesion-level data-collection form are provided as Supplementary Materials [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#MOESM1) and [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#MOESM2), respectively. For each lesion, the following quantitative metrics were extracted:

*   SUV max (Maximum standardised uptake value) values from VPFX PET reconstruction, recorded as the highest pixel value within a manually drawn volume of interest (VOI) encompassing the lesion.

*   HU mean (Mean Hounsfield unit) from the corresponding CT component of the PET/CT images.

At patient level, androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT) status at imaging, previous definitive treatment, ISUP grade group and, for patients with biochemical recurrence, PSA doubling time (PSA-DT) at imaging were recorded where available. Patients with biochemical recurrence were classified according to European Association of Urology criteria when the necessary variables were available. Following radical prostatectomy, low-risk biochemical recurrence required a PSA-DT of > 12 months and pathological ISUP grade group _<_ 4, whereas high-risk biochemical recurrence was defined by a PSA-DT of ≤ 12 months or pathological ISUP grade group 4-5. Following definitive radiotherapy, low-risk biochemical recurrence required an interval to biochemical failure of > 18 months and biopsy ISUP grade group _<_ 4, whereas high-risk biochemical recurrence was defined by an interval of ≤ 18 months or biopsy ISUP grade group 4-5. Patients with insufficient information were reported as not classifiable [[12](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR12 "Van den Broeck T, van den Bergh RCN, Arfi N, Gross T, Moris L, Briers E, et al. Prognostic value of biochemical recurrence following treatment with curative intent for prostate cancer: a systematic review. Eur Urol. 2019;75:967–87. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2018.10.011
                
              ."), [13](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR13 "Preisser F, Abrams-Pompe RS, Stelwagen PJ, Böhmer D, Zattoni F, Magli A, et al. European association of urology biochemical recurrence risk classification as a decision tool for salvage radiotherapy—a multicenter study. Eur Urol. 2024;85:164–70. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2023.05.038
                
              .")]. The BUMP score for each lesion was calculated using the combined values of SUV max, HU mean and ADT status (at the time of scanning), based on the published formula by Bauckneht et al. ([https://scorecalc.shinyapps.io/BUMP/](https://scorecalc.shinyapps.io/BUMP/)) [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")].

PSMA-RADS v2.0 categories (1 to 5) were assigned to each lesion by a single experienced reporter to assess agreement against the BUMP score. PSMA-RADS v2.0 scoring was agreed in consensus by a second unblinded reader. PSMA-RADS 3 represented equivocal lesions.

To facilitate direct comparison between BUMP score and PSMA-RADS v2.0 performance lesions labelled PSMA-RADS 3 (equivocal) were removed from confusion matrices and diagnostic performance metric analysis. Sensitivity analysis evaluating the impact of including lesions labelled PSMA-RADS 3 and treating them as benign or malignant was performed. In addition, a patient-level sensitivity analysis was performed to assess the influence of unequal numbers of lesions contributed by individual patients.

### Reference standard for ground truth

Ground truth classification of each lesion (benign, indeterminate or malignant) was established using a composite reference standard which involved review of electronic patient records of individual patients to obtain relevant clinical outcome data. This incorporated:

*   Follow-up imaging: Progression (increase in size/number or new lesions) or stability/resolution on subsequent imaging.

*   Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) kinetics: PSA response to treatment or rising PSA without other clear cause.

*   Multi-disciplinary clinical consensus: decision by the clinical or study team after reviewing all available clinical, biochemical and imaging data.

Lesions lacking adequate follow-up data were deemed indeterminate and excluded from analysis.

### Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis and graphical representation generation was performed in R (version 4.5.2) and Python (3.14.0). Comparison of continuous lesion characteristic variables was performed using linear mixed effects statistical testing with a significance level of _p <_ 0.05. Linear mixed effects models were used to account for intra-patient clustering arising from the inclusion of multiple lesions from the same patient.

BUMP and PSMA-RADS v2.0 discrimination for identifying malignant lesions were assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. ROC curves were constructed and the area under the ROC curve (AUROC) computed. To facilitate comparison of BUMP vs PSMA-RADS v2.0 while accounting for within-patient correlation of multiple lesions, patient-level bootstrap resampling (_n_ = 1000 iterations) was performed. Patients were sampled with replacement, including all their lesions, preserving clustering structure. AUROCs and their differences were calculated for each iteration. The 95% confidence intervals were derived from bootstrap percentiles and statistical significance (_p <_ 0.05) was assessed via two-tailed bootstrap testing.

The Liu criterion was utilised to derive the optimum local threshold for BUMP score in characterising benign/malignant lesions [[14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR14 "Liu C, White M, Newell G. Selecting thresholds for the prediction of species occurrence with presence-only data. J Biogeogr. 2013;40:778–89. 
                https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.12058
                
              .")]. This technique matches the methodology used in development and validation of BUMP score [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")].

Confusion matrices and diagnostic performance statistics were generated at two key thresholds of BUMP score for comparison:

*   Literature derived cut-off (0.25): Obtained from Bauckneht et al.’s publication [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")].

*   Locally derived optimum cut-off: Determined by the Liu criterion [[14](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR14 "Liu C, White M, Newell G. Selecting thresholds for the prediction of species occurrence with presence-only data. J Biogeogr. 2013;40:778–89. 
                https://doi.org/10.1111/jbi.12058
                
              .")] in this new cohort.

Confusion matrices and diagnostic performance statistics were also generated for PSMA-RADS v2.0 scoring.

## Results

Patient and lesion characteristics are displayed in Tables [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab1), [2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab2) and [3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab3). A total of 130 patients (median age 70 years) with 412 bone lesions were included in ROC analysis (Fig.[1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Fig1)). Of these, 251 lesions (60.9%) were confirmed to be malignant prostate cancer metastases by multidisciplinary consensus. 161 lesions (39.1%) were deemed to represent benign lesions. The median follow-up length was 22.8 months (IQR 18.8, Range 1.3–130.8).

### BUMP Score Performance

The calculated median BUMP score was higher for malignant lesions (median 0.427, (IQR 0.78)) compared to benign lesions (median 0.08 (IQR 0.07)) (_p <_ 0.001). ROC analysis of BUMP score demonstrated an area under the curve (AUROC) of 0.812 (95% CI 0.636–0.931) for discriminating malignant from benign lesions (Fig.[2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Fig2)).

**Fig.2**

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve demonstrating diagnostic performance of BUMP score and expert PSMA-RADS v2 for characterising benign and malignant bone lesions on PSMA PET/CT. AUROC = Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve

The optimal probability threshold using the Liu criterion within the cohort was a threshold of 0.134. At this probability threshold, after exclusion of lesions labelled PSMA-RADS 3 (_n_ = 44), BUMP achieved sensitivity of 73.9% (95% CI 46.3–93.4), specificity of 80.0% (95% CI 71.9–87.2), balanced accuracy of 77.0% (95% CI 62.5–87.4) and F1 score 0.800 (95% CI 58.2–91.9). At the literature sourced probability threshold of 0.25, sensitivity decreased (62.6%, 95% CI 34.0–83.4) with improved specificity (88.5%, 95% CI 82.5–93.8), resulting in an F1 score of 0.74 (95% CI 47.7–88.3) (Fig.[3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Fig3)).

### Comparison with expert PSMA-RADS v2.0

In PSMA-RADS v2.0 evaluation, 234 out of 251 (93.2%) malignant lesions were scored as 4: probable prostate cancer or 5: definite prostate cancer. 125 out of 161 (77.6%) benign lesions were scored as benign or probably benign. Score 3 was assigned to 31 benign and 13 malignant lesions.

ROC analysis of PSMA-RADS v2.0 scoring demonstrated an AUROC of 0.980 (95% CI 0.952–0.993) (Fig.[2](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Fig2)). When PSMA-RADS 1–2 were treated as benign and PSMA-RADS 4–5 as malignant with exclusion of PSMA-RADS 3 lesions (_n_ = 44), this resulted in a diagnostic performance with sensitivity 98.3% (95% CI 94.5–100), specificity 96.2% (95% CI 90.3–100), balanced accuracy 97.3% (95% CI 93.9–99.5), F1 score 0.981 (95% CI 94.4–99.7) (Table[3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab3), Fig.[4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Fig4)). PSMA-RADS v2.0 performed better than BUMP score with an AUROC difference of 0.1679 (95% CI: 0.050 to 0.337, _p <_ 0.001).

In equivocal PSMA-RADS 3 lesions (_n_ = 44) BUMP scoring demonstrated reduced discriminative power with an AUROC of 0.605 (95% CI 0.457–0.781) (Table[3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab3), [Supplemental Figure](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#MOESM3)).

### Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis showed that PSMA-RADS v2.0 retained superior performance to BUMP regardless of how PSMA-RADS 3 lesions were handled. When PSMA-RADS 3 lesions were treated as benign, sensitivity, specificity and balanced accuracy were 93.2% (95% CI 84.2–96.9), 96.9% (95% CI 92.6–100) and 95.1% (95% CI 90.2–97.8), respectively. When PSMA-RADS 3 lesions were treated as malignant, sensitivity increased to 98.4% (95% CI 94.5–100) with reduced specificity of 77.6% (95% CI 67.9–86.1) and balanced accuracy of 88.0% (95% CI 82.8–92.3). By comparison, BUMP showed lower performance across all lesions at both the locally derived threshold of 0.134 and the literature-derived threshold of 0.25, with balanced accuracies of 75.4% (95% CI 62.0–85.4) and 74.4% (95% CI 61.4–85.2), respectively (Table[3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab3)).

In the patient-level sensitivity analysis of 130 patients, including 79 without and 51 with skeletal metastatic disease, PSMA-RADS retained superior performance to BUMP. After excluding 24 patients with a maximum PSMA-RADS category of 3, sensitivity was 95.6%, specificity was 95.1% and balanced accuracy was 95.3%. When category 3 was treated as negative, balanced accuracy was 90.3%, compared with 84.7% when treated as positive. For BUMP, balanced accuracy was 75.0% at the 0.134 threshold and 72.2% at the 0.250 threshold. Patient-level AUROC was 0.966 for PSMA-RADS after exclusion of indeterminate patients and 0.840 for BUMP (Table[4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#Tab4)).

## Discussion

In this multicentre retrospective study, the diagnostic performance of the Bone Uptake Metastatic Probability (BUMP) score with structured reporting using PSMA-RADS Version 2.0 for characterising bone uptake on PSMA-1007 PET/CT scans was compared. The findings demonstrate that although the BUMP score achieved good overall diagnostic performance (AUROC 0.812, 95% CI: 0.636–0.931), it was outperformed by structured expert reporting incorporating PSMA-RADS. The patient-level sensitivity analysis was consistent with the primary lesion-level findings, with PSMA-RADS retaining superior diagnostic performance to BUMP. This suggests that the principal conclusion was not driven solely by greater weighting of patients contributing multiple lesions.

While structured PSMA-RADS assessment demonstrated superior diagnostic accuracy, the reproducibility and objective nature of BUMP score offers potential utility as a decision support adjunct for bone lesions, particularly in high-volume centres or to assist reporters with limited subspecialist expertise. The BUMP score was developed and validated by Bauckneht et al. who reported AUROC values of 0.87 and 0.92 in internal and external validation respectively [[5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR5 "Bauckneht M, D’Amico F, Albano D, Balma M, Cabrini C, Dondi F, et al. Composite prediction score to interpret bone focal uptake in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients imaged with [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT. J Nucl Med. 2024;65:1577–83. 
                https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.124.267751
                
              .")]. In the current multicentre dataset, the AUROC value of 0.812 represents a modest reduction in performance, likely reflecting broader real-world heterogeneity. In addition, the optimum probability threshold in our centres differed. This is a common challenge when generalising quantitative models across different institutions and highlights the importance of local threshold assessment prior to clinical deployment. A benefit of the BUMP score is that parameters are relatively simple to obtain through delineation with a volume of interest and through reviewing the patient record to determine ADT status. More complex radiomic or artificial intelligence analysis of lesions with contextual awareness may better emulate human interpretative reasoning and improve predictive performance [[15](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR15 "Bauckneht M, Pasini G, Di Raimondo T, Russo G, Raffa S, Donegani MI, et al. [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT-based radiomics may help enhance the interpretation of bone focal uptakes in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer patients. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2025;52:2076–86. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-025-07085-6
                
              ."), [16](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR16 "Islam MZ, Spiro E, Yap PT, Gorin MA, Rowe SP. The potential of generative AI with prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET/CT: Challenges and future directions. Medical Review. 2025;5:265–76. 
                https://doi.org/10.1515/mr-2024-0086
                
              .")]. This is supported by a recent multicentre externally validated radiomics-based machine-learning model for [18F]PSMA-1007 bone uptake classification, which demonstrated robust external test performance and significantly improved discrimination compared with SUVmax alone [[17](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR17 "Di Raimondo T, Pasini G, Mantarro C, Russo G, Cosentino S, Laudicella R, et al. Development and external validation of a multicenter radiomics-based machine learning model for differentiating metastatic and non-metastatic bone uptakes on [18F]PSMA-1007 PET/CT in prostate cancer. EANM Innovation. 2026. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eanmi.2026.100275
                
              .")].

The lower discriminative power of BUMP scoring in the context of equivocal (PSMA-RADS 3) lesions (AUROC 0.605, 95% CI 0.457–0.781) highlights limitations of quantitative metrics when clinical and anatomical context is not incorporated. Equivocal lesions may demonstrate intermediate SUV max values and ambiguous CT density characteristics. Expert interpretation can integrate lesion distribution, morphology and uptake with overall disease burden and clinical pre-test probability, features that are not represented in BUMP. A systematic review found that ribs were the commonest site of unspecific bone uptake across PSMA tracers and that isolated rib uptake was usually non-metastatic. However, unspecific uptake also occurs in the spine and pelvic girdle, particularly with [18F]PSMA-1007. Lesion location should therefore inform, rather than determine, metastatic probability and should be considered alongside uptake intensity, CT morphology and the overall clinical context [[18](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR18 "Woo S, Tong A, Becker AS, Friedman KP, Leithner D, Charbel C, et al. Evaluating indeterminate bone lesions and lymph nodes on PSMA-PET: a multidisciplinary consensus algorithm and 1-year implementation results. Eur Radiol. 2026;36:5068–81. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-025-12301-w
                
              ."), [19](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR19 "Rizzo A, Morbelli S, Albano D, Fornarini G, Cioffi M, Laudicella R, et al. The Homunculus of unspecific bone uptakes associated with PSMA-targeted tracers: a systematic review-based definition. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2024;51:3753–64. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-024-06797-5
                
              .")]. This interpretation is supported by a recent study comparing MDT assessment with PROMISE criteria for indeterminate bone uptake on staging PSMA PET, which demonstrated substantial agreement overall but found that long-term follow-up favoured MDT interpretation in all discordant cases [[20](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR20 "Priftakis D, Aziz F, Bin Essa N, Davda R, Lyasheva M, Maitra S, et al. Multidisciplinary team interpretation performance for indeterminate bone uptake on PSMA PET during prostate cancer staging: comparison with PROMISE criteria. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2026. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-026-07763-z
                
              .")]. That study also reported that MDT interpretation was influenced by Gleason score and tracer used, and specifically highlighted pitfalls in rib and thoracic spine lesions, particularly on [18F]PSMA-1007 imaging [[20](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR20 "Priftakis D, Aziz F, Bin Essa N, Davda R, Lyasheva M, Maitra S, et al. Multidisciplinary team interpretation performance for indeterminate bone uptake on PSMA PET during prostate cancer staging: comparison with PROMISE criteria. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2026. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-026-07763-z
                
              .")]. These findings are consistent with the reduced performance of BUMP in our equivocal subgroup and support the continued importance of expert contextual assessment when interpreting borderline lesions.

There is an increasing drive towards standardised reporting within radiology. Multiple studies have identified that structured or proforma-based reporting significantly increases the completeness of cancer staging reports [[21](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR21 "Pesapane F, Tantrige P, De Marco P, Carriero S, Zugni F, Nicosia L, et al. Advancements in standardizing radiological reports: a comprehensive review. Medicina (B Aires). 2023. 
                https://doi.org/10.3390/medicina59091679
                
              ."),[22](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR22 "Neri E, Brady AP, Gibaud B, Visser JJ, Goldberg SN, Pyatigorskaya N. ESR paper on structured reporting in radiology. Insights Imaging. 2018;9:1–7. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s13244-017-0588-8
                
              ."),[23](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR23 "Schwartz LH, Panicek DM, Berk AR, Yuelin Li M, Hricak H. Improving communication of diagnostic radiology findings through structured reporting 1. RadiologyRsnaOrg n Radiol. 2011;260:174–81. 
                https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.11101913/-/DC1
                
              ."),[24](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR24 "Nobel JM, van Geel K, Robben SGF. Structured reporting in radiology: a systematic review to explore its potential. Eur Radiol. 2022;32:2837–54. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-021-08327-5

                https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-018-5340-3
                
              .")]. Initiatives driving standardised reporting are also being promoted for PSMA PET/CT reporting [[26](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR26 "Esfahani SA, Morris MJ, Sartor O, Frydenberg M, Fanti S, Calais J, et al. Standardized template for clinical reporting of PSMA PET/CT scans. Eur J Nucl Med Mol Imaging. 2024;52:335–41.

                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2025.08.005
                
              .")]. A recent study examining the use of standardised scoring systems and frameworks in PSMA PET/CT reporting in patients referred to NYU Langone Health in the United States of America highlighted only 12% of reports (12 of 111) used a standardised reporting framework and in the context of nodal or distant metastases only 27% (_n_ = 11) documented lesion size and 10% (_n_ = 4) referred to corresponding CT characteristics [[28](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR28 "Vargas HA, Fanti S, Herrmann K, Eiber M, Rowe SP, Rivera A, et al. This heterogeneity in reporting style could potentially lead to misinterpretation, diagnostic errors, inconsistent follow-up and management delays [[24](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR24 "Nobel JM, van Geel K, Robben SGF. Structured reporting in radiology: a systematic review to explore its potential. Eur Radiol. 2022;32:2837–54.

The Standardised PSMA PET/CT Analysis and Reporting Consensus (SPARC) project aimed to combat heterogeneity in PSMA PET/CT through standardised reporting frameworks [[27](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR27 "Herrmann K, Walz J, MacLennan S, Briganti A, Cornford P, Czernin J, et al.               .")] and PSMA-RADS for structured reporting [[8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR8 "Werner RA, Hartrampf PE, Fendler WP, Serfling SE, Derlin T, Higuchi T, et al. Recent validation studies have shown excellent reader agreement even among less experienced readers, supporting its broader implementation as a structured reporting tool [[9](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR9 "Almalki YE, Basha MAA, Ali SA, Donkol RH, Hamed MAG, Metwally MI, et al. An important contextual consideration is that most patients in this study cohort underwent PSMA PET/CT for post-treatment recurrence detection reflecting current commissioning policy from NHS England [[33](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR33 "England N.                 www.england.nhs.uk/publication/prostate-specific-membrane-antigen-radiotracers-in
                
              - (accessed May 27, 2026)")]. In this setting, UBU can often be managed more conservatively through watchful waiting or commencement of ADT. However, there is a strong evidence base for use of PSMA PET/CT for staging high-risk localised prostate cancer [[7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR7 "Hofman MS, Lawrentschuk N, Francis RJ, Tang C, Vela I, Thomas P, et al. Prostate-specific membrane antigen PET-CT in patients with high-risk prostate cancer before curative-intent surgery or radiotherapy (proPSMA): a prospective, randomised, multicentre study. Lancet. 2020;395:1208–16. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(20)30314-7
                
              .")] and in this clinical scenario the implications of UBU differs. Therapeutic consequences of UBU in up-front staging are more immediate and potentially more significant for patient management [[34](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR34 "Parker C, Castro E, Fizazi K, Heidenreich A, Ost P, Procopio G, et al. Prostate cancer: ESMO clinical practice guidelines for diagnosis, treatment and follow-up†. Ann Oncol. 2020;31:1119–34. 
                https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2020.06.011
                
              .")]. Only a small proportion underwent imaging for initial staging, precluding a meaningful subgroup analysis by clinical indication. Consequently, the present findings primarily reflect the performance of BUMP and PSMA-RADS in the biochemical recurrence setting and should not be extrapolated uncritically to upfront staging. The integration of structured reporting to more holistically capture reporters’ certainty when describing osseous lesions could become even more important to mitigate overtreatment or delayed therapy in upfront staging. There is a paucity of evidence quantifying the impact of structured and standardised reporting systems on clinical decision making and patient outcomes related to PSMA PET/CT and this should be explored further.

The strengths of the study include its multicentre real-world design, with a reasonable cohort of bone lesions on PSMA PET-CT and moderate follow-up length. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. The retrospective design introduces inherent selection bias, the range of follow-up varied significantly with the shortest length of follow-up representing 40 days. Several lesions per patient were included and so the dataset likely contained intra-patient clustering. While patient level bootstrapping was used for ROC confidence intervals and performance thresholds, diagnostic performance metrics and confusion matrices were calculated at the lesion level without adjustment for this clustering and the precision of these estimates may therefore be mildly inflated. The study evaluated discrimination and threshold-based classification performance but did not formally assess calibration of BUMP as an absolute probability estimate. The reported BUMP values should therefore not be interpreted as calibrated lesion-level risks of malignancy in this cohort. The analysis of lesions labelled PSMA-RADS 3 was limited by a small sample size and the results should be interpreted cautiously. Generalisability is also limited to [18F]PSMA-1007, which has a higher prevalence of equivocal bone lesions but a lower malignancy rate than other PSMA radioligands; these findings should therefore not be directly extrapolated to other tracers [[35](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00259-026-08154-0#ref-CR35 "Woo S, Freedman D, Becker AS, Leithner D, Mayerhoefer ME, Friedman KP, et al. Equivocal bone lesions on PSMA PET/CT: systematic review and meta-analysis on their prevalence and malignancy rate. Clin Transl Imaging. 2024;12:485–500. 
                https://doi.org/10.1007/s40336-024-00631-6
                
              .")]. A significant limitation in this area of research is in determining the ground truth of bone lesions. In this clinical scenario, histological proof of malignancy of bone lesions is rarely available and ground truth analysis relies on multidisciplinary consensus and assessment of the overall clinical picture through interrogation of the electronic patient record, which is often influenced by the reporter’s interpretation of PSMA PET/CT imaging findings. This increases the likelihood of incorporation bias. Six patients in this study were deemed to have lesions that were truly indeterminate and this represents a key patient group which were excluded from statistical analysis. Reader-specific experience was not recorded in sufficient detail to permit an experience-stratified analysis. The utility of BUMP scoring may be more prominent in less experienced reporters, and this should be explored.

## Conclusion

BUMP scoring performed well in predicting metastatic bone lesions but underperformed compared to expert structured PSMA-RADS v2.0 assessment. In equivocal lesions BUMP score offered limited value, highlighting the importance of expert interpretation for evaluating uncertainty raised by unspecific bone uptake.
