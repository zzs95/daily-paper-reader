Title: Deep learning-based diagnostic report generation for low-resolution functional medical images via cross-modal visual and textual alignment

URL Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10489-026-07456-y

Published Time: Tue, 23 Jun 2026 02:06:19 GMT

Markdown Content:
## Abstract

Automatic diagnostic report generation holds great significance in reducing physicians’ workload while improving the reliability and effectiveness of diagnostic and treatment processes. However, generating clinically faithful diagnostic reports from low-resolution nuclear medicine images remains a major challenge due to limited visual representation capacity and weak cross-modal alignment. In response, we propose a unified framework for automatic report generation from SPECT bone scintigrams that integrates domain-adaptive representation learning, fine-grained image–text alignment, and anatomy-guided supervision. Specifically, we design a Domain-Adaptive Visual Feature Extractor (DAVFE) that combines ImageNet initialization, contrastive pretraining on large-scale unlabeled SPECT data, and attention-based recalibration to enhance modality-specific visual encoding. Next, a Feature Interaction Alignment Module (FIAM) explicitly models the interactions between global narrative semantics and lesion-level textual cues, enabling fine-grained and clinically consistent cross-modal alignment. Finally, an Anatomy-guided Progressive Granularity Loss (APGL) introduces hierarchical supervision from global to lesion level, mimicking the coarse-to-fine diagnostic reasoning process of physicians. Experiments performed on 2,091 clinical bone scintigrams indicate that our approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in both conventional natural language generation metrics (BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L) and a hierarchical Clinical Efficacy metric designed for lesion localization. Ablation studies further validate the complementary contributions of DAVFE, FIAM, and APGL. Human evaluation and visualization analyses confirm that the generated reports are more clinically faithful, interpretable, and reliable. Overall, the proposed approach offers a valuable pathway to achieving trustworthy and intelligent diagnostic support within nuclear medicine.
