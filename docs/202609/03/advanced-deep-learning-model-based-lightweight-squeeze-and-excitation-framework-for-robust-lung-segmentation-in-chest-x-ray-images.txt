## Abstract

Accurate lung segmentation in Chest X-rays (CXRs) is essential for Computer-Aided Diagnosis systems targeting respiratory diseases. However, current deep learning models present a trade-off: they are either computationally intensive, restricting deployment in resource-constrained clinical settings, or lightweight but compromised in boundary precision. To resolve this challenge, we propose a lightweight hybrid framework addresses the channel-capacity mismatch inherent to MobileNet-based UNet3+ architectures, a structural limitation overlooked in prior lightweight adaptations. Specifically, Squeeze-and-Excitation attention blocks are embedded within full-scale skip connections to recalibrate inter-channel feature responses, enabling lightweight encoders to effectively feed dense multi-scale decoders without an information bottleneck. The framework couples a pre-trained MobileNet-V2 encoder with deep supervision and Test-Time Augmentation to enforce boundary-aware gradient flow and spatial invariance during inference. With 2.55 million parameters, the model achieves a 98.4% reduction in computational complexity (from 1891.71 to 29.76 GFLOPs) compared to standard UNet3+. Evaluation on three public benchmarks under fivefold cross-validation yielded Dice Similarity Coefficients of 98.72% on Montgomery, 98.62% on JSRT, and 97.10% on Shenzhen, alongside precise boundary tracking with an Average Symmetric Surface Distance down to 0.96 pixels and a 95th-percentile Hausdorff Distance of 2.68 pixels. Cross-dataset validation confirms robust generalizability across independent clinical cohorts, while Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping explanations verify that the model accurately targets clinically relevant anatomical boundaries. Achieving an inference latency of 281 ms on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU, this provides a high-fidelity, interpretable, and resource-efficient solution for point-of-care diagnostics.
