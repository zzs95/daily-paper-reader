Abstract 

The advancement in technology has transformed different aspects of our daily lives. The most profound is its influence on medicine. Whether diagnosis or treat-ment, advances in technology have improved the quality of medical care. This article proposes a model to classify pneumonia effectively with the help of three models: InceptionV3, VGG-16, and EfficientNet. After getting the results for each model, the accuracy and AUC score of each model are compared with the ensem-ble of the three models, which is the proposed model. With the help of the models, binary classification was performed to categorize the chest radiograph datasets into normal and pneumonic lung detection. The Inception model achieved a max-imum accuracy of 84.13% and the VGG16 model obtained a maximum accuracy of 60.42%. The EfficientNet model provided a maximum accuracy of 86.38%, the highest of the three models implemented individually. The proposed model is the ensemble of these three models which yielded a better accuracy as compared to them which is 90.38% which makes the proposed model efficient than the three models. 

Keywords: InceptionV3, VGG-16, EfficientNet, Pneumonia, Ensemble learning 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS 1 Introduction 

Pneumonia is a pulmonary infection, the air sacs in the lungs, known as alveoli, get loaded with fluid or pus, blocking oxygen intake and making breathing difficult. Child mortality due to pneumonia accounts to 18% worldwide. Fifty percent of child deaths caused due to pneumonia occur in India. This means almost 3.7 lakh children die of pneumonia in India every year. According to reports by WHO, one in three newborn babies die due to pneumonia. While pneumonia is a deadly condition, the increased mortality rate especially among infants is due to late detection. Early and correct detection of pneumonia is crucial for timely and effective remedy. Convolutional neural network architecture and image processing domains are continuously advancing, and so is the potential of using these technologies in automated crop disease detection and classification through image analysis. This study chose and evaluated 3 models and their ensemble, each offering varied capabilities and features unique to itself. The findings of this study can evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of these models in the field of pneumonia identification, and help researchers to overcome limitations and deploy the ones promising accurate results.The ensemble improved accuracy on this public test split, but further validation on external datasets is needed before clinical use. In this study, the three deep learning models which are EfficientNet, InceptionV3, and VGG16 are considered for pneumonia detection from human chest radiographs. These models have demonstrated high performance during image classification tasks due to advanced architectures. To boost the efficiency and accuracy of the model, an ensemble learning approach is also employed, combining the predictions of VGG16, EfficientNet, and InceptionV3 through average voting. Ensemble learning combines the strengths of multiple models to produce a better and reliable prediction. This method includes the strength of each model which aims to outperform their performances. By evaluating the performance of each model individually and in combination, we aim to identify the most promising approaches for improving detection accuracy. The major contributions of the proposed work are listed below. 

• An ensemble learning framework using InceptionV3, VGG16, and EfficientNet to detect pneumonia based on chest X-ray images is proposed. 

• An accuracy of 90.38% for ensembled model, surpassing the individual models (InceptionV3: 84.13%, VGG16: 60.42%, EfficientNet: 86.38%) is attained. 

• An average voting to merge the advantages of all three models, making the classification more reliable is applied. 

• Class imbalance ios solved within the dataset by undersampling the training set without altering real-world class distribution within the test set for improved generalization. This article is organized as follows: Section 2 provides Literature review and section 3 discusses proposed methodology. Section 4 and section 5 provides description of the dataset and experimental results obtained respectively. Section 6 concludes the article followed by section 7, future work.

3 Proposed Methodology 

For the classification of the chest radiographs, three models are implemented which are InceptionV3, VGG16, and EfficientNet. The three models were then ensembled using their predictions to get better accuracy, proving that the ensembled model has higher accuracy than the three models. The proposed framework will use a hetero-geneous ensemble learning approach where VGG16, EfficientNetB0, and InceptionV3 are combined as an ensemble. Each of the models has its unique feature represen-tation abilities as a result of having varied depths, designs of convolutional layers, pooling methods, and optimization procedures. The heterogeneous ensemble learning will combine the probabilities of each of the models using equal weight average voting. Analysis is done on the training set to identify the size of the samples in each class.

The training data is first afflicted with a class imbalance where the pneumonia class has a much larger number of samples compared to the normal class. To counter this and prevent model learning bias, undersampling is performed only on the training set of the dataset. The process starts by analyzing the distribution of the class label, therefore identifying the majority class (pneumonia) and minority class (normal). To have a balanced training set, a random selection of the pneumonia class instances was selected, thereby lowering its representation to be the same as the number of images that are within the normal class. Retaining the original distribution in the test set provides a more accu-rate model performance estimate, particularly the generalization to new cases. The approach enhances the reliability and generalizability of the trained models and, in contrast, also ensures the measurement metrics are representative of real cases. The dataset was employed through the use of its predefined split between training, validation, and testing samples present in the public chest X-ray dataset. The training subset was employed for learning and optimizing the parameters of the model, whereas the validation sample was employed to keep track of the performance of experiments during the training process and to evaluate the performance difference between various hyperparameter configurations. Meanwhile, the test subset was not employed during the training and validation process but was solely used for the final evaluation of the trained models.

3.4 Ensemble model 

For the ensemble, the images are trained with each model separately and their pre-dictions are taken. The architecture for the VGG16 model is shown in Figure. 4. Each model provides probability scores for each sample in the test set which is between 0 and 1. Then these predictions are combined using the average voting method. And it predicts the probabilities across the three models. Then each model’s predictions are binarized with the threshold of 0.5 and the final model’s predictions after ensembling are also binarized with the threshold of 0.5 which gives the final model predictions for each sample based on average probabilities across all the models. The evaluation met-rics used are log loss, confusion matrix, accuracy, and roc curve. The average voting is selected as the three models had different strengths and weaknesses and the pre-dictions are taken in an equalized manner. It is observed that the accuracy improved when the three models were ensembled using average voting. When the three mod-els, VGG16, InceptionV3, and EfficientNet, were ensembled, to maintain consistency across the models, a shared input shape of 150 × 150 × 3 was applied at training time so that equalized feature extraction was performed for all architectures. For ensembling, the three models using average voting different optimizers and pooling are used and not the same as the individual models to ensemble them properly. All the same hyperparameters used for implementing the three models individually were not used for the ensembled model for proper functioning of the ensemble model.

The ensem-ble framework increases computational complexity because predictions from multiple deep learning architectures must be generated simultaneously. However, the improved diagnostic robustness may justify its use in clinical decision-support systems where prediction reliability is critical. The averaging approach allows the ensemble framework to overcome any erroneous predictions made by individual models and achieve better generalization performance, especially in difficult chest x-ray cases with pneumonia related changes. The performance of the ensemble was excellent since each model was capable of recognizing distinct discriminative characteristics on radiographs. Through using predictions of diverse models, the method lowers variance and is more resilient to the individual models’ weaknesses. In order to make the results of the model reproducible, all of the constituent mod-els in the proposed ensemble were implemented using TensorFlow/Keras and trained using the identical input image resolution of 150 × 150 × 3. The ensemble was made up of three deep learning models, namely: VGG16, EfficientNetB0, and InceptionV3. As for the VGG16 model, only the convolutional part was used without the top layer. After that, the Flatten layer and a fully connected layer with 128 units and ReLU activation were used to feed the model into the sigmoid output layer. All of the pre-trained convolutional layers were frozen during training. This model was trained using Adam optimizer, Binary Cross Entropy Loss, and Accuracy. EfficientNetB0 comprised the Global Average Pooling layer, dense layer comprising 256 neurons with ReLU activation function, and finally the sigmoid layer. In the training process, Adam optimizer having a learning rate of 0.0001 along with the binary cross-entropy loss function was employed. A ReduceLROnPlateau callback with reduction factors of 0.05 and 5 epochs for patience along with a minimum learning rate of 2 × 10 −6 were used. Early stopping and model checkpointing were also incorporated. Regarding the development of the InceptionV3 model, it is not equipped with any classifier as it has been pre-trained on the ImageNet database. Consequently, only the convolutional base was used to obtain the feature map, which passed through the Flatten layer, the fully connected layer containing 1024 neurons and ReLU activation, Dropout layer with a dropout ratio of 0.5, and the sigmoid layer. Training was done using Stochastic Gradient Descent with a learning rate of 0.0001, a momentum of 0.9, and Nesterov acceleration. Binary Cross Entropy was used as the loss function, while accuracy was used as the performance measure.

Final classification was performed based on the probability average. Binary cross-entropy was used as the loss function, a sigmoid function for the output layer activation, early stopping with patience of 5 epochs, and model checkpoints. The number of epochs for the training process was experimentally tuned for each model while all other hyperparameters were held constant. As a means to assess the generalization ability and robustness of the proposed ensemble algorithm, a cross-validation experiment has been performed.
