## Abstract:

Compositional Zero-shot Surgical Triplet Recognition (CZSTR) requires models to recognize unseen <instrument, verb, target> combinations that never co-occurred in training, reflecting the generalization needed in real surgery. Current Chain-of-Thought (CoT) using in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) lack sufficient surgical semantics and thus struggle on CZSTR. We propose a more intuitive, human-like reasoning framework, Cognitive awakening Chain-of-Surgery (CoCoS), which mimics how surgeons think: first briefly viewing the scene, then tracking the operation over time, and finally deriving structured conclusions. This stepwise cognitive-awakening guides LVLMs to better understand surgical scenes. Observing that LVLMs can hallucinate even on simple subtasks such as instrument recognition accidentally, we further introduce a Multimodal Image–Sequence–Text (MiST) fusion module to enhance framework robustness. To assess our approach, we reorganize existing surgical triplet datasets into a compositional zero-shot benchmark. Experiments show that our framework generalizes better to unseen triplets, surpassing both traditional models and LVLMs on this challenging task. Code will be released.
