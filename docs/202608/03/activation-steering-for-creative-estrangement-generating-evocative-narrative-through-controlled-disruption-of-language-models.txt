Abstract 

We investigate activation steering as a method for gen-erating extended narrative that can function as a textual environment for creative inspiration. Using contrastive pairs inspired by the aesthetics of Tarkovsky and Lynch, we construct steering vectors encoding modes of per-ceptual estrangement and apply them to Llama 3.1 8B Instruct. Single-layer steering operates in two regimes: a moderate regime that elevates literary register with-out producing estrangement, and a high regime where estrangement emerges but coherence collapses. Dual-layer steering resolves this by applying a compositional vector at layer 13 simultaneously with a content-level vector at layer 14, accessing a region where estrange-ment and coherence coexist. The dual-steered outputs exhibit emergent disruptions (person shifts, narrative self-contradiction, referential instability) absent from all training pairs, arising from competing representa-tional pressures during generation. Comparing steered outputs against explicitly prompted fragmentation re-veals two qualitatively distinct modes: prompting pro-duces conventional fragmentation devices, while steer-ing produces intact sentences whose content is self-contradicting. A blind analysis of evaluator reasoning texts and human reader feedback confirm this distinc-tion. Human readers tended to perceive dual-steered outputs as stranger than prompted outputs, suggesting that occasional and surprising disruption within coher-ent prose is more estranging than pervasive and catego-rizable fragmentation. 1

# Introduction 

Viktor Shklovsky’s concept of ostranenie (defamiliarization or making strange) proposes that art’s essential function is to restore how things are perceived rather than how they are known (Shklovsky 1965). The target of estrangement is not the exotic but the ordinary, seen as if for the first time. We see this across artistic domains: in Andrei Tarkovsky’s So-laris (1972), rain falls inside a cabin and a father does not react; in David Lynch’s Black Lodge ( Twin Peaks , 1990), characters speak in reversed recordings and coffee does not pour from an inverted cup. In each case, the estrangement operates through minimal displacement from the familiar.

Petric (1989) describes Tarkovsky’s films as suffused with an oneiric air that transcends the commonplace significa-tion of objects to reach what the eye ordinarily neglects. Rodley (2005) characterizes Lynch’s work in similar terms: Lynch’s interest lies in the defamiliarization process and the waking/dream state, rather than in incongruity as specta-cle or overt surrealism. This is close to Shklovsky’s “stone made stony”, not by adding to it but by making us see it again. We ground our approach in these filmmakers’ aesthetics because their estrangement targets perception itself rather than the content of reality. Genre estrangement (fantasy, science fiction, magic realism) introduces rule-governed al-terations that the reader eventually assimilates; cognitive tension resolves as the rules become clear. Tarkovsky and Lynch embed unexplained impossibilities within naturalis-tic observation. Neither the reverse speech inside the Black Lodge in Lynch’s Twin Peaks, nor the rain inside the cabin in Tarkovsky’s Solaris is ever explained. More fundamen-tally, the ontological status of the figures in the Lodge and of those who appear on the station in Solaris is left unre-solved. Hari appears to Kelvin as his wife, long dead, and comes to understand that she is not. What the Lodge figures are is never specified. As Petric (1989) observed, the viewer feels that something is “wrong” but cannot detect sufficient proof to discredit the events on the basis of everyday logic. The indeterminacy is sustained and is often left unresolved, maintaining the heightened attention that Berlyne’s inverted U-curve (Berlyne 1971) identifies as maximally engaging. This choice shapes our experimental design. Because we model disruptions of perception rather than alterations of content, the resulting steering vectors encode abstract per-ceptual modes (temporal self-contradiction, ontological un-certainty, identity instability) rather than genre conventions. Defamiliarization of this kind has direct implications for computational creativity. Rubin (2023) describes the Seed phase of the creative process as the stage where “we are completely open to any stimulus that might lead us to that state of mind where art becomes inevitable.” At this stage, artists need stimuli that are indeterminate, surprising, and open to interpretation. Leonardo Da Vinci advised young artists to look at old stained walls and streaked stones for inspiration (Turner 2011). The stains are not art in them-selves but projective stimuli whose meaning is unresolved. The artist forms meaning through the process of attempted resolution, and it is this process that activates the creative state. Prior research demonstrated this with LSTM-based Variational Autoencoders trained on small curated datasets, which generate lines characterized by syntactic indetermi-nacy and semantic openness (“clouds humanity without light”, “through the wrong moon”) that function effectively as seeds for creative inspiration (Vechtomova, Sahu, and Ku-mar 2021). However, this approach operates at the sentence level. The question then becomes: can we generate extended narrative that sustains these qualities, text that functions not as a finished story but as a textual environment for creative inspiration? Activation steering is a technique that modifies a lan-guage model’s internal representations during generation by adding directional vectors to hidden states. In this paper, we demonstrate that this technique can produce long-form narrative with precisely these qualities. We steer Llama 3.1 8B Instruct using contrastive vectors derived from paired lit-erary and neutral passages and evaluate the outputs using a seven-metric framework scored by LLM evaluator (Claude Sonnet 4.5). Our key findings are: 1. Two regimes of first-order steering. Steering at layer 13 operates in two distinct regimes: a moderate regime ( α

2.0–6.0) that elevates literary register without producing estrangement, and a high regime ( α 7.5–8.0) where es-trangement and syntactic disruption emerge abruptly but coherence collapses. No first-order alpha value produced both high estrangement and high coherence. 2. Dual-layer steering achieves coherent estrangement. 

Applying a first-order vector at layer 13 simultaneously with a second-order content-level vector at layer 14 pro-duces estrangement comparable to the highest first-order alphas while maintaining substantially higher coherence, accessing a region single-layer steering does not reach. 3. Emergent syntactic estrangement. Dual-steered outputs exhibit syntactic disruptions (person shifts, temporal self-contradiction, logical impossibility) absent from all train-ing pairs. These features emerge from the interaction of competing representational pressures during generation. 4. Disruption of syntax vs. disruption through syntax. 

Explicitly prompted fragmentation produces conventional devices (ellipses, sentence fragments, lowercase). Steer-ing produces grammatically intact sentences whose con-tent is self-contradicting or ontologically unstable. 

# Related Work 

# Defamiliarization and Aesthetic Engagement 

The relationship between aesthetic engagement and stim-ulus complexity follows an inverted U-shaped curve, also known as Wundt effect, first formalized by Berlyne (1971). Completely predictable stimuli fail to hold attention; com-pletely random stimuli offer no foothold for pattern recogni-tion; stimuli of intermediate complexity produce the great-est engagement. Gold et al. (2019) offered a learning-based explanation for this effect: when events are completely pre-dictable or fully random, little learning occurs as patterns are either trivial or impossible to discern. Intermediate pre-dictability creates conditions for meaningful cognitive en-gagement, as the perceiver is rewarded for the effort of par-tial pattern recognition. As Pepperell (2006) noted, indeter-minate visual images create heightened perception and at-tention as habitual recognition is suspended, a state that is itself a precondition for creative work. Zeilinger (2023) ex-tended this to AI-generated visual art, arguing that visual in-determinacy invites active meaning-making rather than pas-sive reception. Artists have long developed deliberate techniques for in-ducing this productive disruption. The cut-up technique, from Tzara through Burroughs, fragments existing text to break habitual language patterns (Burroughs 2012). David Bowie described its function as “igniting anything that might be in my imagination” (BBC 1975), aligning it with the Seed phase of creativity. However, pure randomness does not pro-duce art. As Nees (1969) observed, there must be structure to narrow random outcomes into discernible aesthetic infor-mation. The productive zone lies between predictability and randomness, the same intermediate territory that the Wundt curve identifies as maximally engaging. Tarkovsky and Lynch exemplify this productive zone in cinema. Unlike genre estrangement, which introduces rule-governed alterations that the viewer eventually assimilates, their work embeds unexplained impossibilities within natu-ralistic observation, maintaining the state of heightened per-ception that Pepperell identifies as a precondition for cre-ative work. 

# Computational Approaches to Creative Text Generation 

Boden’s influential typology distinguishes combinational, exploratory, and transformational creativity (Boden 2004). In exploratory creativity, a system searches within an estab-lished conceptual space; in transformational creativity, the space itself is altered to make previously impossible outputs conceivable. Wiggins (2006) formalized this distinction, providing a mathematical framework for describing creative systems in terms of the conceptual spaces they traverse and transform. Colton’s creative tripod framework argues that a system perceived as creative must demonstrate not only skill but also appreciation and imagination (Colton 2008), and the FACE/IDEA descriptive models provide formal criteria for evaluating creative acts (Colton, Charnley, and Pease 2011). Within computational text generation, Oliveira’s PoeT-ryMe (Oliveira 2012) demonstrated autonomous poetry generation using semantic and syntactic templates, while Gerv´ as (2009) surveyed computational approaches to sto-rytelling with attention to how creativity manifests in nar-rative structure. More recently, LLMs have prompted new questions about machine creativity. Franceschelli and Mu-solesi (2025) analyzed LLM creativity through the lens of Boden’s typology and Colton’s tripod, while Ismayilzada et al. (2025) found that LLMs generate stylistically complex stories but fall short on novelty and surprise compared to hu-man writers. In human-AI co-creative writing, Calderwood et al. (2020) found that novelists use generative models as constraining tools that push the boundaries of their writing. This finding aligns with our interest in text that functions as creative stimulus rather than finished output. The connection between Shklovsky’s defamiliarization and computational text generation has been explored in prior work on neural poetry generation (Vechtomova and Sahu 2025), where LSTM-VAE outputs were analyzed as in-stances of productive estrangement. The present work asks whether this connection holds at the level of extended narra-tive. Ho et al. (2025) proposed a “Familiarity-Estrangement Space” that uses topic modeling to project textual materials along dimensions of topical familiarity and novelty, allow-ing users to browse for texts at a desired level of thematic strangeness. Their system operates on existing text rather than generating new text with estranging qualities. The distinction between systems that generate polished outputs ( pushing content toward the artist) and systems that generate indeterminate stimuli ( pulling the artist into the cre-ative state) is central to understanding how computation can support creativity. LSTM-VAE sentence generators (Vech-tomova, Sahu, and Kumar 2021) are pull-oriented. They produce open, indeterminate fragments rather than finished text. But the architecture operates at the sentence level, gen-erating individual lines rather than extended narrative that sustains these qualities across a unified passage. The present work is also pull-oriented. It aims to generate not polished stories but textual environments for creative inspiration. In Boden’s terms, LSTM-VAE models perform exploratory creativity within a learned latent space. The present work investigates whether activation steering can achieve some-thing closer to transformational creativity by altering the model’s generative space to produce outputs with qualities absent from any training example. 

# Activation Steering 

Activation steering modifies a language model’s hidden states during inference by adding learned directional vec-tors, enabling fine-grained control over output properties without retraining. Subramani et al. (2022) first demon-strated that steering vectors can be extracted from pretrained language model decoders and applied via addition to hid-den states, achieving high-fidelity generation control and unsupervised sentiment transfer. Turner et al. (2023) intro-duced Activation Addition (ActAdd), computing mean ac-tivation differences between contrastive prompt pairs and injecting the resulting vector during generation to reliably modulate behavioral properties such as sycophancy and hal-lucination tendency. Rimsky et al. (2024) extended this to Llama 2, systematically evaluating steering effective-ness across layers and scaling factors. In parallel, Zou et al. (2023) proposed Representation Engineering (RepE), a top-down framework that reads and controls high-level con-cepts (honesty, fairness, harmfulness) in LLM internal rep-resentations at the population level rather than through in-dividual neurons or circuits. Li et al. (2023) demonstrated Inference-Time Intervention (ITI), shifting activations dur-ing inference to improve truthfulness, suggesting that mod-els possess internal representations of properties they do not reliably express. The present work applies activation steering to a qualita-tively different domain: generating aesthetically productive text for creative inspiration. Rather than steering toward or away from a binary behavioral property, we steer along a continuous dimension, from prosaic description to literary estrangement, defined by contrastive pairs that model spe-cific aesthetics of defamiliarization. This requires navigat-ing a narrow productive zone where the output is sufficiently estranged to defamiliarize but sufficiently coherent to sus-tain engagement. This challenge has no direct analogue in the behavioral steering literature.

# Steering Vector Construction 

We construct steering vectors from contrastive pairs of matched passages describing similar scenarios, where one version is written in neutral, expository prose and the other employs techniques of literary estrangement. The vector is the mean difference between the model’s internal represen-tations of the literary and neutral sides, capturing the direc-tion in activation space that corresponds to the transforma-tion from prosaic description to estranged perception. We designed five sets of 12 pairs (60 total), each em-bodying a distinct mode of estrangement: (1) Contempla-tive , modeled on Tarkovsky’s sustained perceptual attention; (2) Lynchian , the eruption of wrongness within the famil-iar; (3) Lost Highway , identity instability and spatial in-consistency; (4) Stalker , landscape as responsive environ-ment; and (5) Solaris , ontological uncertainty and memory as material substance. In each pair, the neutral side is a bland, surface-level recounting of events or surroundings, while the literary counterpart describes the same scenario with imagistic depth and a specific mode of estrangement. The neutral and literary sides are length-matched to within 15%. # Layer Selection 

We swept all 32 layers of Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, comput-ing the contrastive vector at each layer and evaluating four metrics: direction norm (L2 magnitude), relative L2 (nor-malized by activation scale), consistency (within-set pair-wise cosine similarity), and cross-file alignment (between-set cosine similarity). Direction norm peaked at final layers, merely tracking activation magnitude. Relative L2 peaked at early layers, which show high relative differences for any contrastive signal. The two predictive metrics, consistency and cross-file alignment, both peaked at layer 13 (consis-tency 0.654, cross-file alignment 0.741). Full layer analysis is available on the supplementary materials website. The So-laris pairs produced the strongest signal (consistency 0.704), consistent with ontological uncertainty requiring the deepest semantic displacement. Layer 13 sits at approximately 40% depth, a region as-sociated with semantic composition rather than discourse planning (Tenney, Das, and Pavlick 2019; Wendler et al. 2024). Steering at this layer targets how the model con-structs meaning while leaving its coherence and syntactic planning machinery undisturbed. All first-order experiments accordingly use the composite vector at layer 13, injected with the cosine-decayed schedule. 

# Validation of Training Pairs 

We evaluated both sides of all 60 pairs using the same met-ric pipeline applied to steered outputs (Table 1). The literary sides scored substantially higher on estrangement (4.42 vs. 1.03), indeterminacy (4.14 vs. 1.09), and originality (3.98 vs. 1.00), all p < . 001 (Welch’s t-test). Crucially, coher-ence was near-equivalent (4.73 vs. 4.97), confirming that the contrastive signal captures qualitative transformation rather than fragmentation. The estrangement gap (3.39) exceeded the register gap (2.47), indicating that the primary dimension encoded is perceptual defamiliarization rather than register inflation. A blind identification procedure provided convergent ev-idence that the five sets encode distinct aesthetic dimen-sions. An independent LLM evaluator (GPT-4o, OpenAI), given each set without labels, correctly identified the domi-nant aesthetic mode for three of five sets and swapped only the two Lynch-derived sets, which is an expected confusion given their shared origin. 

# Evaluation Metrics 

Choice of evaluators. We chose Claude Sonnet 4.5 (An-thropic) as the primary evaluator because prior work on aesthetic evaluation of literary text (Sahu and Vechtomova 2025) found that frontier models with explicit reasoning pro-duce reliable discrimination on literary dimensions where smaller or less capable evaluators do not. We chose GPT-4o (OpenAI) as the cross-validator specifically be-cause it comes from a different model family and training pipeline than Claude, so agreement between the two pro-vides stronger evidence than agreement within a single fam-ily. For token-level surprisal we use GPT-2 (117M) rather than a peer-scale reference: a small, weak model captures token-level statistical regularity rather than the same seman-tic competencies the steered model possesses, avoiding the circularity of using the steered model’s own representational neighborhood as the standard against which to measure sur-prisal. The blind reasoning-text analysis uses Claude Opus 4.6, which had not seen the evaluation rubric during scoring. 

Scoring protocol. We evaluate steered outputs using seven metrics scored on a 1–5 scale by Claude Sonnet 4.5, following a similar protocol to (Sahu and Vechtomova 2025) but with a modified set of metrics and a different LLM eval-uator. We queried the LLM evaluator five times per story per metric at temperature 0.7 and averaged the resulting scores; temperature 0.7 with averaging reduces the influence of any single sampling artifact in the evaluator’s rationale, whereas temperature 0 would lock onto one rationale that may not be representative. Each evaluation prompt requires the model to produce a one-sentence reasoning statement before com-mitting to a numerical rating. This forced reasoning step proved essential during development. Without it, the evalu-ator exhibited reduced discrimination, conflating rhetorical intensity with genuine literary achievement. Ornate but se-mantically closed prose ( “a symphony of despair, a requiem for the living” ) scored comparably to compressed but gen-uinely open prose ( “I am a cry without a mouth” ). The rea-soning step forces the evaluator to articulate why a passage merits a given score, producing reliable discrimination be-tween register elevation and aesthetic transformation. 

Choice of metrics. The seven metrics are: imagery (sen-sory vividness), indeterminacy (semantic openness and re-sistance to singular interpretation), originality (unusual col-locations, surprising metaphors, unexpected verb choices), 

register (distance from plain prose toward heightened lit-erary language), coherence (logical connection and over-all readability), estrangement (defamiliarization of ordinary experience), and syntactic estrangement (departures from conventional prose syntax, including person shifts, sen-tence fragmentation, and sentences that undermine their own logic). Imagery, originality, and coherence are con-ventional in literary-generation evaluation (Sahu and Vech-tomova 2025; Ismayilzada, Stevenson, and van der Plas 2025); indeterminacy and estrangement operationalize the aesthetic targets of our theoretical framing (Shklovsky 1965; Pepperell 2006; Zeilinger 2023); register and syntactic es-trangement were added to test the disruption-of/through dis-tinction. The first five characterize the mode of the prose; estrangement measures the overall effect; syntactic estrange-ment captures a specific mechanism that proved critical for distinguishing first-order from dual-layer steering. The training-pair validation (Table 1) provides evidence that the metrics discriminate as intended: neutral and literary sides separate cleanly on the targeted dimensions while coherence remains near-equivalent, which is non-trivial evidence the metrics are picking up the targeted constructs rather than overall “quality”. Full rubric definitions are available in the supplementary materials. We additionally compute token-level surprisal using GPT-2 (117M) as a model-independent complement (Sahu and Vechtomova 2025). 

Cross-validation with GPT-4o. To test whether our find-ings depend on idiosyncrasies of a single evaluator, we re-scored all 204 outputs with GPT-4o using identical prompts and rubrics. Spearman rank correlations between the two evaluators are strong across all metrics: register ( ρ = 0 .94 ), originality ( ρ = 0 .90 ), indeterminacy ( ρ = 0 .87 ), imagery (ρ = 0 .86 ), syntactic estrangement ( ρ = 0 .78 ), estrange-ment ( ρ = 0 .77 ), and coherence ( ρ = 0 .68 ), all p < 10 −28 .GPT-4o rates consistently higher than Claude Sonnet 4.5 (mean offset 0.67 points), which depresses Krippendorff’s α

for some metrics, but since our arguments depend on ordinal patterns rather than absolute scores, rank-order agreement is the more informative measure. Condition-level means con-firm that both evaluators reproduce the key trajectories: es-trangement rising with α, coherence dropping sharply be-tween α 7.5 and 8.0, and dual-layer compositions accessing higher estrangement at comparable coherence. # Results 

# First-Order Steering: Two Regimes 

We applied the composite vector at layer 13 with cosine de-cay across alpha values of 0 (baseline), ±2.0, ±4.0, ±6.0, 

±7.5, and ±8.0 to 15 prompts spanning three alignment tiers (high, medium, low). Tier assignment was determined by cosine similarity between each prompt’s sentence embed-ding and the literary training-pair centroid in the sentence-embedding space using a sentence-transformer model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2). Figure 1 shows the full bidirectional met-ric profiles. The results reveal two distinct regimes rather than a smooth curve with a single optimum. 

Moderate regime ( α 2.0–6.0): register elevation without estrangement. Imagery rises from ≈2.8 to ≈3.5, regis-ter from ≈1.8 to ≈3.3, and indeterminacy modestly from 

≈1.3 to ≈2.3. But estrangement barely moves, remaining Figure 1: All metrics vs. alpha for first-order steering at layer 13 (aggregated across alignment tiers; dashed = nega-tive steering). Syntac-tic estrangement stays at floor (1.0) throughout. Coherence remains high ( ≈4.0–4.6). High regime ( α 7.5–8.0): abrupt shift. Between alpha 7.5 and 8.0, multiple metrics shift sharply. Estrangement jumps from 2.5 to 3.5, originality from 2.9 to 3.75, and syn-tactic estrangement from 1.4 to 2.8. Simultaneously, co-herence drops from 3.8 to ≈2.7. The transition between regimes is steep: the model absorbs moderate perturbation as register elevation, maintaining conventional narration, un-til the steering force exceeds some threshold and the output transforms rapidly. The productive qualities (estrangement, originality) arrive together with the destructive ones (coher-ence loss). No first-order alpha value produces both high estrangement and high coherence. 

Bidirectional asymmetry. Negative steering compresses all metrics toward a uniform floor: imagery 2.0, originality 1.0, estrangement 1.0, while coherence remains relatively high (3.5–4.5). The resulting prose is affectless and colour-less. This asymmetry suggests the literary-estrangement direction is a narrow channel. There are many ways to write flat prose, but the path toward defamiliarization is spe-cific and unidirectional. The asymmetric response to nega-tive steering (affectless, register-flattened prose distinct from baseline) also provides empirical evidence that dℓ corre-sponds to a meaningful direction rather than an arbitrary axis in activation space. 

# Dual-Layer Steering: Coherent Estrangement 

The first-order results establish that layer 13 alone did not produce estrangement without losing coherence. Dual-layer steering resolves this by separating the two transformations. 

Second-Order Pair Design To isolate content-level prop-erties (what impossible events occur, as opposed to how sentences construct meaning), we constructed second-order pairs in which both sides are matched for literary register and imagery, differing only in whether something impossi-ble happens. We developed two sets of 12 pairs: Lynchian 

(e.g., a duplicate suitcase, a reflection acting independently, a childhood self mirroring the protagonist) and Solaris (e.g., a dead person’s letter changing content, a pillow indentation reappearing with strands of hair, a husband imperceptibly re-placed). Validation confirmed the design: both sides scored equivalently on register (2.45 vs. 2.68) and imagery (3.17 vs. 3.21), while estrangement was the sole discriminator (4.12 vs. 1.88, p < . 001 ). Both sides scored at floor on syntactic estrangement (1.13 vs. 1.00). Layer analysis showed consistency and cross-file align-ment peaking at layer 14 (consistency 0.457, alignment 0.503), adjacent to the first-order optimum. 

Second-Order Vectors in Isolation Applying the second-order vectors alone at layer 14 produced no productive steer-ing zone. Below alpha 6.0, outputs remained indistinguish-able from unsteered baseline prose. Above alpha 8.0, out-puts collapsed into perseverative repetition, but the content confirms the vectors encode the intended semantics. The Lynchian vector produced “the buildings to her left were re-flected in the polished side of the building, yet the buildings to her right were not reflected”; the Solaris vector produced “The street light is a copy. She is a copy. The street light is a copy. She is a copy.” At intermediate alpha values (6.5–7.0), target semantics emerged as isolated phrases within other-wise bland prose. 

Dual-Layer Composition We applied the first-order com-posite at layer 13 ( α = 6.0) simultaneously with each second-order vector at layer 14, sweeping alpha from 2.0 to 5.0. 

# Findings 

The dual-layer experiments reveal three main findings, each describing a qualitative transformation that single-layer steering does not achieve. 

Finding 1: Coherent estrangement. At layer-14 alpha 3.0–4.5, dual-layer steering produces estrangement gener-ally in the range of 2.0–3.3, above the layer-13-only baseline (≈1.6), while coherence generally remains above the first-order alpha 8.0 collapse ( ≈2.7). The dual-layer approach accesses a productive zone that does not appear in first-order steering, achieving moderate estrangement at lower cost to coherence. Figure 2a visualizes this. The first-order path traces a diagonal from high-coherence/low-estrangement to the reverse, with no alpha value occupying the upper-right region. Dual-layer steering accesses the intermediate space between these extremes. The character of this estrangement is qualitatively dis-tinct. A Lynchian-steered story about public transit: The bus was coming. The bus was late. The bus was early. The bus was now. Time becomes self-contradictory within intact grammar. Later, absence gives way to presence without transition: Emma looked at the space beside her, saw no one. She looked at the one across from her, saw no one. There was a boy with his eyes fixed on his phone. She looked away. 

Finding 2: Emergent qualities absent from training data. 

In first-order steering, syntactic estrangement remains at floor (1.0) through alpha 6.0, begins rising at alpha 7.5 (≈1.4), and jumps to ≈2.8 at alpha 8.0, when coherence has already collapsed. In dual-layer steering, syntactic estrange-ment increases gradually across the alpha range, reaching 2.5–3.0 by alpha 5.0. Figure 2b shows this contrast: first-order steering produces no syntactic disruption until the high regime, while dual-layer steering generates a gradual in-crease. A Solaris-steered hospital story shifts person mid-paragraph: A single figure sat in the chair. I sit here. I am a shape, lost in a shapeless night. My eyes are fixed on the window. The sky outside is black as a void. My lips are still. My heart is a bullet that shoots with each heartbeat, rips through my chest. I am a cry with-out a mouth. The syntax enacts identity dissolution: a figure observed from outside becomes a voice experiencing itself from within. But alpha 5.0 also produces collapses (“The sound of the sound of the sound”), confirming a narrower produc-tive zone. These syntactic disruptions do not exist in the training data. Both the first-order pairs (syntactic estrangement 1.63) and second-order pairs (1.13) are grammatically conven-tional. The person shifts, temporal self-contradictions, and identity dissolutions emerge from the interaction of compet-ing representational pressures during generation: the first-order vector pushes toward coherent literary prose while the second-order vector destabilizes the reality that prose de-scribes. This distinguishes steering from style transfer. The training pairs define a direction , and the steering magni-tude carries the output past where the pairs sit, into territory where the model’s representations produce transformations absent from any template in the training signal. Notably, the steered outputs contain none of the literal content of the training pairs (no physical doubles, no return-ing dead). Instead, the vectors transmit abstract perceptual structures: the simultaneous truth of contradictory states, the presence of what should be absent. Estrangement is elevated across all prompts, including those (laundry, public transit) with no natural affinity for such devices, consistent with en-coding perceptual modes rather than narrative events. A first-order passage at alpha 6.0 illustrates what layer 13 alone produces: The coat hung in the closet like a ghost of another era, its wool still carrying the faint scent of pipe tobacco and winter mornings, a relic of the man who had worn it through decades of quiet devotion. High register, embedded metaphor, formal diction, but not estranging. Compare the Lynchian dual-steered output: Emily stood on the edge of the cliff, staring at the cliff. She had been staring at the cliff for hours. They said the cliff was not there. Simple vocabulary without metaphors, yet the effect is more estranging. Where the first-order output achieves its lit-erary quality through complex sentence structure and for-mal diction, the dual-steered output achieves estrangement through a simple impossible observation: “They said the cliff was not there,” stated matter-of-factly, congruent with the Lynchian aesthetic in which impossibility is embedded within the mundane. 

Finding 3: Register decreases as estrangement increases. 

An unexpected result is that register decreases under dual-layer steering. The first-order output achieves literary qual-ity through complex sentence structure and formal dic-tion; the dual-steered output achieves greater estrange-ment through reduction, producing a two-dimensional space where first-order occupies the high-register/low-syntactic-estrangement quadrant and dual-layer occupies the reverse. 

# Comparison with Explicit Prompting 

To contextualize the emergent estrangement in dual-steered outputs, we compared them against explicit prompting for disruption. We generated stories under three instruction con-ditions directing the model to produce fragmented, exper-imental prose, and evaluated all conditions with the same pipeline. The three prompting conditions cover the recog-nizable categories of instructed literary disruption (stream-of-consciousness, experimental register, and formal frag-mentation) that the model can identify as targets. The prompted outputs score higher on syntactic estrange-ment, but the gap reflects a qualitatively different mode of disruption rather than a greater degree of the same one (3.78 vs. 2.44 for dual-steered at layer-14 α 4.5–5.0). This gap is visible in Figure 2b, where the three prompting conditions (green) occupy a region of high syntactic estrangement that neither steering method reaches. Prompted outputs frag-ment using conventional devices, including ellipses, sen-tence fragments, and lowercase first person: foggy light... orange and grey, the world around me... blur. I... wait. for... something. a bus. i think. Each of these devices — ellipses, sentence fragments, lower-case first person — is a recognized convention in twentieth-century experimental prose. The steered outputs achieve their effects differently: “The bus that had been on time, was not. The bus that was coming, was not.” These are grammatically complete sentences whose content is self-contradicting.

# Replication Study with Mistral 7B 

To test whether these findings depend on a specific model ar-chitecture, we replicated the steering experiments on Mistral 7B. A layer sweep identified layer 14 (44% depth) as optimal for first-order steering, closely analogous to layer 13 (40% depth) in Llama. The same two-regime pattern emerged, with the transition between regimes occurring at a lower alpha threshold ( α 4.0–6.0 rather than 6.0–8.0), consistent with a smaller model having less capacity to absorb the per-turbation before coherence gives way. Dual-layer steering (layer 14 + layer 17, where layer 17 was the layer-sweep op-timum for the second-order vector in Mistral, analogous to layer 14 in Llama) accessed coherent estrangement at cor-respondingly lower alphas, with the Lynchian second-order vector producing the strongest results. The bidirectional asymmetry also replicated. Full results, graphs, and gen-erated stories are available on the supplementary materials website. 

# Reader Responses 

Six people read 16 stories (4 prompts × 4 conditions) blind to condition. Two are practicing artists (design and visual art/poetry, both with 10+ years of creative practice) and four are computer science graduate students. For each story, par-ticipants rated strangeness and coherence (both 1–5), with an optional free-text field: “If you find this text strange, briefly describe what makes it strange.” Readers also com-pleted four paired comparisons. With six readers, we treat this as a qualitative probe and a check on the automated eval-uation rather than a standalone statistical study. 

Ordinal consistency with automated scores. Human strangeness ratings follow the same ordering as the auto-mated estrangement scores: dual-steered (3.75) > prompted (3.46) > first-order (2.04) > baseline (1.42). Coherence ratings are high across all conditions (4.25–4.92), with no reader reporting difficulty following any text. The ordering is consistent with the automated metrics. 

Qualitative descriptions reveal the disruption-of/through distinction. The most informative result comes from the free-text descriptions, which show a systematic difference in how readers characterize strangeness across conditions. For dual-steered stories, readers point to specific content :“The duplicate phrasing felt like an emotional or psycho-logical fracture”; “She wore no shoes, because she had not expected to stand”; “mangled souls trapped in the spin cy-cle.” These responses identify what is strange in the narra-tive world. For prompted stories, readers point to format-ting and mode : “The text is very jarring”, “all lowercase, with each statement presented line by line”; “The rambling narrative creates a sense of meaninglessness”; “Other than punctuation and text formatting, not very strange.” These responses identify how the text is written. We deliberately left ‘strangeness’ undefined to avoid cue-ing readers toward any particular kind of disruption. With-out guidance about what to attend to, readers consistently described dual-steered texts in terms of content-level con-tradiction and prompted texts in terms of formal mode, inde-pendently reproducing the distinction we found in the LLM evaluator reasoning texts. 

Paired comparisons. Across four paired comparisons, dual-steered outputs were chosen as stranger 16 out of 24 times. In two comparisons (childhood home, laundry), all six readers chose the dual-steered text. The public tran-sit comparison was evenly split, the one case where perva-sive formal disruption was as compelling as content-level estrangement. One reader explained this choice by noting a “rambling quality” that “creates the impression of someone who has psychologically checked out,” suggesting that for-mal dissolution can be effective when readers interpret it as characterizing a psychological state rather than as a stylistic device. Unprompted, readers described dual-steered stories using language that converges with the training pair aesthetics: “a sinister and surreal quality,” “a dark and grainy feel.” These descriptions echo the perceptual modes the vectors encode without the readers knowing what those modes were. 

# Discussion and Conclusion 

The central finding is that activation steering produces ex-tended narrative neither standard generation nor prompting produces: prose in which the stability of voice, reference, and represented reality breaks down within otherwise in-tact syntax. These disruptions are the trace of the coher-ence machinery operating on displaced representations, not the imitation of experimental conventions a prompt would target. The dual-layer approach (compositional steering at layer 13 combined with content-level steering at layer 14) offers practitioners two independently controllable dimen-sions: a register dial (layer 13 alpha) and a reality-stability dial (layer 14 alpha). The lower density of syntactic disruption in dual-steered outputs is essential to its aesthetic function. Constant dis-ruption, as in the prompted condition, is rapidly assimi-lated. The reader categorizes the mode (“stream of con-sciousness,” “experimental prose”) and habitual processing resumes within the new frame. Occasional disruption within otherwise coherent prose preserves the contrast necessary for defamiliarization to operate: the strange moment is ex-perienced against a ground of the familiar, precisely the con-dition Shklovsky identifies as art’s mechanism. This aligns with the aesthetics our training pairs model. In Tarkovsky’s films, the uncanny moment arrives within sustained natu-ralistic observation; in Lynch’s work, the wrongness erupts from within the mundane. The estrangement depends on the familiar context it disrupts. The high disruption regime (high alpha) need not be un-derstood as a failure mode. It represents a different kind of output serving a different aesthetic function. At moder-ate alphas, steered text operates as enriched narrative: con-templative, imagistic, readable. At high alphas, the out-put shifts toward something closer to experimental poetry: syntax dissolves, imagery fragments and recombines, and language resists transparent reading. Shklovsky’s argument applies not only to represented objects but to language as such: in habitual reading, we process words as a transpar-ent vehicle for meaning, passing through them to their ref-erents without perceiving them. High-alpha outputs arrest this process, forcing attention to language as material. For an artist in the Seed phase, such outputs may be precisely what is needed: a perceptual disruption that breaks the ha-bitual language model before new work begins. The steering spectrum thus offers several tools: moderate alphas for con-templative richness, dual-layer composition for coherent es-trangement within intact narrative, and high alphas for raw disruption of the kind found in experimental poetry. Dif-ferent practitioners at different stages of their process will reach for different points on this spectrum. For computational creativity, the most significant impli-cation is the emergence of qualities absent from the train-ing pairs. The training pairs are all grammatically conven-tional; the steered outputs produce person shifts, narrative self-contradiction, and referential instability that no train-ing pair contains. The distinctive quality of these outputs is matter-of-fact delivery of disruption that emerges in the prose itself. The wrongness is stated and not noticed. This is the signature of a model whose representations have been displaced rather than instructed: the conversational prior that ordinarily domesticates strangeness (explaining it, thema-tizing it, reaching for recognizable literary devices) is dis-rupted when steering operates on hidden states directly, no longer able to fully assimilate the displaced trajectory before it manifests in the output.
