ABSTRACT Complete multimodal Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) data are usually required for accurate brain tumor segmentation; however, missing modalities are frequently encountered in clinical practice, which severely degrades the performance of conventional models. In addition, it remains challenging to capture long-range volumetric context while preserving fine-grained boundary details in high-resolution 3D MRI. To address these issues, this paper proposes SMB-Net, a unified framework for brain tumor segmentation under incomplete multimodal MRI. SMB-Net adopts a weight-shared hybrid encoder that combines hierarchical Swin Transformer stages with a cascade Mamba stage to jointly model local texture patterns and global contextual dependencies. To handle arbitrary missing-modality patterns, a MaskFusion strategy is introduced to aggregate modality-aware features at the feature level without relying on modality synthesis. Furthermore, a multi-class boundary enhancement module is embedded into the decoder to predict the boundaries of the enhancing tumor, tumor core, and whole tumor, and to use these boundary cues for feature refinement. Extensive experiments on BraTS 2018 and BraTS 2020 datasets demonstrate that our SMB-Net achieves strong, consistent segmentation performance across complete and incomplete MRI modalities, outperforming state-of-the-art methods. On BraTS 2018, SMB-Net attained higher average Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) scores for the whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor regions (87.66%, 78.01%, and 59.78%), compared with SegMamba (87.13%, 76.78%, and 56.91%). Similarly, on BraTS 2020, SMB-Net scored higher DSC scores in these three brain tumor regions (88.71%, 81.71%, and 64.48%) compared to IMS 2Trans (87.33%, 79.09%, and 62.11%). The code for SMB-Net will be made publicly available at https://github.com/sxsxsx-dev/SMB-Net

In clinical practice, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the gold standard for evaluating gliomas. Multi-modal MRI for brain tumor segmentation, including FLAIR, T1, T1ce, and T2, provides comple-mentary pathological information: FLAIR highlights edema regions, T1ce emphasizes enhancing tumor cores [2], while T1 and T2 capture anatomical structures and tissue contrasts. Leveraging complete multi-modal MRI data is therefore essential for achieving high-precision automatic brain tumor segmentation. However, the assumption of complete modalities is often violated in real-world clinical workflows. Due to scanning time constraints, motion artifacts, equipment fail-ures, or patient-specific conditions, one or more MRI modalities may be missing. Most existing segmentation models, such as UNET [3] and Swin-UNETR [4], adopt channel concatenation to process multimodal inputs jointly. Once a modality is absent, these models do not adapt their learned parameters, resulting in severe performance degradation or even infeasible inference [5]. This practical limitation underscores an urgent clinical need for segmenta-tion models that are robust to incomplete multimodal MRI inputs. Several approaches have been proposed to handle missing modalities, including modality synthesis and shared-representation learning [6]. Nevertheless, synthesizing missing modalities often requires computationally expen-sive generative models and may introduce artifacts that mislead downstream segmentation [7]. Meanwhile, shared-representation methods tend to sacrifice modality-specific discriminative features. Therefore, an effective incomplete-modality solution should avoid image generation while preserving the full semantic capacity of the available modalities. Beyond modality incompleteness, effective model-ing of long-range dependencies in high-resolution 3D medical images remains another fundamental challenge. Convolutional neural networks are inherently limited by local receptive fields, restricting their ability to capture global anatomical context [8]. Transformer-based architectures alleviate this issue through self-attention mechanisms; however, their quadratic computational complexity O(N 2)makes them inefficient for volumetric data [9]. Recently, state space models (SSMs), such as Mamba, have emerged with linear complexity O(N ) and strong global modeling capability [10]. Despite their efficiency, purely SSM-based models may struggle to capture fine-grained local textures that are crucial for delineating tumor structures [11]. This motivates a hybrid design that combines the local modeling strength of Swin Transformer [12] with the global context modeling capability of Mamba. Previous studies in quantitative medical imaging have demonstrated that segmentation dimensionality and lesion boundary definition substantially influence the reliability and reproducibility of downstream image analysis [13], highlighting the importance of accurate volumetric and boundary-aware segmentation frameworks. Therefore, another persistent challenge in brain tumor segmentation lies in boundary ambiguity. Due to the partial volume effect and tumor infiltration [14], the boundaries between tumor core (TC), edema (WT), and surrounding healthy tissue are often highly blurred. Conventional loss functions, such as Dice loss, treat all pixels equally and are insufficient to enforce accurate boundary delineation. Moreover, simple binary edge modeling fails to capture the nested structure of glioma subregions, in which WT contains TC and TC contains Enhancing Tumor (ET). This calls for an explicit multi-class boundary-aware mechanism to guide the network toward precise sub-region separation [15]. To address these issues, we present SMB-Net, a uni-fied framework for incomplete multimodal brain tumor segmentation. SMB-Net adopts a weight-shared hybrid encoder that combines hierarchical Swin Transformer stages with a Cascade Mamba stage to model local texture patterns and long-range volumetric dependencies jointly. To accom-modate arbitrary missing-modality patterns, a dynamic MaskFusion strategy is introduced to aggregate modality-aware features at the feature level without relying on modality synthesis. In addition, a Multi-Class Boundary Enhancement Module (MC-BEM) is embedded into the decoder to predict the boundaries of Enhancing Tumor, Tumor Core, and Whole Tumor, and to use these boundary cues for feature refinement. Extensive experiments on the BraTS 2018 and BraTS 2020 datasets demonstrate that SMB-Net achieves strong and consistent performance across complete and incomplete-modality settings, with partic-ularly robust behaviour in challenging missing-modality scenarios. II. RELATED WORK 

This section reviews existing solutions for the incomplete multi-modal issues in MRI analysis as summarized in Table 1 and discusses how our method differs from existing solutions. 

A. INCOMPLETE MULTI-MODAL MEDICAL IMAGE SEGMENTATION 

Multi-modal MRI, including FLAIR, T1, T1ce, and T2, provides complementary information and has been shown to significantly improve brain tumor segmentation. Most existing methods assume complete modality availability and directly concatenate all modalities into a multi-channel input. However, this assumption is often violated in clinical practice due to variations in scanning protocols and patient conditions, making segmentation from incomplete modalities challenging. Synthesis-based approaches attempt to recover missing modalities using generative models such as GANs [16], Pix2Pix [17], and CycleGAN [18], followed by standard multi-modal segmentation. Although effective to some extent, these methods entail high computational costs and may introduce artifacts or inaccurate structures, which can adversely affect segmentation performance. Shared-representation methods, such as HeMIS [19] and U-HVED [20], aim to learn modality-invariant features by aggregating statistical information across modalities. While avoiding explicit image synthesis, these approaches often lose modality-specific discriminative details and lack explicit awareness of modality availability during feature fusion. To overcome these limitations, we seek an efficient solution that preserves modality-specific semantics without relying on costly image generation. Accordingly, we employ a weight-sharing Siamese encoder to ensure consistent feature extraction across modalities, together with a feature-level dynamic mask imputation strategy that explicitly models missing-modality patterns [21]. This design enables robust segmentation under arbitrary incomplete-modality settings. 

B. ARCHITECTURAL EVOLUTION: FROM CNN TO TRANSFORMER AND SSM 

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), represented by U-Net [3] and V-Net [22], have long dominated medical image segmentation due to their strong ability to capture local spatial features. However, the inherently limited receptive field of CNNs restricts their capacity to model long-range dependencies, which are crucial for understanding global anatomical context in volumetric data. To address this limitation, Transformer-based architec-tures, such as TransUNet [23] and Swin-UNETR [4], have been introduced into medical image segmentation. By lever-aging self-attention mechanisms, these models significantly enhance global context modeling. Nevertheless, the quadratic computational complexity of self-attention with respect to the number of tokens ( O(N 2)) makes Transformers ineffi-cient for high-resolution 3D medical images, often leading to excessive memory consumption or forced resolution reduction.

Recently, state space models [24], exemplified by Mamba, have emerged as a promising alternative. Owing to their linear computational complexity ( O(N )), SSMs enable efficient global sequence modeling and the capture of long-range dependencies. SK-VM ++ [25] embeds Mamba’s state-space modeling within UNet ++ ’s densely nested skip connec-tions [26], achieving favourable segmentation accuracy with substantially mitigated computational scaling relative to conventional convolutions. SegMamba [27] is a 3D medical image segmentation Mamba model that can better capture the long-range dependencies of whole-volume features at each scale than Transformer-based methods and thus achieve better performance in whole-volume feature modeling. Despite these advantages, purely SSM-based architectures may struggle to capture fine-grained local textures [28], such as subtle tumor boundaries, compared to CNNs or window-based Transformers. Motivated by the complementary strengths and limita-tions of these architectures, we propose a hybrid design that combines Swin Transformer and Mamba. Specifically, Swin Transformer is employed to identify ambiguous boundaries between tumor subregions, while Mamba is introduced at the bottleneck to model global contextual dependencies. This hybrid architecture effectively balances the preservation of local details with global representation learning. 

> C. BOUNDARY-AWARE MEDICAL IMAGE SEGMENTATION

Medical image segmentation commonly suffers from the partial volume effect and low tissue contrast, which leads to ambiguous boundaries between different tumor sub-regions, such as edema and tumor core. This issue is particularly pronounced in brain tumor segmentation, where accurate delineation of sub-region boundaries is crucial for reliable diagnosis and treatment planning. To mitigate boundary ambiguity, existing methods mainly fall into two categories. Loss-based approaches introduce boundary-sensitive objectives, such as Hausdorff distance loss or boundary loss, to explicitly penalize contour errors [29]. Auxiliary-task-based methods incorporate addi-tional edge detection branches to enhance boundary aware-ness during training [29]. Despite their effectiveness, most existing boundary-aware strategies [30], [31], [32] focus on binary boundary modeling (foreground versus background). Such designs overlook the nested structure of brain tumors, in which the entire tumor contains the tumor core (TC), which in turn contains the enhancing tumor. Consequently, simple edge detection is insufficient to discriminate complex multi-class boundaries among tumor sub-regions [32]. Motivated by this limitation, we employ a Multi-Class Boundary Enhancement Module (MC-BEM). By explicitly supervising the boundaries of ET, TC, and WT, and feeding the learned boundary cues back into the feature stream as spatial attention gating signals, the proposed module effectively enhances boundary discrimination and improves segmentation accuracy in regions with severe boundary ambiguity. 

> III. METHODOLOGY
> A. OVERVIEW OF THE NETWORK ARCHITECTURE

The overall architecture of our proposed SMB-Net is illustrated in Figure 1. It follows a U-shaped encoder-decoder design, integrated with a weight-shared Swin Transformer, a Cascade Mamba-based bottleneck, a MaskFusion mecha-nism and a boundary-aware decoder. The network is designed for multimodal brain tumor segmentation under incomplete-modality settings and follows an encoder–fusion–decoder architecture. Specifically, each available MRI modality is processed independently through a shared-weight Siamese hybrid encoder to learn modality-consistent representations. This design avoids modality-specific parameter explosion while ensuring feature alignment across different imaging sequences. Given M imaging modalities {xm}Mm=1, where each modality is represented in channels-first format as xm ∈

RB×1×D×H ×W , each input is initially transformed via 3D patch embedding followed by hierarchical Swin Transformer blocks. The encoder captures texture-aware local context through window-based self-attention and enhances inter-window communication via the shifted-window mechanism. To further capture long-range spatial dependencies beyond local receptive fields, the hybrid encoder incorporates a Cascade Mamba stage that employs a selective State Space Model (SSM) to aggregate global contextual information efficiently. To address the challenge of incomplete modal-ity availability, we introduce a MaskFusion module that dynamically aggregates modality-specific features according to modality-availability indicators. This design enables the network to operate flexibly across arbitrary combinations of input modalities without requiring retraining. Finally, the fused multimodal representation is passed to a lightweight decoder with skip connections to restore spatial resolu-tion progressively. To improve boundary delineation and structural consistency, a Multi-Class Boundary Enhancement Module (MC-BEM) is incorporated to refine tumor boundary regions through multi-class boundary prediction, boundary-aware gating, and feature refinement. Overall, SMB-Net integrates local texture modeling, global dependency learning, adaptive modality fusion, and boundary-aware refinement into a unified end-to-end frame-work, enabling robust and accurate segmentation under incomplete multi-modal MRI settings. The proposed network is composed of three core com-ponents, each serving a distinct yet synergistic function: (i) the Hybrid encoder, which combines Swin Transformer stages with a Cascade Mamba stage and feature-level zero-imputation for robust and discriminative feature extraction; (ii) the MaskFusion mechanism for aggregating modality-aware features in arbitrary missing-modality patterns; and (iii) the decoder equipped with MC-BEM for precise sub-region delineation.

> A. DATASET AND EVALUATION METRICS
> 1) BraTS 2018 AND BraTS 2020 BENCHMARK DATASETS

We evaluate the proposed model on two public brain tumor segmentation benchmarks, namely the BraTS 2018 collec-tion [34] and the BraTS 2020 collection [35]. The brain tumor segmentation datasets consist of multi-institutional, multi-modal magnetic resonance imaging scans. For each patient, four MRI modalities are provided: T1, T1c (contrast-enhanced T1), T2, and FLAIR. All images are preprocessed using standardized procedures, including spatial registration, resampling, and intensity normalization. Each voxel in the BraTS datasets is annotated into four classes: necrotic and non-enhancing tumor core (NCR/NET), enhancing tumor, edema, and background. Following the official BraTS evaluation protocol, we assess segmenta-tion performance on three clinically relevant tumor sub-regions: (i) Whole Tumor, consisting of NCR/NET, ET, and ED; (ii) Tumor Core, consisting of NCR/NET and ET; and (iii) Enhancing Tumor. The BraTS 2018 dataset contains MRI scans from 285 patients. We randomly split the dataset into 190 cases for training and 95 cases for testing. The BraTS 2020 dataset comprises 369 patients, of whom 246 are used for training and 123 for testing. We use the same train/test split for all compared models to ensure a consistent evaluation protocol. 

> 2) EVALUATION METRICS

Following the standard BraTS evaluation protocol [34], [35], we employ the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) to evaluate the segmentation performance of the proposed model quantitatively. DSC quantifies the overlap between the predicted segmentation results and the ground-truth annotations across tumor classes, including necrotic and non-enhancing tumor core, enhancing tumor, edema, and background. A higher DSC value indicates a greater degree of similarity between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth. We report DSC scores for three clinically relevant tumor subregions: Whole Tumor, Tumor Core, and Enhancing Tumor. 

> 3) IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS

All experiments are implemented using Python 3.10 and PyTorch 2.6.0. The proposed framework and all comparison methods are trained and evaluated on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA A100 GPU (80 GB memory) and an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Silver 4314 CPU. For each sample, the input tensor corresponding to a single modality has a spatial resolution of 1 × 128 × 128 × 128. At the same time, the corresponding segmentation label is represented as a tensor of size 4 × 128 × 128 × 128, where the four channels denote different tumor classes. The Adam optimizer is adopted for network training, with an initial learning rate of 2 × 10 −4 and a weight decay factor of 1 × 10 −4. In all experiments, the batch size is set to 1 due to the high memory consumption of 3D volumetric data. To ensure a fair comparison with other methods, we re-trained the comparison models under the same data split, input resolution, and training schedule, with the total number of training epochs fixed to 1000 for all models. The proposed model requires approximately 46 GB of GPU memory during training and takes about 60 hours to converge.

D. DISCUSSION 

The experimental results in Table 3 and Table 4 show that SMB-Net provides consistent improvements over representative incomplete-modality segmentation methods on both BraTS 2018 and BraTS 2020. Compared with synthesis-based or shared-representation methods, the proposed feature-level MaskFusion strategy avoids the uncertainty introduced by generated modalities while pre-serving useful modality-specific cues from available scans. This advantage is reflected in the 15 modality-combination evaluations, where SMB-Net maintains strong aver-age DSC scores even in the case of severe modality absence. As shown in Figure 3, the comparison between SMB-Net and Transformer-based baseline models, such as RFNet, mmFormer, and IMS2Trans, and Mamba-based baseline like SegMamba, further indicates that combining local and global modeling is important for 3D brain tumor segmentation.

The stages of Swin Transformer in the SMB-Net preserve local texture and boundary-sensitive information, while the Cascade Mamba stage captures long-range volumetric context with linear computational complexity. The ablation results from Table 5 support this design: adding Cascade Mamba improves all three tumor regions, and adding MC-BEM provides an additional gain, especially for the enhancing tumor region where boundaries are often small, irregular, and difficult to distinguish. The boundary-aware design also helps explain the qualita-tive improvements. In the visual comparisons from Figure 4 and Figure 5, our SMB-Net produces smoother and more coherent tumor structures than several competing methods, particularly around transition regions between edema, tumor core, and enhancing tumor. By explicitly supervising ET, TC, and WT boundaries rather than using a single foreground-background edge target, MC-BEM better matches the nested anatomical structure used in the BraTS evaluation protocol. Nevertheless, the current study has several limitations. First, the experiments are conducted on BraTS bench-mark datasets, and additional cross-institutional or prospec-tive clinical validation is needed to assess robustness under broader acquisition protocols. Second, the evalua-tion mainly uses DSC, while boundary-sensitive metrics such as Hausdorff distance or average surface distance could provide a more comprehensive assessment of con-tour quality. Third, although SMB-Net is effective, 3D volumetric training remains computationally demanding. Future work will therefore investigate broader external validation, more detailed boundary-oriented evaluation, and lightweight implementations suitable for practical clinical deployment. 

V. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we introduce SMB-Net, an innovative and robust framework for brain tumor segmentation in incomplete multimodal MRI data. To address missing modalities, we developed a mask fusion strategy that performs feature-level zero imputation, enabling the network to learn modality-invariant representations without relying on generative synthesis. We built a hybrid Swin-Mamba backbone that con-nects local texture extraction (using Swin Transformers) and linear-complexity global context modeling (using Cascade Mamba). This solves the problem of long-range dependencies in high-resolution 3D volumes. Additionally, we incorporated a Multi-Class Boundary Enhancement Module (MC-BEM) into the decoder to improve segmentation of fuzzy tumor subregions. This module uses explicit edge supervision to sharpen the prediction boundaries. Extensive experiments

He is currently a Lecturer with the School of Computer and Information Technology, Xinyang Normal University. He has authored five papers in prestigious international journals and conferences, including Chaos, Solitons, and Fractals , Applied Mathematics and Computation , and Physica A. His research interests include reinforcement learning, evolutionary games, mechanism design, and intelligent decision-making, with a particular focus on the intersection of reinforcement learning and evolutionary games. Dr. Yang is the Principal Investigator for the Youth Project of the Natural Science Foundation of Henan Province and the Key Research Project of Higher Education Institutions of Henan Province. He also serves as a Peer Reviewer for several international journals, such as Information Sciences , Applied Mathematics and Computation , and Neural Computing and Applications .

CHONGYANG CHEN received the B.S. degree in information and computing science from Linyi University, Linyi, China, in 2017, the M.S. degree in operations research and cybernetics from China University of Mining and Technology, Xuzhou, China, in 2020, and the Ph.D. degree in control science and engineering from the Huazhong Uni-versity of Science and Technology, Wuhan, China, in 2024. He is currently a Lecturer with the School of Computer and Information Technology, Xinyang Normal University. He has authored or co-authored more than ten articles indexed by SCI. He is the Principal Investigator for the Youth Project of the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC), the Key Research Project of Higher Education Institutions of Henan Province, and the HUST Technology Innovation Fund. He has also participated in several high-level research initiatives, including the NSFC Joint Fund Integration Project and two major national projects under the Science and Technology Innovation 2030 Agenda: ‘‘Brain Science and Brain-like Research’’ and ‘‘New Generation Artificial Intelligence.’’ His research interests include multi-agent system coordination, intelligent control, and finite-time control.
