Title: Tokenization Matters

URL Source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1016/j.procs.2026.06.244

Markdown Content:
## Abstract

Byte-tokenized multilingual neural machine translation (MNMT) models often face tokenization bias and data sparsity, especially for low-resource languages such as Dogri and Kashmiri. To address these issues, this work presents a byte-tokenized MNMT framework that integrates language-specific representations derived from pretrained byte-level masked language models. Two encoder-only transformer models, doiByte and kasByte, on monolingual Dogri and Kashmiri corpora are trained from scratch respectively. The trained BPE and byte-level embeddings from these models are then used to initialize the corresponding encoder representations in a 13-language Indic-to-English MNMT model. Each model employs a 6-layer architecture with 1024-dimensional embeddings. During MNMT training, byte-token encoder embeddings for Dogri and Kashmiri are initialized from the corresponding pretrained models and held fixed, while other language embeddings are learned from scratch. The downstream byte-level MNMT model, trained over 13 Indic languages for English translation, is evaluated on the IN22-Gen benchmark. Relative to the BPE-MNMT baseline, the Byte-MNMT+DoiByte configuration yields BLEU score improvements ranging from +0.35 (Bodo) to +7.85 (Do-gri), with additional gains observed for Assamese (+2.49), Urdu (+1.09), and Nepali (+0.13). The Byte-MNMT+kasByte variant delivers targeted gains for Kashmiri (+3.45 BLEU) and Dogri (+4.96), but leads to marginal drops (-0.3 to -0.7 BLEU) in unrelated languages, attributed to cross-lingual embedding drift. Comparable trends are observed on the IN22-Conv dataset. These findings indicate that controlled injection of byte-level pretrained embeddings offers a corpus-efficient and scalable mechanism for improving translation quality in low-resource Indic language scenarios.
