# DAMamba-UNet3D: A Parameter-Efficient Mamba State Space U-Net with Dynamic Adaptive Scan for 3D Medical Image Segmentation 

Mohammad Arafat Hussain, Ellen Grant, Yangming Ou 

Boston Children’s Hospital, Harvard Medical School 

mohammad.hussain@childrens.harvard.edu 

Abstract. We propose parameter-efficient SSM-based U-Net architec-tures for 3D medical image segmentation. Convolutional U-Nets afford 

O(n) local mixing per layer but lack explicit global context; transform-ers provide global reasoning at O(n2) cost in sequence length n. State-space models (SSMs), such as Mamba, offer O(n) global propagation per block. Yet, existing medical SSM segmenters rely on fixed scan patterns and large parameter budgets. Dynamic Adaptive Scan (DAS), which learns data-dependent reordering before selective scan, has not been applied to medical imaging or extended to 3D volumes. We pro-pose DAMamba-UNet3D , a hybrid encoder–decoder that integrates tri-plane 3D-DAS blocks at encoder stages E2–E4 while retaining convo-lutions elsewhere ( ∼5.3 M parameters). On BraTS 2020 five-fold cross-validation, DAMamba-UNet3D achieves mean Dice 0.815 ± 0.013 (full-volume per-case evaluation) at ∼13 × lower parameter cost than Seg-Mamba ( 0.824 ± 0.014 , ∼70 M). At comparable scale, DAMamba-L 

(∼70 M), a wide DAS-native variant with encoder-only DAMamba and a convolutional bottleneck, reaches 0.829 ± 0.012 , surpassing retrained SegMamba by 0.5 pt. Component ablations show that encoder-only DAS placement is critical as bottleneck and decoder SSM blocks lower Dice. Together, the results suggest that learned tri-plane DAS in a hybrid U-Net is competitive with, and under our large-scale design may im-prove upon, SegMamba’s fixed Tri-orientated Mamba (ToM) scanning on BraTS 2020. Code: https://github.com/marafathussain/DAMamba-UNet3D. 

Keywords: Dynamic adaptive scan · Efficient deep learning · 3D seg-mentation · State space models · Mamba · U-Net 

## 1 Introduction 

Three-dimensional (3D) volumetric segmentation requires both precise local bound-ary delineation and coherent global spatial context [1], under tight compute and parameter budgets. For a volume with n voxels (or tokens), convolutional, trans-former, and state-space architectures trade accuracy against complexity differ-ently: Convolutional U-Nets [2] are parameter-efficient with O(n) compute per arXiv:2607.22718v1 [cs.CV] 21 Jul 2026 2 Hussain et al. 

layer for local mixing, but each layer aggregates information only within a lim-ited receptive field; global 3D context requires depth scaling rather than explicit long-range mixing in one pass. Vision transformers [3,4] provide global context via self-attention at O(n2) memory and compute. For example, a 128 3 patch con-tains n ≈ 2.1 M tokens, prohibitive for naive global attention on a single clinical GPU. State-space models (SSMs) , especially Mamba [5], propagate information along a 1D sequence in O(n) time and memory per block, offering global context without quadratic attention maps. SegMamba [6] is the state-of-the-art representative of this third family for dense 3D segmentation. It embeds Mamba blocks in a U-shaped 3D network with Tri-orientated Mamba (ToM) modules that serialize features along three anatomical directions at O(n) cost per scan. At ∼70 M parameters, however, SegMamba’s scan trajectories are fixed by design; the model achieves long-range context through capacity and multi-orientation flattening rather than content-adaptive reordering. Recently, DAMamba [7] introduced Dynamic Adaptive Scan (DAS) for 2D natural-image recognition. DAS is learnable, input-dependent spatial reordering via deformable resampling before selective scan. To our knowledge, DAS has not been used in medical imaging, nor extended beyond 2D. We address both gaps with DAMamba-UNet3D, the first DAS-based architecture for medical image segmentation, with a tri-plane formulation that lifts DAS to 3D volumes. Our contributions are: 1. First DAS application to medical segmenta-tion. We transfer DAS from 2D natural-image classification to dense 3D medical segmentation for the first time, embedding it in a hybrid U-Net. 2. 2D-to-3D DAS extension. We lift the 2D DAS operator to volumes via tri-plane adap-tive scanning (axial, coronal, sagittal) fused before volumetric selective scan, as an alternative to hand-designed multi-orientation flattening. 3. Parameter-efficient DAMamba-UNet3D. Convolutions in the stem, bottleneck, and de-coder for local detail; tri-plane DAS–SSM blocks only at encoder stages (about 5.3 M parameters, ∼13 × fewer than SegMamba). 4. Two-scale validation on BraTS 2020. The compact model reaches near-parity with retrained Seg-Mamba ( 0.815 vs. 0.824 mean Dice); DAMamba-L ( ∼70 M), with stacked en-coder DAMamba and a convolutional bottleneck, surpasses SegMamba ( 0.829 

vs. 0.824 ), suggesting that learned DAS in an encoder-only hybrid design can outperform fixed ToM under identical training and evaluation protocol.

2.3 Dynamic Adaptive Scan and Tri-Plane DAMamba Block 

Each DAMamba block (Fig. 1) combines Dynamic Adaptive Scan (DAS) [7] with the selective scan of Sec. 2.2. We adopt the 2D DAS operator from [7] and extend it to volumes via tri-plane scanning (step 2 below); our contribution is this medical-first use and 3D formulation, not a redesign of the core DAS mechanism.

2.4 DAMamba-UNet3D Architecture 

DAMamba-UNet3D is a 4-level 3D U-Net with base width C=16 (channels 

C, 2C, 4C, 8C, 16 C at encoder stages). The stem (E1), bottleneck, and entire decoder are convolutional. At each encoder stage E2–E4, a convolutional block (two 33 convolutions) extracts local features, followed by one tri-plane DAMamba block (conv → DAS → selective scan; Sec. 2.3) for global context. DAMamba-L (large-scale variant). To evaluate DAS at SegMamba-scale capacity, we train DAMamba-L ( ∼70 M), a wide DAS-native U-Net with SegMamba-aligned stage widths, two stacked DAMamba blocks per encoder stage (E2–E4), and a convolutional bottleneck (no SSM at the bottleneck), mir-roring the encoder-only placement validated on the compact model (Sec. 3.1).

2.6 Evaluation Protocol 

All results in this paper use the same full-volume evaluation pipeline. For each fold’s best training checkpoint, we reconstruct the complete BraTS volume with sliding-window inference (patch 128 3, overlap 0.5, Gaussian blending). We then compute per-case Dice and HD95 (mm) for ET, TC, and WT under the BraTS-official empty-region convention (both empty → excluded; empty target with non-empty prediction → Dice = 0 ). Reported mean Dice averages ET, TC, and WT per case, then aggregates across cases and folds. This protocol avoids optimistic estimates from slice batches or cropped validation regions. 

## 3 Results 

All experiments in this paper are evaluated on BraTS 2020 (Sec. 2.1). Segmen-tation performance by DAMamba-UNet3D ( ∼5.3 M), SegMamba [6] ( ∼70 M), and DAMamba-L ( ∼70 M) are evaluated under identical splits, augmentation, loss, optimizer, scheduler, and epoch budget. SegMamba is the fixed-scan ToM reference. In ablation studies, we vary block placement, loss, or scan order on the full-volume pipeline. Table 1 summarizes five-fold full-volume performance of DAMamba-UNet3D. Mean Dice is 0.815 ±0.013 , with strong WT ( 0.897 ±0.012 ) and TC ( 0.826 ±0.018 )scores. ET remains hardest ( 0.721 ± 0.014 ) due to small lesion volume. Surface distances are favorable for WT and TC (mean HD95 5.9 mm and 8.4 mm). DAMamba-L surpasses SegMamba at large scale ( 0.829 ±0.012 vs. 0.824 ±0.014 ; +0.5 pt mean Dice), with the largest gain on TC ( 0.842 vs. 0.827 ). All ablations use five folds, 50 epochs, and the full-volume evaluator (Sec. 2.6). Table 3 reports mean Dice across folds. ## 4 Discussion 

Two-scale results. Table 2 supports a dual narrative. DAMamba-UNet3D (∼5.3 M; 0.815 mean Dice) targets parameter-efficient deployment at ∼13 × lower capacity than SegMamba with near-parity. DAMamba-L ( ∼70 M; 0.829 ) sur-passes retrained SegMamba ( 0.824 ) on the same BraTS 2020 protocol, with the clearest improvement on TC (+1.5 pt). 

Encoder-only placement. Component ablations on the compact model show that DAMamba at the bottleneck or decoder lowers Dice (Table 3). DAMamba-L adopts the same encoder-only, convolutional-bottleneck design at SegMamba scale ( 0.829 mean Dice). Placement therefore matters as much as scan type. 

Learned DAS vs. fixed ToM. Scan ablations on the compact model show learned DAS ahead of fixed orders when encoder placement is matched (Table 4). At SegMamba scale, the full DAS-native hybrid exceeds fixed ToM SegMamba. We do not isolate scan protocol from architecture in a single swap experiment, so we state the inference cautiously: on BraTS 2020, learned tri-plane DAS in our hybrid U-Net appears competitive with and may improve upon fixed ToM under identical training and full-volume evaluation. 

Limitations. All experiments use BraTS 2020 only. Despite DAS enabling per-formance in parity with fewer parameter counts than its fixed-scan-based coun-terpart, multi-dataset validation and statistical testing are needed before general claims about DAS versus ToM. 8 Hussain et al.

Broader impact. Regardless of the BraTS scores above, extending DAS to tri-plane 3D volumes and applying it to medical segmentation for the first time is a substantive contribution in its own right. It offers a learnable alternative to fixed scan trajectories that can be reused in other volumetric tasks without redesigning the core operator. In addition, it opens a way to reduce parameter space multi-folds without losing performance significantly. 

## 5 Conclusion 

We presented DAMamba-UNet3D, the first 3D medical extension of Dynamic Adaptive Scan in a parameter-efficient hybrid U-Net (encoder-only DAMamba at E2–E4, ∼5.3 M). On BraTS 2020 it achieves near-parity with retrained Seg-Mamba at ∼13 × lower capacity ( 0.815 ± 0.013 vs. 0.824 ± 0.014 ). At large scale, DAMamba-L with encoder-only DAS and a convolutional bottleneck surpasses SegMamba ( 0.829 ± 0.012 vs. 0.824 ± 0.014 ), with component ablations confirm-ing that encoder-only placement is essential. The combined evidence suggests that learned tri-plane DAS in a hybrid U-Net is a strong alternative to fixed ToM scanning for 3D brain tumor segmentation.
