## Abstract

Hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures have achieved promising performance in medical image segmentation, yet the high computational cost of self-attention limits their application in resource-constrained clinical scenarios. Moreover, medical images exhibit domain-specific challenges, including ultrasound speckle noise, low contrast, and ambiguous lesion boundaries, which require robust feature representation beyond conventional local modeling. To address these issues, we propose RV-Mixer, a lightweight module for efficient global representation learning with medical-domain priors. Instead of explicit attention-based token interaction, RV-Mixer employs Random Fourier Features (RFF) to project local representations into a shared frequency-domain embedding space, providing nonlinear feature enhancement with low computational overhead. Furthermore, Variance-Guided Channel Recalibration (VGCR) introduces global statistical aggregation through channel-wise variance modeling, enabling adaptive emphasis of discriminative pathological textures and lesion boundaries that may be weakened by mean-based feature aggregation. Extensive experiments on five public medical image segmentation datasets, covering ultrasound, endoscopy, dermoscopy, and MRI scenarios, demonstrate that RV-Mixer achieves competitive or superior performance compared with Transformer-, MLP-, and Mamba-based approaches while maintaining fewer parameters and lower computational complexity. These results highlight the effectiveness of domain-informed frequency representation and statistical global aggregation for efficient and robust medical image segmentation.

## Introduction

Medical image segmentation is one of the core tasks in medical image analysis (Shu, Wang, Zhang, Shi, Wu, 2024, Zhang, Jia, Li, Zhang, Shi, Ma, Ying, 2025). Its primary objective is to accurately delineate organs, tissues, or lesion regions within medical scans, thereby providing essential support for clinical diagnosis, treatment planning, and prognosis evaluation (Li, Huang, Wang, Liu, 2025, Liu, Fu, Shi, 2026). In recent years, deep learning-based methods have significantly advanced this field, with convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly UNet (Ronneberger et al., 2015), becoming the mainstream architecture. Variants such as ResUNet (Xiao et al., 2018), Attention UNet (Oktay et al., 2018), and UNet++ (Zhou et al., 2018) have further improved segmentation accuracy and boundary perception. Despite their success, CNNs are inherently limited by the local receptive field of convolutional operations, which constrains their ability to capture long-range dependencies and global contextual information (Chen et al., 2021).

To address this limitation, Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) models-originally proposed for natural language processing-have been introduced into computer vision and medical image analysis due to their strong capability for capturing long-range dependencies (Farooq et al., 2024). Recent works have explored hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures (Guo et al., 2024), embedding Transformer blocks into CNN backbones such as UNet to combine the local inductive bias of CNNs with the global modeling power of Transformers. Representative approaches, including TransUNet (Chen et al., 2021), TransFuse (Zhang et al., 2021), MT (Wang et al., 2022b), and UCTransNet (Wang et al., 2022a), have shown competitive results across various segmentation benchmarks.

However, Transformer-based methods face several practical challenges in medical imaging. Their self-attention mechanism is computationally expensive, resulting in large model sizes and slow inference (Valanarasu and Patel, 2022). Moreover, in scenarios with limited annotated data, high noise, or imaging artifacts, Transformer-based models can underperform traditional CNNs. Lightweight alternatives such as MLP-based models (UNeXt Valanarasu and Patel, 2022, Rolling UNet Liu et al., 2024) and Mamba-based architectures (U-Mamba Ma et al., 2024, VM-UNet Ruan and Xiang, 2024, VM-UNetV2 Zhang et al., 2024) approximate long-range dependencies through token- or channel-mixing and state-space modeling, yet they are largely adapted from natural images and often fail to account for the specific challenges of medical imaging. These include lesion infiltration into surrounding tissues, ambiguous or blurred boundaries, heterogeneous tissue intensities, and prevalent noise or artifacts in modalities such as ultrasound, MRI, and endoscopy (Basumatary, Aborahama, Zhang, Zhang, Zeng, Liu, Zhou, Wang, 2026, Jiang, Liu, Qiu, Zeng, Jin, Li, Lu, He, Zhou, Chen, 2026). Such domain-specific factors substantially increase segmentation difficulty and limit clinical applicability.

To overcome these limitations, we rethink global feature modeling and propose RV-Mixer, a lightweight and efficient global representation module tailored for medical image segmentation. RV-Mixer combines two complementary strategies designed for medical imaging. First, Random Fourier Features (RFF) approximate kernel-induced global feature representations by mapping local features into a shared randomized frequency space. According to kernel approximation theory, the Fourier feature mapping implicitly preserves high-dimensional similarity structures in the transformed feature space, enabling efficient encoding of global structural information without explicitly constructing quadratic token relationships. Second, Variance-Guided Channel Recalibration (VGCR) leverages channel-wise variance as a proxy for discriminative information, allowing the model to emphasize subtle pathological textures, lesion boundaries, and other clinically relevant structures often lost in conventional mean-based aggregation. While high variance could potentially arise from noise, we empirically observe that in medical imaging it is often associated with important structures, such as lesion boundaries, making it a useful, though not formally guaranteed, cue for recalibrating channels. Together, these mechanisms enable RV-Mixer to maintain global structural awareness, enhance robustness to modality-specific degradations, and focus computational resources on lesion-relevant regions. The main contributions of this work are as follows:

*   1.
We propose RV-Mixer, a lightweight module that integrates Random Fourier Features and variance-guided channel recalibration to enhance global feature modeling.

*   2.
We introduce a frequency-domain global representation mechanism based on Random Fourier Features, which approximates kernel-induced feature structures and captures global information.

*   3.
A variance-driven channel recalibration mechanism uses token-wise variance as a proxy for global discriminability, enhancing the model’s focus on salient lesion structures.
