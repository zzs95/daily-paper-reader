Title: MRI‐Based Radiomics Model for Predicting Eligibility for Conservative Management of Endometrial Cancer

URL Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/

Markdown Content:
## [](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/)Abstract

### Objective

Various machine learning and deep learning methods were used to develop and validate radiomics models based on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to predict eligibility for conservative management of endometrial cancer.

### Methods

This retrospective study included 184 patients with early endometrial cancer confirmed by histopathology from two medical centers, who were divided into a training set (_n_ = 123), an internal validation set (_n_ = 31), and an external test set (_n_ = 30). T2 weighted imaging (T2WI), dynamic contrast‐enhanced T1‐weighted imaging (DCE‐T1WI), and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps were used to extract radiomics features. A random forest model was used to select the most important features. Radiomics models were constructed using four machine learning methods including support vector machine (SVM), random forest, decision tree, logistic regression, and one deep learning method that is multilayer perceptron (MLP). They were validated using five‐fold cross validation and tested on the external test set. The performance of the models was evaluated by receiver operating characteristic curves, calibration curves, and clinical decision curves.

### Results

The radiomics model established using MLP showed a certain performance in predicting eligibility for conservative management in early endometrial cancer patients. In the validation set, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was 0.946 (95% CI 0.915–0.973). In the external test set, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was 0.792 (95% CI 0.571–0.965). The calibration curve demonstrated good fit of the MLP model. The results of the DCA indicated that the MLP model had high net benefit in predicting eligibility for conservative management when the threshold probability ranged from 0.1 to 0.9.

### Conclusion

The radiomics model based on MRI can be used to predict eligibility for conservative management in early endometrial cancer patients, providing objective imaging evidence for clinical decision‐making.

**Keywords:**conservative management, endometrial cancer, magnetic resonance imaging, radiomics model

## [](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/)Introduction

1
Endometrial cancer is one of the most common cancers among women. According to global cancer statistics, there were 420,242 newly diagnosed cases of endometrial cancer in 2022 [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0001)]. A proportion of 4.2% of endometrial cancer patients are younger than 40 years of age, and the incidence rate of early‐stage and low‐grade endometrial cancer in young women is rising [[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0002)]. The standard treatment for early endometrial cancer is hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo oophorectomy (with or without lymph node dissection), which may be problematic for women who wish to have children in the future [[3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0003), [4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0004)]. Conservative therapy refers to delaying surgery and first using hormones to treat cancer. Evidence suggests that conservative therapy is feasible and safe in certain situations, but the risk of disease progression is higher than that in patients who undergo surgical treatments [[5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0005)]. Therefore, identifying patients who are suitable for conservative treatment is important. Patients with endometrial cancer must meet the following conditions to undergo conservative therapy: the tumor is endometrioid carcinoma; the tumor does not extend beyond the endometrium, as determined by imaging methods; pathological grade 1 [[6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0006), [7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0007)]. The above criteria are determined by pathological biopsy and imaging examination. On the one hand, pathological biopsy is invasive and affected by the sampling site. On the other hand, imaging tests are influenced by subjective factors. Therefore, new biomarkers need to be developed to accurately determine the eligibility of early endometrial cancer patients for conservative therapy thus bringing hope to women of childbearing age who have not yet fulfilled their desire to become mothers.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is considered an important imaging tool for evaluating endometrial cancer because it provides excellent soft tissue contrast and resolution. MRI can accurately assess the spread of local tumors without requiring exposure to ionizing radiation [[8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0008), [9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0009)]. Radiomics refers to the comprehensive characterization of tumor phenotypes using a large number of quantitative imaging features [[10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0010)]. The radiomics features extracted from MRI images show potential for predicting clinical and pathological findings and may help radiologists and clinicians assess whether endometrial cancer patients meet the criteria for conservative treatment. Radiomics research in endometrial cancer has mainly focused on the stratification of endometrial cancer patients to predict the risk of recurrence and to evaluate patient survival rates [[11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0011), [12](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0012), [13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0013), [14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0014)]. However, there are no studies predicting eligibility for conservative management in patients with early endometrial cancer. In addition, most studies have used one traditional radiomics method, and there are few studies comparing multiple machine learning methods.

The aim of this study was to develop and validate MRI‐based radiomics models to predict eligibility for conservative management of early endometrial cancer. In addition, we examined the utility of different machine learning methods.

## [](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/)Materials and Methods

2
### Patients

2.1
This study was approved by the ethics committees of the medical institutions. Because of the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent from patients was not required. A search of the institutional database between January 2020 and June 2024 identified 223 patients with early endometrial cancer confirmed by surgical pathology. Early endometrial cancer was defined as the tumor confined to the uterus. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Primary early endometrial cancer confirmed by surgical pathology with a complete postoperative pathological report; (2) pelvic MRI examination within 1 month before gynecological surgery; and (3) the patient did not receive tumor treatment before MR examination. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) lack of T2 weighted imaging (T2WI), dynamic contrast‐enhanced T1‐weighted imaging (DCE‐T1WI), or apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) images (_n_ = 29); (2) the lesion was not visible on MRI (_n_ = 7); and (3) poor image quality or significant artifacts affecting the visualization of tumors (_n_ = 3). The detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria and the inclusion process are shown in Figure[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-fig-0001).

Finally, 184 patients were enrolled, including 154 patients from Center A and 30 patients from Center B. This study used a five‐fold cross validation method to randomly divide the patients from Center A into a training cohort (_n_ = 123) and an internal validation cohort (_n_ = 31) at a ratio of 4:1, with the patients from Center B serving as an external test cohort (_n_ = 30).

### Clinicopathological Data

2.2
Clinical and pathological data were collected retrospectively from the medical record system, including age, tumor histopathological type, grade, depth of muscle infiltration, lymphatic vessel space infiltration, lymph node metastasis, and FIGO stage (2023). The grouping criteria were as follows: (1) conservative treatment‐eligible group: endometrioid carcinoma with no muscle infiltration according to imaging examination and pathological grade 1; (2) surgical treatment group: early endometrial cancer patients who did not meet the above conditions.

### MR Image Acquisition

2.3
All patients underwent pelvic MRI scans before surgery. Sagittal T2WI without fat saturation, sagittal DCE‐T1WI, and transverse diffusion‐weighted imaging (DWI) with b = 0 and 800 s/mm 2 were performed. ADC images were automatically calculated by the MRI device software according to the DWI. Gadobenate dimeglumine was used as the contrast agent for multiphase contrast‐enhanced scanning and injected through the median cubital vein at a dose of 0.2 mL/kg and a rate of 2 mL/s. The detailed scanning parameters of different scanners across all participating centers are listed in Table[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-tbl-0001).

### Tumor Segmentation and Repeatability Analysis

2.4
The MR raw images of all patients were exported in DICOM format and then imported into ITK‐SNAP 3.6 software. All tumor contours were manually segmented slice‐by‐slice independently on each sequence (T2WI, DCE‐T1WI, and ADC maps) by an attending physician with more than 5 years of experience in pelvic MRI diagnosis, to ensure that the tumor contours accurately corresponded to the tumor volume on each respective sequence. To assess interobserver consistency, 20 patient images were randomly selected and independently subjected to three‐dimensional segmentation by another radiologist with 20 years of experience in pelvic MRI diagnosis. The segmentation results from the two radiologists were analyzed for consistency using the Bland–Altman method. The remaining images were all reviewed and confirmed case by case by the chief physician to ensure segmentation quality. A detailed tumor segmentation sequence is shown in Figure[2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-fig-0002).

### Feature Extraction

2.5
The PyRadiomics library was used to extract 1524 radiomic features for each VOI from T2WI, ADC, and DCE‐T1WI images. Different image types or filters were used for feature extraction, including Original, Wavelet, Laplacian Gaussian (LoG), Square, SquareRoot, Logarithm, Exponential, Gradient, LocalBinary Pattern 2D, and LocalBinary Pattern 3D.

### Feature Selection

2.6
The extracted feature data were normalized using the _Z_‐score to ensure that feature values were within a similar range, which can improve the performance of the model. Variance Threshold was used to remove low variance features, and the most important features were selected using a random forest model. Finally, to avoid model bias toward the majority class and ensure robust classification performance, we applied the Borderline SMOTE algorithm for data processing. The original sample ratio of the two groups was 34:120, and the dataset was fully balanced to a 1:1 ratio after data augmentation. The Wilcoxon rank sum test was used to evaluate the differences in selected features between the conservative treatment‐eligible group and surgical treatment group.

### Model Establishment and Evaluation

2.7
Five models including support vector machine (SVM), random forest, decision tree, logistic regression, and multilayer perceptron (MLP) were constructed for radiomic feature training. Nested cross‐validation was adopted for model development. In the outer loop, five‐fold cross‐validation was applied, and the internal dataset was split into training folds and validation folds at a ratio of 4:1 to evaluate the model's generalization performance on internal data. In the inner loop, threefold cross‐validation was performed within each outer training fold, and grid search was used to determine the optimal hyperparameters for each model. Model testing was subsequently conducted using the external test set.

Next, the predictive performance of the established model was calibrated and evaluated for clinical application. The receiver operating characteristics (ROC) area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and precision were calculated. The 95% confidence interval (CI) of the AUC was determined by bootstrap resampling and repeated 1000 times. The DeLong test was used to compare the differences in AUC values between different models. Calibration curves were plotted to evaluate the consistency of the model. Finally, decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed on the entire dataset, and the clinical utility of the prediction model was estimated by calculating the net benefit of the threshold probability interval.

### Statistical Analysis

2.8
Independent sample _t_‐tests, chi square tests, Fisher's exact tests, and Wilcoxon rank sum tests were used to analyze the differences in clinicopathological and radiomics features between the conservative treatment‐eligible group and surgical treatment group. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS23.0 software. A two‐tailed _p_ value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Other data analyses and processing were performed using Python (version 3.7).

## [](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/)Discussion

4
In this study, we established five radiomics models based on multi‐sequence MRI to predict eligibility for conservative management of endometrial cancer. The MLP model showed the best performance in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical practicality. We selected a relatively large dataset comprising 184 early endometrial cancer patients from two centers. For patients from center A, a five‐fold cross‐validation method was adopted, while center B was used as an independent external test set. Different scanners were utilized at center A and center B, resulting in MR images with varying contrast and quality. Nevertheless, the MLP radiomics model still exhibited moderate performance after being tested on datasets acquired by different scanners. This indicates that the model is reliable to a certain degree [[15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0015), [16](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0016)].

These models extracted 1524 features from T2WI, DCE‐T1WI, and ADC images, including first‐order features, shape‐based features, and texture features. The features that contributed the most to the model were from T2WI sequences, which is consistent with the findings of Otani et al. [[17](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0017)]. Han et al. [[18](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0018)] reported that the combination of T2WI and DWI improved the diagnostic performance of the model, which allows healthcare providers to make accurate diagnoses without the need for injection of contrast agents. The GLSZM feature, which contributed the most to the model, is a common feature in the construction of radiomics models that is important for multiple studies [[19](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0019), [20](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0020), [21](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0021)].

In this study, we compared four machine learning and deep learning models, including SVM, random forest, decision tree, logistic regression, and MLP. MLP showed relatively better performance, with an AUC of 0.946 in the validation set and 0.792 in the external test set, suggesting its reference value for application in clinical practice. The logistic regression method used in this study is commonly used for constructing radiomics models, and the efficacy of logistic regression models has been demonstrated [[22](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0022)]. The random forest algorithm is a nonparametric method that is highly suitable for analyzing high‐dimensional data, such as radiomics data[[23](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0023), [24](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0024)]. Models constructed using random forest methods have shown good robustness [[25](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0025)]. Zheng et al. [[26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0026)] compared logistic regression, random forest, and SVM models, and the results showed that SVM exhibited higher prediction efficiency and robustness. MLP is a deep learning model based on feed forward neural networks that can fit complex nonlinear relationships. Currently, there are few studies using this algorithm. In this study, MLP showed the best performance, which is consistent with the results of Huang et al. [[27](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0027)] This provides a basis for improving the role of the modeling method in the future. The calibration curve showed a good fit of the MLP model. DCA indicated that when the threshold probability is 0.1–0.9, the MLP model combined with T2WI, ADC, and DCE‐T1WI is more effective than the “conservative all” or “surgical all” schemes.

Previous studies performed preoperative risk stratification based on the pathological characteristics of endometrial cancer patients [[28](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0028), [29](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0029), [30](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0030)]. In the past 2 years, the postoperative prognosis and recurrence of endometrial cancer patients have been the focus of attention [[31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0031), [32](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0032)]. For patients who have not yet reached their family‐building goals, the consequences of surgical treatment are serious. This study recognizes the importance of conservative therapy and provides a reliable imaging‐based strategy to help women of childbearing age make conservative management decisions. A novel molecular classification system for risk assessment of endometrial cancer that categorizes endometrial cancer patients based on mismatch repair defects, polar exonuclease domain mutations, and p53 expression has been proposed. The goal is to use precision medicine to provide optimal treatment recommendations for patients. However, the application of this classification system to patients seeking conservative management remains to be explored. The use of this system has not been clarified by current research [[33](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0033), [34](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489816/#cam472141-bib-0034)].

This study had several limitations. First, the enrolled patients in this study were generally older, showing an age mismatch with the target population seeking fertility‐sparing treatment. Due to the epidemiological features of the disease, cases of childbearing age were insufficient, which limited the model's applicability in this group. As a preliminary exploratory finding, this model is not ready for direct clinical application. We will cooperate with multiple centers to recruit more young patients of childbearing age for further model optimization and validation. Second, the feature extraction process requires manual segmentation of tumors on several image slices, which is a time‐consuming process. The rapid development of deep learning techniques has resulted in the proposal of several methods for automatic segmentation of medical images that could be adopted in the near future. Third, this study excluded cases with non‐visualizable lesions on magnetic resonance imaging and did not conduct a comparative analysis with patients with noncancerous endometrial diseases, which to a certain extent limits the generalizability of the study findings. In addition, although the model retains certain predictive capacity, its generalizability across different imaging devices and patient cohorts is limited. This is a common challenge for radiomics models, and we will strive to address this issue in future studies. Finally, the standards for determining whether conservative treatment is applicable will be defined by expert consensus and guidelines. In future work, we will conduct systematic long‐term follow‐up of patients receiving conservative treatment, collecting clinical outcomes such as pregnancy rate, postoperative recurrence rate, and progression‐free survival. This will help further validate the clinical performance of the model and facilitate its practical use in clinical decision‐making. In summary, radiomics models based on multi‐sequence MRI can be used to predict eligibility for conservative management of endometrial cancer. This provides objective imaging evidence for clinical decision‐making.
