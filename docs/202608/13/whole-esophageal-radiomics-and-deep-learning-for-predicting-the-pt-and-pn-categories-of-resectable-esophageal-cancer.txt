Abstract 

Objectives: To investigate the application of whole-esophageal radiomics and deep learning features derived from enhanced arterial phase CT images along with clinical data to differentiate the pT and pN categories in patients with resectable esophageal cancer. 

Methods Clinical and CT data of 625 patients from Center 1 and 56 patients from Center 2 with esophageal cancer were analyzed. The dataset of Center 1 was randomly divided into a training cohort and an internal testing cohort at an 8:2 ratio. The whole-esophageal ROIs were delineated semi-automatically. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator algorithm was employed for radiomics and deep learning feature reduction and selection, leading to the development of Radscore and Deepscore. Clinical, radiomics, deep learning, and combined models were constructed. The diagnostic performance of the models was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. 

Results In the training cohort for predicting pT categories, the AUC of the combined model was 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS 0.901, which was significantly higher than that of the clinical (0.869), radiomics (0.833), and deep learning (0.830) models. For predicting pN categories, the AUC of the combined model was 0.800, significantly higher than that of the clinical (0.748), radiomics (0.748), and deep learning (0.764) models. Calibration curves also demonstrated good agreement between predicted and actual status, and the decision curve analysis further confirmed its clinical applicability. 

Conclusion The combined model offers the most reliable method for predicting thepT and pN categories in patients with resectable esophageal cancer, thereby beneficial to treatment planning. 

Keywords: resectable esophageal cancer, deep learning, radiomics, CT imaging, TNM stage 

Introduction 

Esophageal cancer is a common malignancy of the digestive tract, ranking as the seventh most common cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide 1. Its major histological subtypes include squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, and small-cell carcinoma 2. Squamous cell carcinoma predominates in Eastern countries, whereas adenocarcinoma is more prevalent in Western countries 3. Despite substantial progress in esophageal cancer treatment, the overall prognosis remains poor, with a 5-year survival rate of less than 25% 4.Early diagnosis and accurate staging are crucial for treatment planning, prognostic evaluation, and improving survival. Endoscopic or surgical resection is typically recommended for early-stage patients (cT1-2N0M0). For locally advanced stages (cT3-4aN0, T1-4aN1M0), treatment decisions include surgery, neoadjuvant therapy, or definitive chemoradiotherapy. Patients with distant metastasis or unresectable lesions are generally managed with palliative 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS therapy, such as chemotherapy and supportive care 5,6 . Resectable esophageal cancer mainly includes tumors infiltrating the submucosa (T1b) or deeper, for which surgery is indicated. Although multiple or multistation nodal metastases are a relative contraindication, patients with regional nodal involvement (N1) and T1–T3 tumors may still undergo resection depending on their age and physical condition. Tumors invading adjacent structures such as the pleura, pericardium, or diaphragm (T4a) are also considered resectable 7.Preoperative clinical tumor node metastasis staging (cTNM) relies primarily on cross-sectional imaging, with contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) being widely used. However, CT often struggles to accurately assess the depth of wall invasion and lymph node metastasis because of its limited soft-tissue resolution. As a result, conventional visual assessment frequently leads to discrepancies between cTNM staging and true tumor burden 8.Pathologic tumor node metastasis staging (pTNM), the gold standard, can only be obtained post-resection. Radiomics extracts quantitative features from images that reflect intratumoral heterogeneity and biological characteristics beyond visual perception, providing a new perspective for diagnosis, precise staging, and prognostic evaluation 9-11 . Deep learning can automatically learn high-level abstract features from imaging data because of its powerful capacity for complex image representation through its hierarchical neural network architecture 12-14 . Combining radiomics and deep learning to mine CT features associated with pTNM stage may therefore enable accurate, noninvasive preoperative prediction for resectable esophageal cancer and facilitate clinical decision-making. While previous studies have used radiomics or deep learning to predict pT or pN stage, most have relied on manually delineated tumor-level regions of interest. This study evaluated

Methods 

1.1.1 Patients 

This retrospective study included patients with resectable esophageal cancer treated at Center 1 and Center 2 from January 2025 to December 2025. Patients were included if they had complete clinical data, a clinical diagnosis of resectable esophageal cancer, a contrast-enhanced chest CT performed within two weeks before surgery, and postoperative pathological confirmation enabling pTNM stage. The exclusion criteria included poor-quality images unsuitable for region of interest delineation, the presence of multiple lesions or concurrent malignancies, and any prior surgery or chemoradiotherapy before the CT examination. A total of 625 patients from Center 1 and 56 from Center 2 were included. Pathologic staging was performed according to the AJCC 8th edition 15 . In Center 1, 280 patients were classified as pT1-2(pT1=133, pT2=147) and 345 patients as pT3-4(indivisible); in Center 2, 26 patients were pT1-2(pT1=12, pT2=14) and 30 patients were pT3-4(indivisible). In Center 1, 375 patients were classified as pN0 and 250 patients as pN1-3(pN1=91, pN2=136, pN3=23); in Center 2, 36 patients were pN0 and 20 patients were pN1-3(pN1=7, pN2=11, pN3=2).The patient inclusion flowchart is presented in Figure 1. 

1.1.2 CT Acquisition 

CT scans were acquired on a Siemens Somatom Definition Flash dual-source CT system with patients positioned supine and head-first. The scan range extended from the hard palate to 3 cm below the costophrenic angle. The acquisition parameters were 120 kV, 250 mA, a 5-mm slice thickness, and a 5-mm reconstruction interval. Following a fast of at least four hours, 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS patients received breath-hold training. A 75 mL bolus of iopromide (350 mgI/mL) was administered intravenously at 2.5 mL/s, with arterial-phase imaging initiated 25–30 seconds after injection. Arterial-phase images, reconstructed at a thickness of 5 mm, were used for CT feature assessment and region-of-interest (ROI) delineation. All patients provided written informed consent for contrast administration. 

1.2 Study methods 1.2.1 General CT Features Assessment 

Two radiologists with 5 and 8 years of experience (author 1 and author 2, respectively) jointly reviewed the CT images and resolved any disagreements through consensus. The CT features assessed included tumor location 16 , maximum tumor thickness, length, and the presence of esophageal dilation (exceeding 2 cm). Additional evaluated characteristics included boundaries, clarity of the surrounding fat gap, enhancement pattern, and lymph node enlargement (short-axis diameter greater than 10 mm). 

1.2.2 Image Segmentation 

Esophageal ROI delineation is challenging because of the organ’s thin wall (2–4 mm) and proximity to complex anatomy. Consequently, we employed a semiautomatic segmentation approach with subsequent manual correction. Arterial-phase DICOM images were exported from the picture archiving and communication system (PACS), and two radiologists with 5 and 8 years of experience independently delineated the entire esophagus using ITK-SNAP (v4.2.1) under mediastinal window settings (window level 30–50 HU and window width 300–400 HU). Inter- and intra-observer consistency was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). We randomly selected 100 cases, calculated ICC of all extracted radiomic features between two radiologists’ segmentations; only features with ICC>0.75 were retained (already 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS mentioned in original manuscript), and we added the median ICC of all selected features (median ICC=0.886). Segmentation was performed using a VB-Net-based cascaded network 17,18 , which integrates a bottleneck structure to preserve accuracy while minimizing parameters. Preprocessing involved voxel-spacing normalization via nearest-neighbor interpolation and resampling to an isotropic 1×1×1 mm³ resolution. Optimization uses a Dice loss function. The corresponding segmentation performance metrics are provided in Supplement S1. 

1.2.3 Radiomics Modeling 

Radiomic analysis was performed using the uAI Research Portal (United Imaging Intelligence). Thirteen filters were applied to extract 2,264 radiomic features per patient, comprising first-order statistics, shape, GLCM, GLSZM, GLRLM, NGTDM, and GLDM features. These features were normalized using Z-score transformation. An intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analysis was conducted on 100 randomly selected cases, and features with an ICC greater than 0.75 were retained. Feature selection was performed using a two-stage strategy. First, minimum-redundancy maximum-relevance (mRMR) was applied to eliminate redundant variables and retain the most informative candidates. Subsequently, these mRMR-filtered features were entered into a least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model with fivefold cross-validation to determine the optimal λ and select predictors with nonzero coefficients. Radscores were subsequently constructed for pT and pN categories. Further details are available in Supplements S2 and S3. 

1.2.4 Deep Learning Model 

A 3D ResNet-18 model implemented in PyTorch 2.1. Input patches were cropped around the esophagus region and expanded by 10 mm to incorporate contextual information before 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS being resized to 128×128×128 voxels. All convolution layers employed 3×3×3 kernels with batch normalization and ReLU activation. Training proceeded for 300 epochs with a batch size of 16 and a learning rate of 3×10⁻⁴. A final adaptive average pooling layer produced a 7×7×512-dimensional feature vector, termed the Deepscore. Loss curves, early-stopping strategy, data augmentation methods were taken to prevent from overfitting. Data augmentation: We added random elastic deformation, random rotation (± 15 °), random horizontal flipping, and Gaussian noise injection during the training process; Early stop rule: When the validation accuracy does not decrease for 30 consecutive epochs, the training stops to prevent overfitting; Loss curve: Added training/validation loss and epoch curve as Supplements S4-5; Multi random seed stability test: We trained 3D ResNet with three different random seeds and calculated the intra class correlation coefficient (ICC=0.912) of the extracted Deepscore, proving that the feature output is stable regardless of random initialization. 

1.2.5 Model Construction and Visualization 

Four models were developed for pT and pN categories: clinical, radiomics, deep learning, and a combined model. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression identified significant clinical predictors (P ≤ 0.05). The combined model integrated clinical data, CT features, the Radscore, and the Deepscore through linear weighting, and a nomogram was subsequently generated. Pearson correlation matrix and Variance Inflation Factor(VIF) analysis among clinical predictors, Radscore, and Deepscore were used to assess model stability. Subgroup analyses was performed to test whether model performance is consistent across clinically relevant subgroups. For model visualization, gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) and feature maps were employed to highlight regions relevant to the decision-making process. The overall

1.3 Statistical Analysis 

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 and R 4.4.0. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation and were compared with t-tests, whereas skewed variables are reported as the median (interquartile range) and were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed with χ² tests. We evaluated diagnostic performance by calculating receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy. AUC values were compared via DeLong tests. All AUC values reported for the external cohort are bootstrap-corrected with corresponding adjusted 95% CIs. Model calibration and clinical utility were further assessed using calibration curves and decision curve analysis. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion 

Esophageal cancer remains a major global health challenge, and most patients present with advanced disease, leading to a poor prognosis despite therapeutic advances 19 . Accurate preoperative staging is crucial, as patients with early-stage disease can achieve a 5-year survival rate of 95% following appropriate treatment 20 . Conventional clinical TNM (cTNM) staging based on CT, however, is limited by poor soft-tissue resolution, which complicates accurate assessment. Although the treatment of esophageal cancer is complex and controversial, the treatment of esophageal cancer primarily depends on its TNM staging. Incorrect clinical assessment may mislead clinicians in making erroneous treatment decisions, thereby affecting the treatment outcome and survival benefits of patients. According to treatment guideline of esophageal carcinoma 5,6 , if a patient meets both pT1-2 and pN0 criteria, they are very likely to be suitable for endoscopic or surgical resection alone; if these criteria are not met simultaneously, the patient should not undergo surgery directly but should take preoperative radiotherapy, 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy. By our study, we found the combined model had certain reference value for clinical treatment decisions. In this study, clinical and general CT features demonstrated reasonable discriminatory power for pT and pN categories. Maximum tumor thickness, surrounding fat gap blurring, and unclear margins were independent predictors of a higher pT categories, which is consistent with deeper invasion and periesophageal infiltration 21,22 . In terms of pN categories, tumor thickness and lymph node enlargement were significant predictors. Here we need to talk about the biological and radiological rationale for predicting nodal metastasis using whole-esophageal ROIs. Lymphatic metastasis is just one of the states we need to predict. It may not only be related to the state of the lymph nodes themselves, but also to the state of the primary tumor. Yunlin Zheng 23 et al. constructed a model using the method of delineating gastric cancer lesions to predict lymph node metastasis after neoadjuvant chemotherapy for locally advanced gastric cancer. Radiomics and deep learning have been extensively applied to esophageal cancer staging. Wu et al. reported an AUC of 0.795 using tumor-level radiomics 24 . Tan et al. reported that 3D radiomics could predict TNM stage in patients with resectable squamous cell carcinoma 25 .Notably, our study used whole-esophagus ROIs, a strategy that improves robustness when tumor boundaries are indistinct or when early lesions are small. Similar full-organ ROI approaches have proven successful for other tumors, such as thyroid nodules 26 and expanded tumor ROIs in glioma radiomics 27 .Radiomics features capture a wide range of characteristics, including shape, first-order statistics, texture, and wavelet features, which reflect underlying tumor heterogeneity and microstructural complexity. Deep learning further extracts high-level abstract features, 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS contributing to strong predictive performance 28-31 .The combined model, which integrates clinical variables, CT features, radiomics, and deep learning features, demonstrated the best performance across all datasets and significantly outperformed the individual models (P < 0.05). In this study, the pathological concordance rate of the cT categories was 0.734(367/500) in training cohort and the pathological concordance rate of the cN categories was 0.626(313/500). The clinical model had sensitivity, specificity and accuracy of 0.772, 0.808 and 0.788 in predicting pT categories, while the combined model improved these metrics to 0.873, 0.786 and 0.834, respectively. To predict pN categories, the clinical model had sensitivity, specificity and accuracy of 0.670, 0.647 and 0.656, while the combined model improved these metrics to 0.715, 0.740 and 0.730, respectively. This finding indicates that multimodal feature integration enhances model robustness and generalizability. Nomograms can be used to visualize abstract models, which helps simplify model interpretation and enables clinicians to quickly assess patient risk 32,33 . By DCA net benefit curves, we defined two clinically critical threshold probability ranges: low risk threshold (patients with low probability of deep invasion/lymph node metastasis, suitable for direct endoscopic or surgical resection) and high risk threshold (patients with high probability of pT3-4/pN1-3 disease, who should receive neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery). We concluded that the combined model maintained the highest net benefit within the full clinical threshold range, demonstrating its value for individualized surgical/neoadjuvant treatment decision-making. The visualization of the model employed Grad-CAM as well, which aids in lesion detection and observation of feature distribution. As shown in Figure 8, all lesions in the images present as localized thickening of the esophageal wall. In some cases, it is difficult to determine the specific extent of infiltration with the naked eye alone leading to the incorrect cT staging. However, 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS feature visualization can reveal differences in the overall distribution of these features across the esophagus. By comparing these differences, it is possible to predict the pT categories of esophageal cancer. Such as in case a (Figure 8), the significant thickening of the local esophageal wall and blurred periesophageal fat misled to a cT stage of T3 but the postoperative pathological staging (pT) was T2. Our combined model predicted a result of T1-2. In case d (Figure 8), the lesion was located in the mid-esophagus at a site of physiological narrowing, where surrounding structures exerted considerable pressure on the esophagus, making it difficult to assess the depth of tumor invasion. The estimated stage ranged between T2 and T3 and cT stage assigned T2, but the final pathological result was pT3, while our combined model provided the correct prediction. These cases demonstrate that our model can integrate more comprehensive information, eliminate the influence of individual subjective judgments on the T categories and avoid over-treatment or under-treatment in complex clinical scenarios. Our model also demonstrates high value in predicting the N categories. For case a,b(Figure 9), the preoperative CT scan showed no enlargement of the regional lymph nodes, but the postoperative pathology demonstrated regional lymph node metastasis. The combined model indicated a high probability of lymph node metastasis. For case c,d(Figure 9), the preoperative CT revealed regional lymph node enlargement, and the clinical suspicion was of metastasis. However, the postoperative pathology indicated no lymph node metastasis. The combined model accurately predicted low probability of lymph node metastasis in these cases. It demonstrates that the combined model has the ability to enhance the accuracy of lymph node metastasis prediction. This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the analysis relied solely on arterial-phase CT images. Although this phase is routinely used in clinical practice, incorporating multiphase acquisitions might reveal additional perfusion or morphologic 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS information that could further enhance diagnostic or prognostic accuracy. Multimodal imaging studies can validate image-derived signatures across acquisition settings 34 . Second, all CT data were reconstructed using a 5-mm slice thickness. While adequate for standard interpretation, thicker slices may compromise spatial resolution and constrain radiomic feature diversity. Employing thin-section reconstructions could yield more detailed structural information and potentially improve model robustness. Third, the retrospective design introduces the possibility of selection bias, as patient inclusion and imaging parameters were not controlled prospectively. Future studies with prospective enrollment and standardized imaging protocols are required to validate the reproducibility of these results. Fourth, only two categories, pT1-2 and pT3-4, were studied. Due to the reasons such as the number of cases, further differentiation was not possible. Fifth, the external validation currently relies on a single small center. Sixth, the lack of direct comparison with tumor-centered segmentation remains a limitation of the present study and should be validated in future investigations. Besides, the modeling method is rather simple and conventional and fails to take advanced methods such as interpretable multimodal modeling 35 .Finally, the cohort consisted exclusively of Asian patients. In subgroup stratified by histological type, the limited sample size of non-squamous carcinoma cases leads to low model performance. 

This demographic homogeneity may limit the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic groups. External validation using multicenter and multiethnic datasets is essential to confirm the broad applicability of the proposed approach. 

Conclusion 

This study demonstrated that a combined model integrating clinical features, CT findings, radiomics, and deep learning accurately predicts pTNM stage in patients with esophageal cancer, offering a valuable noninvasive tool to guide individualized treatment planning.
