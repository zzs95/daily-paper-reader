Conclusion. Zero-shot contrastive scoring with two open 3D-CT VLMs at 

n = 96 NSCLC-Radiogenomics produces no cell that clears the pre-registered gate. But this is a limit of the zero-shot access mode , not of the features: a supervised linear probe on the same Merlin features recovers a gate-clearing EGFR signal (AUC 0.705 –0.748 , permutation p < 0.05 ) that the contrastive prompting cannot reach. We localise this failure geometrically: the supervised EGFR-discriminative direction is near-orthogonal to the single text-prompt axis the contrastive score projects onto ( cos ≈ 0.02 , at the random-direction null), so removing that axis from the embeddings leaves the gate-clearing EGFR signal intact. Open 3D-CT VLM features carry decodable EGFR signal, but zero-shot contrastive prompting at n = 96 does not access it because the prompt axis and the discriminative axis are misaligned.

21 Introduction 

EGFR and KRAS mutation status in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) guides therapeutic decisions but requires invasive tissue biopsy or circulating-tumour-DNA assay. A non-invasive CT-derived predictor would be useful for triage and longitudi-nal follow-up, particularly for patients without accessible tissue. Recent open 3D-CT vision-language models (VLMs) raise a methodological possibility for the very-small-cohort regime that dominates radiogenomic research: zero-shot biomarker scoring via contrastive image-text alignment, requiring no labelled training on the target cohort. This work asks whether two publicly released radiology VLMs can clear a pre-registered biomarker prediction gate under zero-shot prompting at n = 96 .The originally specified first-choice VLM (CT-CHAT / CT-CLIP [3]) was found in a Phase 0 inventory to have no released weights reachable from this workspace via a HuggingFace handle with token authentication and a permissive licence; the sibling GenerateCT repository is on HuggingFace, but the CT-CHAT and CT-CLIP weights are distributed through a Google-Drive / GitHub-release pattern that does not satisfy this program’s reproducibility requirement. We therefore pivoted to two open alternatives that are on HuggingFace under permissive licences: 

• Merlin [1]: a 3D-inflated ResNet-152 [4] + Clinical-Longformer CLIP-style archi-tecture originally trained on abdominal CT volumes paired with radiology reports (applied here out-of-domain to chest CT). The default-mode forward returns 512-d projected image features and 512-d projected text features in a shared contrastive space [5], with a learned logit-scale parameter. 

• BiomedCLIP [2]: a 2D ViT-B/16 + PubMedBERT contrastive model trained on a 15M biomedical image-text corpus, used here as a 2D key-axial-slice sanity comparator and not expected to outperform a 3D model on volumetric biomarker tasks. The pivot retains the protocol of the original brief (zero-shot prompting, AUC-vs-supervised comparison, pre-registered confounder check). CT-CHAT remains a conditional third arm should the weights become accessible through direct correspon-dence with the original authors. Figure 1 shows the cohorts, the model pivot, and the two scoring modes. We pre-registered a smoking-confounder probe as Phase 1: on a post-SSL CT-FM substrate of this n = 96 trimodal internal cohort, smoking_never status is predictable from CT-derived features at AUC > 0.70 , at or above the within-cohort biomarker AUC, which on that substrate would foreclose pure-biomarker claims. We later re-run the identical probe directly on the benchmarked Merlin / BiomedCLIP features (Section 3.6, Table 4) and find the ordering does not transfer (on Merlin, EGFR signal exceeds smoking signal), an important caveat on cross-substrate confounder reasoning that we develop in the results. # 2 Related Work 

Contrastive vision-language pretraining. 

CLIP demonstrated that contrastive image-text pretraining over web-scale pairs yields a shared embedding space supporting zero-shot transfer by scoring an image against natural-language class prompts [5]. The zero-shot contrastive protocol used here ( L2-normalised image and prompt embeddings, a softmax over a (positive, negative) prompt pair scaled by a learned logit temperature) is the direct medical-imaging instantiation of that recipe. The dependence of the resulting score on prompt phrasing motivates the two-prompt ( bare , cot_brief ) comparison. 4Vision-language foundation models for medicine. 

The generalist-medical-AI agenda argues that self-supervised models trained on large multimodal corpora can be applied to new tasks with little or no task-specific labelling [6]. In the contrastive-zero-shot branch, CheXzero showed expert-level detection of chest-radiograph pathologies with no explicit labels, generalising to unan-notated findings and external institutions [7], and BiomedCLIP scaled the recipe to a 15M biomedical image-text corpus [2]. In computational pathology, PLIP [8] and CONCH [9] report strong zero-shot histology classification from contrastive caption-ing. These successes are on tasks whose visual signature is directly described in the paired text (e.g. effusion, tumour grade); whether the same mechanism transfers to molecular endpoints absent from radiology reports is exactly the open question this work probes. 

3D-CT vision-language models. 

Most medical VLMs operate on 2D slices or radiographs; volumetric CT poses a distinct modelling problem. Merlin pairs a 3D-inflated image encoder with a Clinical-Longformer text encoder trained on report-paired abdominal CT volumes [1], and is one of the two open 3D-CT VLMs evaluated here. CT-CLIP / CT-CHAT trains a contrastive model on the CT-RATE corpus of report-paired chest CT volumes and reports supervised-level zero-shot multi-abnormality detection [3]; its weights were not reachable under this program’s reproducibility constraints, motivating the pivot. RadFM extends generalist radiology modelling across web-scale 2D and 3D data and a broad task suite [10]. Our 2D BiomedCLIP comparator quantifies how much a key-slice 2D model loses against a true volumetric encoder on these targets. 

Radiogenomics and CT-based mutation prediction. 

The premise that tumour phenotype is partially encoded in routine imaging under-lies radiomics: hand-crafted CT features carry prognostic and gene-expression-linked signal across lung and head-and-neck cancers [11]. For NSCLC specifically, quantita-tive PET/CT metrics have been associated with EGFR and KRAS status [12], and deep-learning models report high EGFR-prediction AUC in lung adenocarcinoma pre-senting as ground-glass opacity [13]. Reported performances vary widely with cohort selection, label prevalence, and the inclusion of clinical covariates such as smoking history, which is itself strongly correlated with EGFR status, a confound this work makes explicit through a pre-registered Phase 1 probe. The NSCLC-Radiogenomics collection [14] provides the paired CT, mask, RNA-seq, and clinical data used here. 

Reproducibility and pre-registration. 

Surveys of machine learning for medical imaging document recurring methodological failures (data leakage, selective reporting, and over-optimistic small-cohort estimates) that inflate apparent performance [15]. Pre-registration of the analysis plan, gate, and outcomes before examining results limits these degrees of freedom and supports publication of negative findings. 3.4 Zero-shot prompt strategies 

For each binary target, two prompt strategies, each a (positive, negative) prompt pair: 

• bare : direct disease-statement positive vs wild-type / low-expression negative. Example for EGFR: (“the patient has an EGFR-mutant lung tumor”, “the patient has an EGFR wild-type lung tumor”). 

• cot_brief : short rationale-style positive vs negative prefix with imaging-feature cues commonly cited in the radiogenomic literature. Example for EGFR: (“imaging 6features suggest an EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma with ground-glass opacity and pleural retraction”, “imaging features suggest an EGFR wild-type lung tumor with smoking-related solid consolidation”). Per target the two prompts are tokenised, encoded by the VLM’s text encoder (Clinical-Longformer for Merlin, PubMedBERT for BiomedCLIP), L2-normalised, and concatenated into a (2 , 512) text-feature matrix. The same is done for each patient’s image features (1 , 512) . The contrastive logit matrix is (image · text ⊤) ·

exp(logit _scale) , and the zero-shot probability of the positive prompt is the softmax over the (positive, negative) pair. Figure 3 shows the zero-shot and supervised scoring pipeline.  

> Fig. 3 Zero-shot and supervised scoring pipeline. Each patient’s CT volume and lesion mask yield a 512-d Merlin / BiomedCLIP feature vector, scored both by zero-shot cosine similarity to a (positive, negative) prompt pair and by a supervised probe (linear / ridge / tiny-MLP), each compared to the per-target gate.

4.4 Zero-shot vs supervised-frozen-feature comparison 

Tables 5 and 6 compare each zero-shot cell to a supervised tiny-MLP baseline on the same frozen image features, and Table 4 adds supervised linear / ridge probes on those same features. The supervised access mode extracts substantially more EGFR signal than zero-shot prompting, and the probe family matters: the tiny-MLP head reaches only 0.622 (internal EGFR), but a supervised linear probe on the identical Merlin features reaches 0.748 [0 .627 , 0.855] and a ridge probe 0.705 [0 .567 , 0.833] , both clear-ing the pre-registered gate, with a 1000 -shuffle permutation null confirming the signal (linear p = 0 .010 , z = 2 .53 ; ridge p = 0 .015 , z = 2 .14 ). The tiny-MLP under-probes at n = 96 (a 64-32-1 head with an early-stopping validation split overfits this sample 10 size); the linear probe is the appropriate supervised baseline and shows the Mer-lin features carry a decodable, gate-clearing EGFR signal that zero-shot contrastive prompting ( 0.599 ) does not access. This reframes the zero-shot null as a limitation of the access mode, not of feature quality. A second consequence concerns the smoking confounder. On these benchmarked Merlin features the supervised EGFR AUC ( 0.748 ) exceeds smoking_never AUC (0.603 /0.588 , Table 4), the reverse of the CT-FM proxy ordering used to pre-register the foreclosure. The smoking-confound mechanism therefore does not explain the Mer-lin EGFR signal: it is substrate-specific. On external EGFR the supervised tiny-MLP reaches 0.707 while zero-shot sits at chance, but a smoking-stratified split of that cell rests on a stratum with only 3 EGFR + patients ( n = 21 , smoking_never = 0 ), too few to interpret as an AUC; we therefore report the external supervised number without attributing it to a smoking stratum. 12 5 Discussion 

5.1 What the headline result means 

Zero-shot contrastive scoring with two open 3D-CT VLMs (Merlin, BiomedCLIP) at 

n = 96 on the internal NSCLC-Radiogenomics cohort produces no cell that clears the pre-registered gate of AUC > 0.65 with bootstrap lower CI > 0.55 and external replication. The pre-registered Outcome C (zero-shot null) is the headline. Figure 7 summarises the outcome.     

> Fig. 7 Results summary. Zero-shot prompting clears no cell (Outcome C), but a supervised linear probe on the same Merlin features clears the EGFR gate (AUC 0.748 , permutation p= 0 .010 ) and EGFR signal exceeds smoking on those features (Outcome D does not hold there): the features carry EGFR signal the zero-shot access mode does not reach.

Two pre-registered claims are revised by the on-substrate analysis: Outcome D (smoking-confound dominates) does not hold on the benchmarked Merlin features, where supervised EGFR AUC ( 0.748 ) exceeds smoking_never AUC ( 0.603 ); and the zero-shot null is not a feature-quality ceiling, because a supervised linear probe on the same features clears the EGFR gate. The honest framing is a reproducible small-cohort point estimate (not an upper bound, since the EGFR bootstrap CI reaches 

0.75 ) for zero-shot biomarker prediction with publicly released radiology VLMs at this regime: approximately 0.55 –0.60 AUC on EGFR / KRAS, near chance on the speculative mRNA-high targets. 

5.2 Why prompt rationales matter only for STK11_high 

The cot_brief rationale-style prompts beat bare disease statements on most cells, but only one cell (STK11_high / Merlin) approaches the gate. The improvement is consistent with the contrastive-score literature: longer, more descriptive prompts 13 shift the text-embedding axis toward the radiology-report territory the VLM was pretrained on. But the magnitude ( 0.05 –0.15 AUC) is small and the floor is too low: on EGFR even the best prompt strategy reaches 0.599 , well below the 0.65 gate. The zero-shot null on EGFR / KRAS is robust across the two prompt strategies tested; a fuller prompt-ensemble sensitivity is left to future work, but the floor is far enough below the gate that it is unlikely to be closed by prompt wording alone. 

5.3 The features carry EGFR signal that zero-shot prompting cannot access 

The decisive within-cohort comparison is between access modes on the same frozen Merlin features. Zero-shot contrastive prompting reaches EGFR AUC 0.599 ; a super-vised linear probe on the identical features reaches 0.748 ([0 .627 , 0.855] , permutation 

p = 0 .010 ) and a ridge probe 0.705 ([0 .567 , 0.833] , p = 0 .015 ), both clearing the gate. The earlier tiny-MLP baseline ( 0.622 ) sat close to the zero-shot value, which had suggested the supervised head “adds nothing”; the linear / ridge probes show that conclusion was an artefact of an over-parameterised head at n = 96 , not a property of the features. The Merlin features therefore do encode a gate-clearing EGFR sig-nal; the zero-shot contrastive score, restricted to the cosine similarity between the image embedding and two hand-written text prompts, simply does not project onto that signal (Section 5.4 makes this precise: the EGFR-discriminative direction is near-orthogonal to the text-prompt axis). This is the paper’s sharpest methodological point: at small n, a zero-shot null with an open radiology VLM reflects the limits of con-trastive text-prompt access, and should not be read as evidence that the features lack the biology. On external, the supervised tiny-MLP reaches 0.707 while zero-shot sits at chance. We previously attributed this to a smoking_never = 0 stratum, but that stratum holds only 3 EGFR + patients ( n = 21 ), too few to support an AUC-level claim, and the on-substrate probe shows EGFR signal on Merlin is not smoking-dominated internally. We therefore report the external supervised number as an isolated, small-n external point without a confound attribution; a properly powered external cohort is needed to interpret it. 

5.4 The EGFR signal lies off the text-prompt axis 

Why can a supervised probe read EGFR from the Merlin features while the con-trastive score cannot? The two access modes are restricted to different directions of the embedding space. Because both image and text features are L2-normalised before scoring, the zero-shot log-odds of the positive prompt is exactly a one-dimensional projection of the image embedding ˆı,

log P (pos )1 − P (pos ) = logit_scale · ˆı · (ˆ tpos − ˆtneg ) = logit_scale · ˆı · ˆv, 

so the contrastive score reads the image embedding only along a single text-defined axis ˆv = ˆ tpos − ˆtneg , whereas a supervised probe may combine all 512 directions. We 14 recover ˆv from the cached zero-shot predictions (reconstruction R2 = 1 .000 , confirming the identity) and compare it with the EGFR-discriminative direction ˆw that a logistic probe fits in the same normalised space (Table 7, Figure 8). The two directions are effectively orthogonal: cos( ˆ w, ˆv) = 0 .018 (cot_brief ; 0.024 

for bare ), against a random-direction null of mean 0.035 (99th percentile 0.113 ). The consequence is decisive. Projecting onto ˆv alone decodes EGFR at exactly the zero-shot level (AUC 0.599 /0.561 ), while removing ˆv and probing the orthogo-nal 511 -dimensional complement leaves the gate-clearing signal intact (AUC 0.748 [0 .625 , 0.855] ). The EGFR information therefore lives almost entirely in the subspace the prompts do not point at: the zero-shot null is a misalignment between the prompt axis and the discriminative axis, not a deficit of EGFR content in the features. Re-encoding the prompts through Merlin’s text encoder explains why the prompt axis is uninformative. First, the positive and negative EGFR prompts are nearly collinear in the text space ( cos = 0 .92 bare , 0.82 cot_brief ), so their difference ˆv is short ( ∥ˆtpos − ˆtneg ∥ = 0 .40 /0.60 ) and dominated by whatever idiosyncratic direction separates two almost-identical sentences. Second, image and text embeddings occupy weakly-aligned cones — a modality gap [16] — with an image–text centroid cosine of only 0.23 ; Merlin’s out-of-domain application to chest CT (it was pretrained on abdominal volumes) can only widen that gap. A short, near-degenerate contrast axis sitting in a different region of the unit sphere from the image manifold has little reason to align with the EGFR direction, and empirically it does not. This is the mechanism behind the headline: open radiology-VLM features can carry decodable biomarker signal that zero-shot contrastive prompting is structurally unable to reach.

5.5 Limitations 

• n = 96 / 42 . All findings are bounded by small-cohort noise. The pre-registered gate (AUC > 0.65 , lower CI > 0.55 ) is calibrated for that regime; in larger cohorts it should be tightened. 

• Two-prompt contrastive scoring. No few-shot in-context prompting and no retrieval-augmented prompting; these were reserved as conditional sensitivities. The headline null at n = 96 does not require them. 

• Median-split mRNA targets. TP53_high / STK11_high are speculative (mRNA z-score median split, not clinical-grade biomarkers); their gate-failures are conservative. 

• Merlin’s text encoder is Clinical-Longformer , trained on radiology reports; the research-statement-style prompts here are partially out-of-distribution for that 16 encoder. A sensitivity rewriting prompts to mimic radiology-report sentences is unlikely to flip the EGFR / KRAS headline. 

• CT-CHAT not evaluated. The first-choice VLM is not reachable on Hugging-Face at this time; reserved for a future arm if the weights become accessible. 

5.6 What this work contributes 

A clean small-cohort benchmark of zero-shot biomarker prediction with publicly released open 3D-CT VLMs under a pre-registered gate, contextualised against super-vised probes on the same image features. The headline pre-registered Outcome C (zero-shot null) is paired with a constructive contrast: the same Merlin features yield a gate-clearing EGFR signal under a supervised linear probe, so the zero-shot null is a limit of contrastive text-prompt access at small n, not of feature content. A representation-geometry analysis (Section 5.4) makes the mechanism concrete — the discriminative direction is near-orthogonal to the text-prompt axis, and the prompt pair is near-degenerate within a sizeable image–text modality gap — which is the kind of diagnosis a zero-shot null should be expected to carry rather than a bare AUC. A secondary methodological lesson is that a confounder ordering (smoking >

biomarker) measured on one feature substrate need not transfer to another: it held on a CT-FM proxy but reversed on the benchmarked Merlin features, so cross-substrate confounder foreclosure must be verified on the substrate actually used. 

# 6 Conclusion 

Zero-shot contrastive scoring with two open 3D-CT VLMs (Merlin and a 2D Biomed-CLIP comparator) at n = 96 NSCLC-Radiogenomics produces no cell that clears the pre-registered gate of AUC > 0.65 with bootstrap lower CI > 0.55 and external repli-cation. No EGFR or KRAS cell clears the gate within internal, and all four external EGFR cells sit at chance or anti-predictive (Outcome C). But a supervised linear probe on the same frozen Merlin features clears the EGFR gate ( 0.748 [0 .627 , 0.855] ,permutation p = 0 .010 ; ridge 0.705 , p = 0 .015 ), so the zero-shot null reflects the limits of contrastive text-prompt access rather than absent feature signal. The pre-registered smoking-foreclosure (Outcome D), established on a CT-FM proxy substrate, does not transfer to the Merlin features, where EGFR signal exceeds smoking signal. Zero-shot prediction with publicly released radiology VLMs sits at approximately 0.55 –0.60 

AUC on EGFR and KRAS at this cohort size, while the underlying features carry more biomarker signal than that access mode can reach.
