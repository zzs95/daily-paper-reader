## Abstract

In medical imaging, generating precise and informative diagnostic reports remains challenging due to persistent issues of factual inconsistencies and pathological hallucinations. This paper proposes a novel framework that synergizes radiologic domain knowledge with multimodal reasoning to improve diagnostic accuracy in medical report generation. Our approach addresses two critical challenges through innovative methodologies: First, we develop a method using Large Language Models (LLMs) to distill knowledge from unstructured reports, creating anatomically grounded question-answering pairs that explicitly align visual features with clinical semantics. Second, to compensate for knowledge gaps in medical image interpretation, we propose a retrieval-augmented strategy that utilizes disease labels and region-specific semantic features as dual indicators for building context-aware retrieval databases, thereby enabling in-context learning to suppress hallucinations. Experiments on two benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in both report generation and clinical diagnosis tasks.
