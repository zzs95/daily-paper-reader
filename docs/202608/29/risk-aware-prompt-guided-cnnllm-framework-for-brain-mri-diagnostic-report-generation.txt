Abstract 

Brain MRI report generation has emerged as a core task for AI-aided radiology, which could potentially fill the gap of low efficiency among radiologists in tumour diagnosis. Yet, current methods are often too weak to fully express the clinical variances in disease and anatomical regions, which results in repetitive genetic reports. To overcome such limitations, the proposed a risk-aware prompt-based medical report generation framework that utilizes the vision-based deep visual perception and Large Language Models (LLMs) to produce brain tumour reports that are accurate and clinically interpretable. This work proposes a semantic prompt engineering framework based on prediction scores and latent semantic embeddings obtained from an CNN-based (Convolutional Neural Networks) tumour classifier. These prompts also utilize improved context and uncertainty aware learning state embeddings as to balance the representation of diagnostic cues and counteract biases over the dominant tumour types. A special prompt selection module, trained by feedback-weighted adaptation with radiologist feedback, acts as a contextual mediator, mapping a classifier's predictions to section-based medical reporting schemas that are compatible with radiological reasoning. Moreover, the suggested work is among the first to include feedback-based adaptation loop with a radiologist, jointly refining the visual and language components based on expert feedback, facilitating learning on the fly and improved alignment to clinical need. This is supported by comprehensive experiments on a curated brain MRI dataset, demonstrating that the proposed framework outperforms conventional classification metrics by a large margin in terms of report quality and clinical consistency, and hence is applicable in practical healthcare settings

1.  Introduction 

Radiology reports are interpretations of medical images of abnormal findings and have a significant impact on patient care. However, the conventional way of writing such reports is radiologist-time intensive, mentally taxing and error-prone because of fatigue, distraction, and inconsistencies and variability of experience between radiologists. A potential candidate solution is the automated medical report generation (MRG), which generates coherent, precise, and timely medical report that can alleviate the burden of radiologists, especially in resource constrained health care systems [1], [2]. 

The task of prompt engineering is to craft good enough input prompts to steer the output of LLMs. In the case of MRG, prompt engineering may refer to the inclusion of tumour type, imaging risk score, and visually contextual information in the form of structured diagnostic prompts that resemble radiologists' reasoning and verbalisation. This allows the LLM to directly generate accurate and context-aware reports without the need to explicitly tag medical entities or conduct massive retraining [3]. 

Although MRG models have achieved more success, they are still constrained in several ways. As opposed to natural image captioning. MRG is also the first system to detect subtle pathologies and make decisions on domain-specific objects (anatomical regions, tumours and tumour-related structures), which can be sparse and visually akin [4]. Previous solutions have suggested task-tailored attention mechanisms, memory network, knowledge graphs to ground medical semantics, but they are limited in modelling diagnostic variations across patients [5]. The LLM-based methods have demonstrated advances via employing medical prompts, finetuning methodologies, while largely concentrating on 2D modalities, such as chest X-rays, in comparison with brain MRI, which is more challenging because of the 3D characteristics of human anatomy and profoundly diverse tumor patterns. The difficulty of the brain MRI challenge is primarily due to the high variability of tumour morphology and anatomy, which commonly results in large learning biases, which cause the model to overweight certain patterns while ignoring others, producing clinical reports that are redundant and partial [6], [7]. Finally, there has been an overreliance on prior propensity reduction approaches and on logit-adjusted classifiers for text-entity labels, which decreases the models attention on scan-contingent visual information. 

To solve these problems, as game-theoretic entity-based methods that would fit to known subsets and other such risks, the work presented proposes a risk-aware prompt-based approach that constructs diagnostic prompts dynamically based on the tumor classification predictions derived from a CNN, on scan-specific embeddings, and on an uncertainty-aware learning condition scorer. This design ensures fair diagnostic prompting, less overfitting to common patterns, and reports on the brain MRI that are more interpretable and clinically consistent, and more in line with radiological reasoning. 

2.  Related works The evolution of LLMs, multimodal learning and prompting have shown promising results in automated radiology report generation. A few recent works have desired to produce coherent textual descriptions from the contents of medical images in order to assist the radiology workflow. On the whole, there are four types of current MRG modes. 

(i) Multimodal and prompt-based radiology report generation models : There has been 

increasing curiosity around prompt‐based methodologies to guide the LLM to build 

structured and clinically relevant radiology report generation in recent times. Shaaban et al. [8] introduced a language-based visual prompting approach to enable feature recognition and matching for two or more radiology scans for multi-image comprehension and report consistency. The author also generalized this technique by means of knowledge-aware prompting, in which clinical entities are leveraged to produce more context-aware and diagnostic chest X-ray reports [9]. MSAT focused on rapid, top-down attention and procedures for developing structured reports of chest X-rays, to make sure they fit with medical term and reporting criteria [6]. Collectively, these works demonstrate the promise of enhancing LLM-generated medical narratives with prompt engineering and medical form schemas. MRG was developed based on vision-language alignment models, such as the Bottom-up and Top-down Attention model [10], which leads to subsequent multimodal alignment models. Prompt MRG and Style-aware Report Generation introduce medical prompts to the Rad graph or the few-shot prompting frameworks to better fit the structure[5], [11]. Furthermore, Zheng et al. [12] proposed an approach, See Detail Say Clear, that combines pathological clue driven attention mechanism for brain CT report generation, to improve localisation and contextual awareness. 

The adoption of new unification methods is demonstrated in Work like a doctor, which combines scan location and dynamic generation aspects to mimic the thinking process of radiologists [13]. In the same way, mePNET, an entity-balanced prompting method for brain CT-based brain imaging, along with medical knowledge graphs for enhanced contextual relevance, was proposed by Zhang et al [14]. 

Radiology report generation through learned knowledge base multi-model alignment is another approach for melding clinical knowledge with visual and linguistic features [15]. For standardisation, structured medical reporting in neuro radiology tumour disease and Laqua et al. proposed automatic structuring of radiology reports, with the aim of transforming free-text reports into uniform templates [16], [17]. In addition, to promote accurate structured outputs, benchmarking, and pushing radiology report generation for 3D high-resolution medical images, the performance in this paper is assessed on volumetric data, and a hybrid retrieval generation agent employs a reinforcement-based selection scheme to deliver quality reports [18], [19]. 

(ii) LLMs for medical imaging tasks . The introduction of transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs) has transformed the field of radiology as well as radiology NLP, allowing for more nuanced understanding of context and enhancement in report generation. Delvin et al. [20] proposed foundational transformer models, including BERT, which has been popularised by its deep contextual representations. The success of LLaMA2 was developed by Tauvron et al.[21]. Newer frameworks such as Dia-LLaMA and parameter efficient fine tuning of multimodal LLM (PefoMed) are specialised for CT based imaging and fine-tuned for cross modal reasoning [22], [23]. The authors present LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) and prompt engineering for multimodal LLM-based glaucoma diagnosis, emphasizing on effective adaptation and prompt grafting approaches for enhancing diagnostic rationale from multimodal inputs. The use of prompt engineering is, too, following this trend since it has to do with diagnosing glaucoma with LLMs [3], [24]. Yi et al. provided a review of multimodal LLM in radiology, which consists of two task-specific LLMs, two visual language transformers, and domain-adaptive frameworks [25]. These are quite distinct methods, illustrating rather profoundly that LLMs and prompt engineering are very powerful tools to effectively connect multimodal inputs and diagnostic reasoning. 

(iii) Dataset foundations and structured knowledge integration . The MRG is built upon annotated datasets and the they constitute part of model training and testing. Among them, undoubtedly the most cited is MIMIC-CXR, which contains paired chest radiographs and free-text reports [26]. Traditional evaluation metrics such as Vedantam CIDEr Consensus-based BMNS-based metrics and BLEU were employed to evaluate the quality of reports, and more recent metrics are attempting to embed them directly into model optimization. In this work, they plan to close the previous gaps with which issues of granularity within the studies including the development of a series of radiology examinations with an impact on standardisation and access to datasets, Granularity issues relate to anatomical segmentation and feature correspondence guided by knowledge to enhance learning in brain CT cases [27] , [28] , [29]. Relying on delocalized anatomical information, Lauzon et al. the articles emphasize the critical role of both curated datasets and structured knowledge in building clinically viable report-generation pipelines. 

(iv) Task-specific report generation and brain tumour imaging. Work of these types is challenging, with 3D anatomical complexity, heterogeneous tumour morphologies and clinically important contextual conditions. Current medical studies in brain tumour imaging and posterior fossa tumours characterization provide clinical insights, AI-based modalities (imaging and resectability of sinonasal tumours and imaging of skull base and orbital invasion) deal with anatomical complexity [31], [32], [33]. Dickerson et al. [34], they exploited the impact of template reporting in brain MRIs, highlighting the manner in which structured templates improve both clarity and completeness of reports and, thus, are in keeping with what may be expected from automated report generators. Decoding and distilling posterior and prior knowledge builds on this by modeling domain priors in language generation models. A large scale modal driven radiology report generation is proposed with clinical quality feedback weighted adaptation, which aligns feedback 

weighted adaptation with radiologist’s scoring that guarantees clinical validity. Meanwhile, weekly guided attention modals and imaging of a sinus tumour explore guided attention and weak supervision for complex anatomical structures [35]. Additional contributions to support report generation include MR angiography and imaging studies, which yield base knowledge of the brain vasculature pertinent to the image-to-text pipelines. Accuracy of MRI for orbital/intracranial examination, assistance in validation and comparative reporting. A posterior fossa tumour study on 64 cases focusing on statistical impressions on patterns in brain tumour for dataset augmentation and to be utilized as benchmark for the proposed model development [36], [37]. 

3.  The proposed Risk-Aware Prompt Guided Framework for Radiological Report Generation (RPFRR) 

This suggested model produces the radiological report automatically for the input brain tumour MRI images. It employs the following approaches to build the prompt selection module . Prompt selection module:  The suggested framework categorises prompts into clinically actionable units that reflect radiological thinking and reporting. MRI Sequences provide a standard platform for interpreting imaging results. It specifies the MRI protocols used, including standard (T1, T2, FLAIR), functional (DWI, SWI), contrast-enhanced imaging, and vascular sequences (MR Angiography (MRA) and MR Venography (MRV)). This is necessary for accurate interpretation, ensuring quality, and cross-comparing the findings. In the case of Cerebral Hemispheres , tumours such as gliomas, metastases, and meningiomas require localisation of the hemisphere, vascular supply, and peritumoral oedema. MR angiography has been observed to involve pial cortical arteries and to exert mass effect on hemispheric structures, with a significant impact on grading and surgical strategy [7], [38]. Equally, the Posterior fossa, where most childhood brain tumours occur, and imaging of this area necessitates special consideration because the anatomy is thick and the area is close to the CSF pathways. DWI and MR spectroscopy have been shown to be useful in distinguishing between the two common posterior fossa tumours, medulloblastomas and ependymomas, and to enhance diagnostic accuracy [39]. Vascular prompts narrow down diagnostic perceptions, and MR Angiogram (MRA) targeted arterial analysis, such as tumour vascularity, feeding arteries, and encapsulation. Studies have suggested that MRA can be used to measure tumour vessel relationships before surgery in hemispheric and skull-base tumours, especially in meningiomas. Whereas MR Venogram 

(MRV) is used to view venous structures, particularly dural venous sinuses and cortical veins, which are significant because of the importance of tumour planning. MRV has been demonstrated to enhance surgical risk assessment of parasagittal tumours and radio-surgical contouring by visualising venous drainage vessels [38], [40], [41]. The architecture also spans tumour extensions to other structures, including the Orbits and Paranasal Sinuses ,where Anterior skull base and sinonasal tumours often spread to the orbits and paranasal sinuses. It is critical to identify periorbital invasion and skull base erosion on MRI, especially coronal T2 and contrast-enhanced fat-saturated images. Its diagnostic accuracy and effect on respectability are highlighted in several studies [42], [43], [44]. The Impression section in structural prompts enables the LLM to generalise high-level diagnostic knowledge and propose differential diagnoses based on multimodal data. The creation of structured impression writing is a typical aspect of radiology reporting and the core of clinical communication [45]. Whereas Key findings guarantee the derivation of clinically significant results, including patterns of enhancement, tumour margins, necrosis, calcification, and diffusion restriction, which are imperative for grading and treatment planning. A well-organised Key Findings prompt will guarantee a high-fidelity capture of diagnostic information [18]. Lastly, Treatment plan and Follow-up Advice prompts support the clinical utility by linking imaging biomarkers to care pathways, indicating emergent practices in radio-genomics and longitudinal tumour management [18], [46]. The framework justifies an approach that builds on anatomical, vascular, and therapeutic considerations by basing LLM results on clinically actionable units, ensuring the resulting text aligns with actual radiology reports. 

Radiologist feedback-enhanced loop:  Moreover, to achieve the sustained adaptation and clinical performance, a radiologist-in-the-loop feedback mechanism is proposed in the present work. An Uncertainty-aware Learning Status Scorer is a CNN tumour classifier to operationalise this, and generate visual and contextual embeddings. They are filtered by prompt selection module that formulates structured prompts with rich semantics about each case of diagnosis. Once the report has been produced, the radiologist decides on the clinical utility of the report by means of a Diagnostic Yield Scale. The output is this structured feedback which is stored in a CSV schema containing prediction probabilities and uncertainty scores and is used to tune the CNN-LLM pipeline on an iteration basis. The frameworks combine automation with interpretability, therefore tying uncertainty estimation with systematic human feedback, which necessitates that outputs should be clinically consistent and reliable.  [47], [48]. 

Deployment and Evaluation:  Unlike past research, the technique emphasises privacy, and implements a locally hosted LLaMA-2 model (through Groq) with high clinical fidelity. This proposed work performs an experiment on a curated dataset of brain MRIs and has a higher accuracy of classification, the quality of the report, and relevance to diagnosis compared to baseline MRG techniques. These findings prove the proposed method an effective and comprehensible step towards the clinical AI-based diagnosis of brain tumours in healthcare [21], [22].

Structured medical prompting : Embedding medically validated diagnostic categories into prompt templates substantially improves LLM performance for clinical report generation. Differing from previous methods built on fine-grained medical entity based prompt construction, the new framework proposes a risk conditioned, section-wise prompt generation based on radiological organization instead of textual entity tagging. In this approach, prediction scores of a CNN-based tumor classifier are employed to dynamically select one among a fixed number of domain-specific prompt templates. The templates consist of semantically meaningful parts, including MRI Sequences, Cerebral Hemispheres (localization, vascular supply, edema), Posterior Fossa (pediatric tumor areas, CSF pathways∑effects), MR Angiogram (arterial supply, tumor vascularity), MR Venogram (Venous sinuses, cortical veins to plan surgery), Orbits and Paranasal Sinuses (Skull base, orbital invasion), Impression (Synthesized diagnostic reasoning), Key Findings (enhancement, necros).  This component by component architecture codices anatomical, functional, and therapeutic reasoning into prompts. 

Status word matching to interpretability: Prediction probabilities are coded into categorical status words (eg, Low, Moderate, Highest) to provide interpretable linguistic cues by converting predication probabilities into status words (see Table 1 and Figure 2). Status words are injected into the text space of the then LLM via its tokeniser, and if they are tokenised into subwords averaged embeddings are used to preserve semantics effectively. These embeddings form the learning status representation into vectors T P={t p1 ,…t pk }∈ Rpk ,which is refined by the learning status scorer to evaluate diagnostic certainty. The scorer produces status word outputs P sw ={P sw1 ,… ,P swk }, which can then be aggregated into prediction status embeddings P se . This depiction has dual goals: to direct the selection of the prompts by explicitly representing the certainty of diagnosis, and to allow the LLM to reason about outputs from the classifiers in a way that is similar to how radiologists communicate.

3.3  Prompt Guided Diagnostic Report Generation using an LLM Agent 

The enriched context C employs an LLM decoder of an LLM agent to produce the final report. In this work, we employ LLaMA 2-13B running inference at ultrafast speeds on Groq hardware. The LLM is steered by rich prompts synthesized under a unified prompting system (Diagnostic-driven prompting by multi-scan embeddings and class-conditioned tumour embeddings + Structured medical prompting by domain-specific medical reporting schemas and entity-aware clinical query formulation), towards semantically rich and clinically coherent reports. The selected LLaMA 2-13B model is an aggressive instruction-tuning after behaviour, and a reasonable compromise for computational costs and quality of language generation. The model operates on semantically structured prompts that are visually contextual, allowing it to generate clinical reporting standard-compliant outputs. The approach marries CNN-style visual cognition with report generation conditioned on language structured by risk-aware prompting. The Groq LLM agent is a smart decoder that translates image-based knowledge into valid, radiology-like texts.This synergy allows for rapid, clinically impactful report generation at scale for diagnostic applications.

Entity-level coverage had a low precision of 0.167, recall of 0.134 with a balanced entity F1 of 0.149, consistent with the previous studies that reported a range of entity coverage between 0.15-0.40 owing to intrinsic sparsity and subtlety of radiological entities (Miura et al., 2021)[51]. Notably, the work is semantically aligned, which is a strength of the proposed work. PubMedBERT BERTSore-F1 scored 0.879, inside the biomedical benchmark range of 0.85-0.90, indicating that the suggested approach does not lose clinical meaning in situations where lexical overlap is not large. In the same way, the average BERTScore-F1 (0.586) and SBERT cosine similarity (0.502) are in the acceptable range of (0.55-0.75 and 0.50-0.70, respectively), showing moderate-strong correlation with radiologist reports[52]. The result indicates that although the system produces a wider range of entities, it has always failed to provide fine-grained radiological discoveries, which are often reported by specialists. This performance is in line with previous results in MRG, and entity sparsity and subtle visual cues continue to be hard to learn by automated models.

The quality of the suggested framework is that it has semantic alignment. PubMedBERT BERTScore-F1 scored well, and it demonstrated that the report using the proposed method maintains biomedical meaning and diagnostic reasoning even in cases where there is low lexical overlap. Moderate correspondence with radiologist reports in general embeddings is further suggested by the SBERT cosine similarity (Zhang et al., 2020)[53]. The proposed system has a higher level of biomedical semantic coherence because it combines scan vision embeddings S we , context-aware embeddings (V fe ), and prediction status embeddings (P se ), that jointly guarantee the production of structured diagnostic prompts. A major distinction point is the case-level DYS being integrated with the radiologist feedback through DYS and adding it to adaptive weighting to allow continuous refinement. Notably, semantic congruence is a strong indicator of clinical value which is more important than lexical differences in the context of diagnosis. 

5.  Conclusions and Future Work 

This paper presents RPFRR brain MRI report generation method, which combines prediction status embeddings and visual embeddings to increase the interpretability of LLM. The model is based on uncertainty informed embeddings, radiologist in the loop feedback, and section wise diagnostic prompts grounded in MRI anatomy and tumor localization. Assessment reveals a moderate level of lexical overlap and high level of semantic congruence, balanced coverage of entities and high diagnostic relevance. This framework forms a practical paradigm of balancing learning of clinical entities and indicates its possibilities of wider use in the sphere of medical imaging. The suggested work shows that uncertainty-aware embeddings that are trained with prompt-guided LLMs can achieve clinically coherent MRG, which provides a scalable paradigm of medical AI use. Future directions include further work on enhancing entity level accuracy and extending the system to multi-institutional multi-modal neuroimaging data. Additionally, integrating with real-time clinical workflows will enhance scalability and deployment readiness.
