Voxel-wise Radiomics Habitat Modeling of Multiparametric Magnetic Resonance Imaging for Isocitrate Dehydrogenase Status Prediction and Survival Stratification in Diffuse Glioma 

## Abstract 

Purpose: To develop and externally validate a voxel-wise probabilistic multiparametric MRI habitat framework for preoperative isocitrate dehydrogenase classification in diffuse glioma and to explore its association with overall survival. 

Material and Methods: This retrospective multicenter study included 269 adults with pathologically confirmed diffuse glioma, comprising 174 training and 95 external-validation patients. Preoperative MRI included contrast-enhanced T1-weighted, T2-weighted, T2-weighted fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, apparent diffusion coefficient, and cerebral blood flow images. Tumor and peritumoral edema were segmented using nnU-Net v2. Sliding-window radiomics generated voxel-wise feature maps, and Gaussian mixture models constructed probabilistic habitats. Habitat metrics, tumor–edema differential features, and clinical variables were used for IDH classification. Exploratory survival analysis used a separate imaging-only signature derived from 13 retained imaging predictors and evaluated by Kaplan–Meier and Cox regression analyses. 

Results: The imaging-only habitat model (TEΔ-ALL) achieved an external-validation an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.921. The combined clinical–imaging model (Clin+TEΔ-ALL) achieved the highest discrimination (AUC, 0.955; 95% CI, 0.905–0.989), outperforming the clinical-only model (AUC, 0.660), 1

with favorable calibration and greater net benefit. SHAP identified perfusion- and diffusion-related entropy metrics as major contributors. In the external survival cohort, the imaging-only signature stratified overall survival (log-rank P < 0.0001) and remained independently associated with survival after adjustment (hazard ratio, 4.225 per standard-deviation increase; 95% CI, 2.355–7.580; P < 0.001). 

Conclusion: Voxel-wise probabilistic MRI habitat modeling captured spatial tumor heterogeneity and showed potential for noninvasive IDH assessment and survival risk stratification in glioma.

## 1 Introduction 

Isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) status is central to the molecular classification, risk stratification, and treatment planning of diffuse glioma [1-4]. Although IDH status is determined by histopathology and genetic testing, preoperative noninvasive assessment may support surgical planning, biopsy targeting, treatment selection, and patient counseling, particularly because tissue sampling can be affected by spatial tumor heterogeneity [5]. 

Conventional MRI-based radiomics and deep learning have shown promise for IDH prediction by extracting quantitative features from structural, diffusion, and perfusion MRI [6-11]. However, most approaches summarize the tumor as a single region of interest using global intensity, shape, or texture descriptors. Such region-level representations may overlook spatially distinct subregions within the tumor and 1

peritumoral edema, thereby underrepresenting intratumoral and peritumoral ecological heterogeneity. 

Habitat imaging has emerged as a strategy for characterizing spatial tumor heterogeneity by partitioning tumors into imaging phenotypes that may reflect differences in cellularity, perfusion, edema, angiogenesis, and microenvironmental gradients [12-14]. This approach is particularly relevant in glioma, where IDH-related biology is linked to vascular remodeling, infiltrative growth, and microenvironmental alterations. However, conventional habitat methods often rely on hard clustering or region-level summaries, which may compress continuous spatial information and produce variable label meanings across patients, limiting interpretability and comparability [15]. 

Voxel-wise radiomics habitat modeling addresses these limitations by extracting local texture features around each voxel and modeling habitat membership probabilistically [16]. This strategy may better capture transitional tissue states and spatially continuous microenvironmental variation. We therefore hypothesized that probabilistic voxel-wise habitat metrics derived from multiparametric MRI could improve preoperative IDH classification and provide prognostic information beyond routine clinical variables. 

The aim of this study was to develop and externally validate a multicenter voxel-wise probabilistic radiomics habitat framework using structural, diffusion, and perfusion MRI for IDH status assessment and overall survival risk stratification in glioma. 

## 2 Materials and Methods 

2.1 Study design and patients 1

This retrospective study included consecutive adults with diffuse glioma treated at two tertiary hospitals from January 2016 to December 2025. Inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years, histopathologically confirmed diffuse glioma after resection or stereotactic biopsy, complete preoperative multiparametric MRI within 2 weeks before surgery, and available clinical and molecular data. Exclusion criteria were prior treatment before MRI, severe imaging artifacts or major protocol inconsistency, and missing or indeterminate IDH testing. Of 297 initially identified patients, 269 were included: 174 from Center A for training and 95 from Center B for external validation. The selection process is shown in Supplementary Figure S1. 

Histopathological and molecular diagnoses were assigned according to the 2021 World Health Organization Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System, fifth edition (WHO CNS5) [17]. Integrated diagnoses included astrocytoma, IDH-mutant; oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted; and glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype. CNS WHO grade was recorded accordingly. IDH status was initially assessed using IDH1 R132H immunohistochemistry. Among immunohistochemistry-negative cases, 126 underwent Sanger sequencing and 85 underwent next-generation sequencing for non-canonical IDH1/2 mutations; 7 were reclassified as IDH-mutant and 204 were confirmed as IDH-wildtype. 

Clinical variables included age, sex, WHO CNS5 integrated diagnosis, and tumor location. Location was assigned by registering tumor masks to MNI152 space and determining overlap with atlas-defined regions; tumors involving two or more lobes were categorized as multilobar. The Ethics Committee of XXX Hospital approved the 1

study (2023KZ065; May 17, 2023) and waived informed consent. All procedures complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. The workflow is shown in Figure 1. 

2.2 MRI preprocessing and segmentation 

All patients underwent preoperative MRI on 1.5-T or 3.0-T Siemens, GE, or Philips scanners. Sequences included contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (T1CE), T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), T2-FLAIR, diffusion-weighted imaging with apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps, and arterial spin labeling-derived cerebral blood flow (CBF) maps (Supplementary Table S1). 

Preprocessing used SimpleITK and ANTsPy. Structural images underwent N4 bias correction. All modalities were rigidly registered to T1CE space, visually inspected, resampled to 1 × 1 × 1 mm³, skull-stripped using HD-BET [18] , and z-score normalized within brain parenchyma. Masks were resampled using nearest-neighbor interpolation. Tumor core and peritumoral edema were automatically segmented using nnU-Net v2 with the 3D full-resolution configuration trained on BraTS 2021 data, followed by visual quality inspection [19]. 

2.3 Voxel-wise Feature Mapping 

Voxel-wise radiomics maps were generated using a three-dimensional sliding-window strategy. For each voxel within the tumor core and edema, local features were calculated from a 7 × 7 × 7 voxel neighborhood, using only voxels inside the mask at segmentation boundaries. Features were extracted independently for each modality using acustomized PyRadiomics-based pipeline and mapped back to MRI space. In total, 33 voxel-wise radiomics features were extracted per modality (Supplementary Methods 1

S1). The pipeline is publicly available at https://github.com/zt3226073/radiomics-habitat-pipeline. 

Voxel-wise habitat modeling was performed separately for each modality using diagonal-covariance Gaussian mixture models. Feature vectors from the tumor and edema regions generated posterior probability maps of habitat membership. The number of habitats was selected in the training set using the Bayesian information criterion across K = 2–8. To prevent information leakage, model parameters were estimated only from training data and fixed for external validation. 

Because habitat labels were not assumed directly comparable across patients, label-independent patient-level metrics were extracted from the probability maps, including habitat entropy, dominance ratio, effective habitat number, Moran’s I, fragmentation index, and Gini coefficient. This approach avoided assigning fixed biological meanings to arbitrary cluster labels and improved comparability across patients. Tumor–edema differential features (Δ), including Δentropy, ΔMoran’s I, and Δfragmentation index, quantified spatial differences between the tumor core and peritumoral edema (Supplementary Methods S2 and S3). 

2.4 IDH Prediction Modeling 

After z-score standardization, ComBat harmonization reduced scanner-related batch effects, with scanner model as the batch variable and the largest training scanner group as reference (Supplementary Methods S4) [20]. IDH status and other outcomes were not included during harmonization, and external validation features were mapped to the training-defined reference distribution. 1

Clinical variables available before surgery included age, sex, and tumor location. WHO CNS5 integrated diagnosis and CNS WHO grade were recorded for cohort characterization but excluded from feature selection and IDH models because they were determined after tissue diagnosis and incorporated IDH-related information. The primary clinical–imaging model, Clin+TEΔ-ALL, used prespecified preoperative clinical variables and habitat features from tumor, peritumoral edema, and matched tumor–edema differences across all MRI modalities. Elastic Net retained 13 imaging predictors together with age and tumor location, whereas sex was not retained. To evaluate spatial compartment and modality contributions within a common selected-feature framework, nested feature-subset ablation models used corresponding subsets of the 13 retained imaging predictors. The TEΔ-ALL model included all retained imaging predictors but excluded clinical variables; T-ALL and E-ALL included only retained tumor- and edema-region predictors, respectively; and TE-ALL combined retained tumor- and edema-region predictors without tumor–edema differential features. Modality-specific models comprised TE-FUNC, TEΔ-FUNC, TE-STRUCT, and TEΔ-STRUCT. These comparator models therefore represented feature-subset ablations of Clin+TEΔ-ALL rather than independently selected models. 

In this nomenclature, T denotes tumor-region features, E denotes edema-region features, TE denotes the combination of tumor- and edema-region features, and TEΔ denotes the combination of tumor-region, edema-region, and tumor–edema differential features. ALL denotes all MRI modalities; FUNC denotes functional modalities comprising ADC and CBF; STRUCT denotes structural modalities comprising T1CE, T2WI, and 1

T2-FLAIR; and Clin+ indicates the addition of retained clinical predictors. Candidate classifiers included logistic regression, random forest, support vector machine, XGBoost, multilayer perceptron, and decision tree. Classifier selection and hyperparameter optimization were performed using Optuna with the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator algorithm, with cross-validated AUC as the objective function [21]. 

All model development used nested stratified five-fold cross-validation in the training cohort; the external cohort was reserved for final evaluation. 

Model performance was evaluated using AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1max. Operating thresholds were determined in training and applied unchanged to external validation. AUC 95% confidence intervals were estimated using 1,000 bootstrap resamples, and DeLong tests compared models. Calibration was assessed using calibration curves and the Brier score, clinical utility using decision curve analysis, and interpretability using Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP). 

2.5 Survival Analysis 

Overall survival was defined from initial diagnosis to death from any cause or last follow-up, with a cutoff date of February 2026. Exploratory survival analysis included external validation patients with available follow-up and complete survival-related clinicopathological and treatment information, including age, sex, location, CNS WHO grade, O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS), extent of resection (EOR), and standard treatment status. Among 95 external validation patients, 61 met these criteria (Supplementary Methods S5). 1

For exploratory survival analysis, a separate imaging-only habitat signature was constructed from the same 13 imaging predictors of the TEΔ-ALL model. A logistic regression model was independently refitted in the training cohort, and the resulting scaling parameters and coefficients were applied unchanged to external validation patients (Supplementary Methods S6). Patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups using the optimal cutoff determined by X-tile software. Kaplan–Meier curves were compared using the log-rank test. Associations with overall survival were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. Given the limited sample size and event count, the primary model included the imaging-only signature, age, KPS, MGMT status, and EOR. Sensitivity models additionally adjusted for CNS WHO grade or standard treatment. IDH status and WHO CNS5 integrated diagnosis were excluded because the signature was developed for IDH prediction and integrated diagnosis incorporates IDH status; their inclusion could introduce circular adjustment and obscure prognostic information carried by the imaging signature. The analyses evaluated whether the signature was associated with survival after adjustment for available clinical, molecular, surgical, and treatment-related covariates rather than independence from IDH status or integrated diagnosis. This survival analysis was considered exploratory because it was restricted to the external validation subset with complete follow-up and covariate data. Proportional hazards assumptions were assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. 

2.6 Statistical Analysis 

Analyses were performed in Python. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk 1

test. Normally distributed variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using independent-samples t tests; nonnormally distributed variables as median and interquartile range and compared using Mann–Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were presented as frequency and percentage and compared using chi-square or Fisher exact tests. All tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered significant. 

## 3 Results 

3.1 Patients 

The training and external validation sets included 174 and 95 patients, respectively (Table 1). Age, sex, tumor location, CNS WHO grade, and IDH status were similar between sets. IDH-mutant tumors were present in 42 training patients (24.1%) and 23 validation patients (24.2%). 

3.2 Feature Selection 

Bayesian information criterion analysis supported K = 3 for most MRI sequences and regions; therefore, K = 3 was selected for overall model stability (Supplementary Table S2). After ComBat harmonization, the proportion of habitat metrics with significant scanner-related differences decreased from 62.7% to 9.3% (Supplementary Figures S2 and S3). 

Among 75 candidate habitat metrics and prespecified clinical variables, Elastic Net retained 15 predictors for the combined clinical-imaging model: 13 imaging predictors, age, and tumor location. Sex was not retained. The 13 imaging predictors formed the imaging-only signature for exploratory survival analysis; age and location were components only of the combined IDH model (Supplementary Figure S4). Selected 1

imaging features were mainly derived from ADC and CBF and included tumor, edema, and differential metrics. Logistic regression achieved the best overall performance and was used for subsequent modeling (Supplementary Table S3). 

3.3 Model Performance and Comparison 

Multiple models evaluated contributions from spatial regions, imaging modalities, and tumor-edema differential features. In external validation, Clin+TEΔ-ALL achieved the best performance, with an AUC of 0.955 (95% CI, 0.905-0.989), compared with 0.660 for the clinical-only model and 0.921 for TEΔ-ALL (Figure 2, Supplementary Table S4, Supplementary Figure S5). 

Among habitat models, TEΔ-ALL significantly outperformed TE-ALL (P = 0.046), T-ALL (P = 0.005), and E-ALL (P < 0.001), indicating added information from tumor-edema differential features. TE-ALL also outperformed T-ALL (P = 0.040) and E-ALL (P = 0.018), whereas T-ALL and E-ALL did not differ (P = 0.234). 

Functional models showed numerically higher AUCs than structural models, but differences between TEΔ-FUNC and TEΔ-STRUCT (P = 0.322) and between TE-FUNC and TE-STRUCT (P = 0.059) were not significant. TEΔ-ALL significantly outperformed TEΔ-FUNC (P = 0.023), TEΔ-STRUCT (P = 0.010), TE-FUNC (P = 0.012), and TE-STRUCT (P < 0.001), supporting complementary contributions from functional and structural phenotypes. Clin+TEΔ-ALL also outperformed the clinical model (P < 0.001) and TEΔ-ALL (P = 0.039), supporting incremental value from integrating clinical and imaging variables. 

3.4 Calibration, clinical utility, and interpretability 1

In external validation, Clin+TEΔ-ALL showed acceptable calibration, with a Brier score of 0.103 (Figure 3A). Decision curve analysis suggested higher net benefit than clinical-only and imaging-only comparator models across clinically relevant thresholds; uncertainty intervals were not estimated, so these findings were interpreted descriptively rather than as formal evidence of clinical superiority (Figure 3B). 

SHAP identified functional habitat features as the main contributors, with CBF_Tumor_habitat_entropy_mean and ADC_Tumor_habitat_entropy_mean ranking highest (Figure 4). Edema and differential metrics, including ΔMoran’s I and Δentropy, also contributed. Representative IDH-wildtype gliomas showed greater perfusion- and diffusion-related heterogeneity and fragmentation, whereas IDH-mutant tumors were relatively homogeneous (Figure 5). 

3.5 Survival analysis 

Among 95 external validation patients, 61 had complete follow-up and survival-related clinicopathological and treatment information. Included and excluded patients showed no significant differences in sex, IDH status, location, or WHO CNS5 integrated diagnosis (Supplementary Table S5). 

The same 13 imaging predictors selected for the Clin+TEΔ-ALL model were used in the separately refitted imaging-only model; definitions and coefficients are provided in Supplementary Table S6. X-tile identified a cutoff of -1.046. High-risk patients had significantly poorer overall survival than low-risk patients (log-rank P < 0.0001; Figure 6). 

In univariable Cox analysis, the signature was associated with overall survival (HR, 1

4.979 per 1-SD increase; 95% CI, 2.903-8.540; P < 0.001). Age, KPS, MGMT status, and EOR were also associated with survival, whereas sex and location were not (Supplementary Table S7). 

In the primary multivariable model, the signature remained independently associated with survival after adjustment for age, KPS, MGMT status, and EOR (HR, 4.225 per 1-SD increase; 95% CI, 2.355-7.580; P < 0.001). This association remained stable after additional adjustment for CNS WHO grade or standard treatment (Supplementary Table S8). 

## 4 Discussion 

This study developed and externally validated a voxel-wise probabilistic radiomics habitat framework for noninvasive molecular and prognostic assessment of diffuse glioma. By capturing tumor–edema habitat organization from multiparametric MRI, the framework improved preoperative IDH classification beyond clinical variables and showed calibration and decision curve benefit. Independent prognostic value of the habitat signature suggests that spatial habitat architecture encodes biologically meaningful heterogeneity, providing an interpretable imaging bridge between glioma molecular status, microenvironmental organization, and survival risk. 

Previous studies have predicted IDH status using whole-tumor radiomics, deep learning, or ROI-level habitat analysis [22-26]. However, these approaches may insufficiently characterize spatial heterogeneity and provide limited interpretability. Although MRI habitat studies based on perfusion, ADC, or diffusion kurtosis have demonstrated differences between IDH-mutant and IDH-wildtype gliomas [10, 27-31] , most rely on 1

hard clustering or global feature extraction, potentially obscuring voxel-scale variation and reducing cross-patient comparability [27, 29, 31, 32]. In contrast, our framework combined sliding-window radiomics mapping with Gaussian mixture model-based probabilistic clustering to generate continuous habitat probability maps [16]. This strategy achieved strong external performance without deep learning and yielded interpretable spatial heterogeneity and tumor–edema gradient metrics. 

Interpretability analysis showed that IDH prediction was mainly driven by perfusion-and diffusion-related heterogeneity. Influential features included CBF- and ADC-derived entropy metrics and spatial measures such as Moran’s I, fragmentation, and Gini coefficient. CBF reflects perfusion, angiogenesis, and microvascular remodeling, whereas ADC is associated with cellular density and microstructure [33]. Previous studies reported higher perfusion in IDH-wildtype gliomas and supported ADC as an imaging biomarker for IDH classification [34, 35]. Our findings extend these observations by suggesting that IDH-related phenotypes are reflected by voxel-wise spatial heterogeneity beyond mean perfusion or diffusion values. Entropy and Gini coefficient quantify phenotype diversity and imbalance, whereas Moran’s I and fragmentation describe spatial autocorrelation and subregional disorganization, potentially capturing vascular, cellular, and infiltrative variation [28, 29, 36]. 

Peritumoral edema contained molecular and prognostic information. Prior dynamic susceptibility contrast perfusion habitat analyses identified infiltrative and vasogenic edema subregions with rCBV differences associated with IDH status and prognosis [28] .Multimodal studies similarly showed that subregions characterized by vascular 1

permeability and cellular density reflect microenvironmental heterogeneity and correlate with progression and survival [29] . Consistent with these findings, the superior performance of tumor–edema differential features suggests that contrast between tumor core and peritumoral edema adds information beyond tumor-only analysis [37] .

Patients classified as high risk had significantly poorer overall survival, and the habitat signature remained independently associated with survival after adjustment for age, KPS, MGMT promoter methylation status, and EOR. This association remained robust after adjustment for CNS WHO grade or standard treatment, suggesting that voxel-wise habitat architecture adds prognostic value beyond established clinical, molecular, pathological, and treatment variables. Prior studies have linked perfusion- and diffusion-defined habitats to treatment resistance and survival [38], while multimodal MRI habitat analyses suggest that subregions with distinct vascular permeability and cellular density may reflect hypoxia, metabolic stress, and aggressive tumor microenvironments [29]. Thus, the habitat signature may complement clinical and treatment-related variables. 

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the median follow-up was 26 months, and the low-risk survival estimate may be affected by censoring; longer follow-up is required. Second, the retrospective design may introduce selection bias because inclusion required complete multiparametric MRI and molecular data. Third, although IDH prediction was externally validated, survival analysis was limited to a subset of the external validation cohort and lacked independent multicenter validation. Fourth, 1

the habitat pipeline may be influenced by preprocessing, segmentation quality, and acquisition heterogeneity, despite ComBat harmonization and external validation. Prospective multicenter studies should confirm its robustness and clinical utility. 

## 5 Conclusions 

Multiparametric MRI-based voxel-wise probabilistic habitat modeling characterizes tumor and peritumoral heterogeneity, supports preoperative IDH prediction, and provides prognostic information for survival risk stratification in glioma.
