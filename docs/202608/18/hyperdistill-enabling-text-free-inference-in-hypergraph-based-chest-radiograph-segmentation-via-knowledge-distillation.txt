## Abstract:

Accurate segmentation of infected regions in chest X-rays is critical for diagnosis and treatment planning. Recently, multimodal language-guided segmentation methods have demonstrated superior performance by incorporating clinical text reports. However, these methods require reports at inference time, limiting their application. We propose HyperDistill, a knowledge distillation framework enabling text-free inference while preserving multimodal performance. Our Visual-to-Semantic Projector (VSP) generates pseudo-text embeddings from visual features, replacing the 110M-parameter BERT encoder with 3M parameters. The student learns from a hypergraph-based teacher through three distillation objectives: semantic embedding alignment, hypergraph attention distillation, and output-level soft supervision. Trained on QaTa-COV19 dataset, HyperDistill achieves 88.1% Dice and 97.2% accuracy without text input. With only 29.9M parameters, a 76% reduction from the teacher, it outperforms SGSeg (87.4% Dice, 186.6M parameters) while approaching the multimodal upper bound (90.7% Dice). Ablation studies confirm that hypergraph attention distillation provides the largest contribution. Code is available at https://github.com/afroozsheikh/HyperDistill.

SECTION 1.

## INTRODUCTION

Medical image segmentation is fundamental to clinical decision-making. It directly impacts patient outcomes with applications in treatment planning, surgical guidance, and disease monitoring [1]. Deep learning has transformed this field, with architectures like U-Net [2], Transformer-based models [3], [4], and attention mechanisms [5]. However, these uni-modal approaches rely solely on image data and require extensive high-quality annotations to achieve accuracy. Recent advances in multimodal learning have demonstrated that incorporating clinical text reports alongside images significantly enhances segmentation accuracy by providing explicit semantic guidance about disease characteristics, locations, and severity [6], [7]. LViT [6] pioneered this paradigm by introducing Pixel-Level Attention Modules (PLAM) to integrate textual features with U-Net feature maps through early fusion. This seminal work demonstrated consistent improvements over both image-only and vision-language pretraining approaches, establishing the value of report-specific semantic guidance. Building on this foundation, subsequent methods developed increasingly sophisticated fusion mechanisms. GuideSeg [8] employs a late-fusion paradigm to bridge the semantic gap between radiology reports and pixel-level masks. By fusing cross-modal features at the decoding stage, the architecture treats textual prompts as a mechanism for feature recalibration. MAdapter [9] introduced bidirectional adaptors connecting multiple encoder layers, enabling mutual information exchange between modalities at various scales. LGA [7] adopted parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategies to preserve the original parameters of large segmentation foundation models [1]. Most recently, Chai et al. [10] proposed an interpretable hypergraph-based framework where text tokens serve as hyperedges connecting semantically relevant image patches, achieving state-of-the-art performance with reduced computational cost. Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNNs) capture high-order relationships among multiple image regions simultaneously, offering advantages over CNN-based approaches limited by local receptive fields and Transformers constrained by quadratic attention complexity [11].

Despite their success, all existing multimodal methods suffer from a fundamental limitation: textual reliance during inference [12]. These frameworks require expert-written clinical reports at inference time, which fundamentally contradicts the clinical workflow where segmentation is typically needed before the radiologist completes their assessment, not after. Recent self-guided approaches attempt to address this limitation, but introduce new challenges. SGSeg [12] generates reports at inference using object detectors and template-based pipelines. However, such methods introduce computational overhead through multi-stage pipelines, making real-time deployment challenging and limiting their practical applicability in resource-constrained clinical environments.

Knowledge distillation (KD) [13] offers a promising approach for bridging multimodal training with unimodal inference. In medical imaging, KD has been successfully applied for model compression [14], cross-modality learning [15], and handling missing modalities during inference [16]. Recent work has begun exploring this paradigm specifically for the text-reliance problem. Dou et al. [15] proposed learning shared representations among imaging modalities to enable modality-specific segmentation when only a subset of modalities is available at test time. Wang et al. [16] introduced prototype-based KD for medical segmentation with missing modalities, using learnable prototypes to approximate absent modality information. Zhong et al. [17] applied feature-level distillation to transfer knowledge from a text-guided teacher to a vision-only student. However, these approaches primarily focus on aligning feature representations without explicitly capturing the structural patterns of multimodal fusion. Specifically, they do not model how semantic concepts spatially associate with multiple image regions simultaneously. This limitation is critical for medical imaging, where understanding which anatomical regions correspond to specific diagnostic terms is essential for accurate segmentation.

We propose HyperDistill, a knowledge distillation framework that achieves segmentation quality nearly equivalent to text-guided methods without requiring text at inference. Our key insight is that rather than explicitly generating text or querying prototype spaces, we can train a lightweight Visual-to-Semantic Projector (VSP) to directly map visual features into the semantic space that the multimodal teacher uses for text representation. Through multi-level distillation targeting semantic features, hypergraph attention patterns, and output predictions, the student learns to implicitly encode "what the report would say" without generating explicit text. Our contributions are:

*   A novel distillation framework combining hypergraph-based multimodal fusion with text-free inference capability, bridging the gap between multimodal training and uni-modal deployment.

*   A Visual-to-Semantic Projector that synthesizes pseudo-text embeddings from visual features alone, achieving 97% parameter reduction for the text encoder (from 110M to 3M).

*   Multi-level distillation losses including a novel hypergraph attention distillation mechanism that transfers structural knowledge about high-order multimodal interactions.

SECTION 2.

## PROPOSED METHOD

Our HyperDistill framework comprises two stages: teacher pre-training and student distillation, as illustrated in [Fig. 2](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/). We first pre-train a multimodal teacher model that fuses visual and textual features through hypergraph neural networks. Then, we train a student model to replicate the teacher’s multimodal reasoning capability using only visual input through multi-level knowledge distillation. In this section, we provide a comprehensive explanation of each component. The overall procedure is summarized in [Algorithm 1](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/).

### 2.1. Stage I: Teacher Pre-training

We begin by pre-training a multimodal teacher network that leverages both medical images and clinical text reports. The teacher architecture consists of three main components:

#### Vision GNN Encoder

Following [10], we employ a Vision Graph Neural Network (ViG) [11] to encode medical images. The image is divided into non-overlapping patches, with each patch becoming a graph node. Edges connect the Top-K most similar nodes based on feature similarity. For each node _i_ with feature _x i_, the graph convolution aggregates neighborhood information: 
x′i=u(x i,g(x i,N(x i),W agg),W update)(1)

The ViG encoder produces a multi-scale feature pyramid {_F 1,F 2,F 3,F_ 4} at four stages.

#### Text Encoder

We employ a frozen CXR-BERT model to encode clinical reports into phrase-level embeddings E text∈R N p×768, where _N p_ is the number of text phrases. These embeddings capture pathological findings, anatomical regions, and disease severity described in the reports.

#### Hypergraph Fusion Decoder

Unlike standard graphs that model pairwise relationships, hypergraphs use hyperedges to connect multiple nodes simultaneously, enabling high-order multimodal reasoning. In our framework, similar to [10], text token embeddings naturally serve as hyperedges that connect semantically relevant image patches. The hypergraph is defined as G=(V,E,H), where V represents image patch nodes, E represents text-guided hyperedges, and H∈R N patches×N phrases is the incidence matrix encoding which image patches are connected by each text phrase. For each text token embedding, the top-K most similar image patches are selected, and their connection weights are normalized via softmax. Given the hyperedge features (text embeddings) _F_ ∈ℝ _E_×_C_ and a learnable weight matrix _W_, the image node features are updated through hypergraph convolution: 
x out=H F W(2)

View Source![Image 4: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

This formulation propagates semantic information from text tokens to their connected image patches, effectively aligning text and visual modalities. By using text tokens as natural hyperedges, the method eliminates the need to explicitly construct hyperedges from visual features.

### 2.2. Stage II: Student Distillation

At this stage, we aim to train a student model that achieves text-guided segmentation quality without requiring text at inference. The student shares the same Vision GNN Encoder with the teacher (parameters can be frozen or fine-tuned), but replaces the heavy BERT text encoder (~110M parameters) with a lightweight Visual-to-Semantic Projector (VSP, ~3M parameters). The student takes only the medical image as input and produces segmentation masks of comparable quality to the multimodal teacher.

#### 2.2.1. Visual-to-Semantic Projector (VSP)

The VSP is a lightweight module that synthesizes pseudo-text embeddings directly from visual features, eliminating the text encoder at inference. Unlike methods that explicitly generate text [12], VSP learns a continuous mapping from visual to semantic representations through three steps: multi-scale aggregation, query-based extraction, and output projection ([Fig. 1](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/)).

##### Multi-Scale Feature Aggregation

The ViG encoder produces a feature pyramid {_F 1,F 2,F 3,F_ 4} with progressively decreasing spatial resolution. Each feature map F i∈R B×C i×H i×W i is flattened and projected to a common dimension _d h_ with learnable scale embeddings: 
V i=GELU(LN(Linear(F i)))+α i∈R B×N i×d h(3)

View Source![Image 5: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif) where _N i_ = _H i_×_W i_ and _α i_ encodes the resolution level. The tokens are concatenated: V=[V 1;V 2;V 3;V 4]∈R B×N×d h.

##### Query-Based Semantic Extraction

Learnable queries Q∈R M×d h extract semantic information via cross-attention, where each query attends to visual content corresponding to clinical concepts (pathology type, location, severity): 
S=CrossAttn(Q,V,V)=softmax(Q W Q(V W K)⊤d h−−√)V W V(4)

View Source![Image 6: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif) producing semantic tokens S∈R B×M×d h.

##### Output Projection

A feed-forward network projects the semantic tokens to match BERT’s dimensionality: 
E~text=LN(Linear d h→768(GELU(Linear d h→d h(S))))(5)

View Source![Image 7: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

The resulting pseudo-text embeddings E~text∈R B×M×768 replace BERT outputs, reducing parameters from ~110M to ~3M (97% reduction).

#### 2.2.2. Multi-Level Distillation Objectives

We train the student through multi-level knowledge distillation that aligns its behavior with the teacher at semantic, structural, and output levels. The total training objective combines four complementary losses: 
L=L seg+α L text+β L hyper+γ L output(6)

View Source![Image 8: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif) where _α,β,γ_ are weighting hyperparameters balancing the distillation objectives.

##### Segmentation Loss (ℒ seg)

We apply standard supervised learning with the ground-truth mask to ensure the student maintains task performance: 
L seg=L Dice(Y^student,Y gt)+L CE(Y^student,Y gt)(7)

View Source![Image 9: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

##### Semantic Embedding Distillation (ℒ text)

This loss forces the VSP’s pseudo-text embeddings to match the teacher’s BERT embeddings, ensuring the student generates semantically meaningful representations: 
L text=MSE(E~text,E text)+(1−CosineSim(E~text,E text))(8)

View Source![Image 10: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

The cosine similarity term ensures directional alignment, which is critical because the hypergraph decoder’s attention mechanism relies on dot-product similarity between image and text embeddings.

##### Hypergraph Attention Distillation (ℒ hyper)

This is a key contribution of our framework. The incidence matrix H∈R N patches×N phrases encodes how each text phrase connects to different image patches. The softmax-weighted connections determine which spatial regions each semantic concept (text phrase) should influence. For example, the text token "bilateral ground-glass opacities" should connect strongly to patches in both lung fields showing diffuse patterns, while "left lower lobe consolidation" should focus on a specific anatomical region. By distilling these incidence matrix distributions, we transfer the teacher’s learned associations between semantic concepts and spatial locations to the student. Even though the student generates pseudo-text from visual features alone, it learns to replicate the same multimodal fusion patterns. Formally, at each of the _L_ hypergraph decoder levels, we compute: 
L hyper=1 L∑ℓ=1 L KL(H(ℓ)student||H(ℓ)teacher)(9)

View Source![Image 11: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif) where _H_(_ℓ_) denotes the incidence matrix at decoder level _ℓ._ This loss ensures that the student’s pseudo-text connects to image patches in the same way that the teacher’s real text does, preserving the high-order multimodal relationships captured by the hypergraph structure.

##### Output Logit Distillation (ℒ output)

Following PromptKD [18], we distill the teacher’s soft predictions to transfer "dark knowledge" about prediction uncertainty and boundary ambiguity. This soft target supervision teaches the student not just where to segment, but also how confident to be at each pixel.

After the distillation process, the well-trained student is employed for text-free inference, requiring only medical images as input while achieving near-multimodal segmentation performance.

SECTION 3.

## EXPERIMENTS

### 3.1. Dataset

We evaluate on QaTa-COV19 [19], consisting of 9,258 chest X-rays developed by Qatar University and Tampere University. The dataset includes manual annotations of COVID-19 infected areas along with extended text descriptions supporting vision-language model training. Text annotations by [6] provide detailed information on infection presence, number of affected regions, and specific locations. Infection types are categorized into unilateral and bilateral pulmonary infection. The dataset is split into 5,716 training, 1,429 validation, and 2,113 test samples.

### 3.2. Implementation Details

Input images are preprocessed to 224 × 224 before feeding to the ViG encoder. We normalize images using MONAI’s NormalizeIntensityd transform with channel-wise standardization, and apply random zoom augmentation with 10% probability. Batch size is 32, optimized with AdamW (initial lr=3 × 10−4) using cosine annealing. The pretrained ViG encoder is used, and the teacher is trained for 15 epochs on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU. Distillation proceeds for 30 epochs with lr=5×10−5. The distillation loss weights are set to _α_ = 1.0, _β_ = 1.0, and _γ_ = 0.5.

### 3.3. Comparison with Existing Methods

To evaluate HyperDistill’s effectiveness, we conducted comprehensive comparisons with uni-modal, multi-modal, and text-free inference methods, as presented in [Table 1](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/). Compared to the best uni-modal method (Swin U-Net), HyperDistill achieved substantial improvements: accuracy increased from 0.950 to 0.972 (2.32%) and Dice coefficient from 0.832 to 0.881 (5.89%). Against existing text-free methods, HyperDistill outperformed SGSeg by 0.80% in Dice and 1.29% in Jaccard, while also surpassing HGNN-Text without text by 0.92% and 1.68% respectively. The multi-modal teacher (HGNN-Text) achieved 0.907 Dice when using text input at inference, while our text-free student achieved 0.881 Dice. HyperDistill also offers substantial efficiency gains: with only 29.9M parameters, it achieves a 76% reduction from the teacher (127.1M) and uses 84% fewer parameters than SGSeg (186.6M) while delivering superior Dice. [Figure 3](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/) shows that HyperDistill produces segmentation masks closely matching both the ground truth and teacher predictions, with more precise boundaries than SGSeg, particularly in challenging cases with bilateral infections.

### 3.4. Ablation Study

To evaluate the contribution of each distillation loss component, we conducted an ablation study by systematically removing individual losses from our framework. [Table 2](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/) presents the results. The full model combines all three distillation objectives with the segmentation loss ([Eq. 6](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/#deqn6)).

The full model achieved 88.1% Dice, substantially outperforming the no-distillation baseline (86.8% Dice) by 1.3%, demonstrating the effectiveness of multi-level knowledge transfer. When removing ℒ text, performance dropped to 87.6% Dice (-0.5%), indicating that semantic embedding alignment is crucial for teaching the VSP to generate meaningful pseudo-text representations. Removing ℒ hyper caused the largest degradation to 87.2% Dice (-0.9%), confirming that hypergraph attention distillation effectively transfers structural knowledge about multimodal relationships. Finally, removing ℒ output reduced performance to 87.9% Dice (-0.2%). These results validate that each distillation component contributes uniquely to the student’s ability to replicate multimodal reasoning without text input.

SECTION 4.

## CONCLUSION

We presented HyperDistill, a knowledge distillation framework enabling text-free inference in hypergraph-based medical image segmentation. Our Visual-to-Semantic Projector learns to synthesize pseudo-text embeddings from visual features, while multi-level distillation transfers semantic, structural, and output-level knowledge from a multimodal teacher. Experiments demonstrate 88.1% Dice on QaTa-COV19, outperforming self-guided methods such as SGSeg while using 84% fewer parameters, and approaching multimodal upper bounds with a 76% parameter reduction from the teacher. Future work will evaluate generalization across diverse pathologies, imaging modalities, and larger-scale datasets.
