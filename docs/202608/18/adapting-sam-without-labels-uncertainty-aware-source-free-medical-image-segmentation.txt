## Abstract:

Foundation segmentation models such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM) exhibit strong generalization capabilities but often suffer performance degradation under domain shifts in medical imaging. Existing source-free domain adaptation methods primarily use foundation models to assist specialist models, while overlooking their own adaptation and sensitivity to noisy pseudo labels. We propose a source-free domain adaptation framework that explicitly adapts a foundation model through uncertainty-aware supervision and cross-model learning. A source-trained teacher generates filtered pseudo labels and refined prompts to guide adaptation on unlabeled target data. The adapted foundation model then supervises a student model under strong data augmentations, while an exponential moving average update mitigates confirmation bias. Experiments on multi-center benchmarks demonstrate state-of-the-art performance under challenging domain shifts without requiring source data or target annotations. Our study highlights the effectiveness of actively and jointly adapting foundation models in source-free medical imaging scenarios.

SECTION 1

## Introduction

Large foundation models pretrained on massive datasets have recently reshaped computer vision, demonstrating strong zero-shot and transfer capabilities across a wide range of tasks [1]. Among them, the Segment Anything Model (SAM) is a promptable segmentation foundation model trained on billions of masks and has shown impressive generalization on natural images. However, when applied to specialized domains such as medical imaging, SAM and similar models often experience significant performance degradation due to severe distribution shifts between pretraining data (e.g., SA-1B) and real-world clinical datasets.

A common strategy to mitigate this issue is domain-specific fine-tuning or retraining. While effective, these approaches require large-scale, densely annotated medical datasets, typically labeled by clinical experts [2]. Such annotations are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. Moreover, even well-adapted models may fail to generalize reliably in clinical practice, where imaging protocols, scanner vendors, and patient populations continuously evolve, leading to unseen target distributions.

To overcome these challenges, Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) has emerged as a practical alternative, where adaptation relies solely on a pretrained source model and unlabeled target data [3]. For example, BEAL addresses uncertainty through boundary and entropy constraints, while DPL adopts a denoising label strategy to provide cleaner supervision. More recently, foundation models with strong zero-shot capabilities have been explored within the SFDA setting. Methods such as SBIF [4] evaluate pseudo-label quality to improve optic disc segmentation, while several approaches leverage a frozen SAM [5], [6] to refine noisy pseudo-labels via prompt-driven mechanisms.

Despite these advances, most existing methods exploit foundation models primarily to enhance specialist or task-specific source models, while largely overlooking the foundation models’ own capacity for self-learning and adaptation to new domains. In addition, these models are often selected based on their strong performance on related tasks, which does not guarantee robustness under unseen domain shifts. Although WeSAM [7] improves cross-domain generalization using weakly supervised bounding boxes, it still introduces additional annotation costs. Meanwhile, teacher-student paradigms are widely adopted in SFDA [8], but improper pseudo-label filtering and self-training strategies can lead to confirmation bias, where incorrect predictions are repeatedly reinforced.

To address these limitations, we propose a novel source-free domain adaptation framework that explicitly integrates a foundation segmentation model into the adaptation process. Instead of treating SAM as a fixed or auxiliary component, we employ it as the primary target-domain adapter guided by a source-trained teacher model. The teacher generates pseudo-labels and spatial prompts to facilitate SAM adaptation, while high-uncertainty regions are excluded from both pseudo-label supervision and prompt construction, which is crucial given SAM’s sensitivity to prompt quality. Once SAM achieves stable target-domain alignment, it supervises a student model under strong data augmentations, and the teacher is updated using an exponential moving average of the student parameters. This cross-model collaboration effectively mitigates confirmation bias and enables self-adaptation of the foundation model. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

*   We propose a SFDA framework that jointly adapts a foundation segmentation model and a specialist model without any form of supervision.

*   We introduce an uncertainty-aware adaptation strategy together with a cross-model supervision scheme, improving adaptation stability, alleviating confirmation bias, and enabling robust knowledge transfer.

*   Extensive experiments on fundus segmentation benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed method under challenging domain shifts.

SECTION 2

## Methodology

### 2.1 Preliminary

In the Source-Free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) setting, a labeled source dataset D s={(x s i,y s i)}N s i=1 is available to train a specialist model _f s_ under full supervision. The goal is to adapt the model to an unlabeled target dataset D t={x t i}N t i=1 without access to source data. The primary challenge lies in the domain shift between the source and target domains.

For fundus image segmentation, each input image _x i_ ∈ ℝ _H_×_W_×3 represents a color fundus photograph, and the corresponding label _y i_ ∈ {0,1}_H_×_W_×_C_ denotes the segmentation mask, where _C_ = 2 corresponds to the optic disc and optic cup.

[Figure 2](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/) illustrates the proposed framework. We introduce an adaptation strategy that leverages the Segment Anything Model (SAM), a foundation segmentation model with strong generalization capability, denoted as _f_ SAM. A teacher model _f T_, initialized from the source-trained model _f s_, guides SAM during adaptation to the target domain. Specifically, the teacher generates uncertainty-aware pseudo-labels and bounding-box prompts through consistency learning, which are used to supervise SAM in the uncertainty-aware adaptation stage. After SAM achieves sufficient alignment with the target domain, it produces spatially refined predictions to train a student model _f S_ in the cross-model supervision stage. The teacher is then updated using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student parameters, enabling it to progressively absorb stable target-domain knowledge.

### 2.2 Overview of Segment Anything Model

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is a prompt-driven foundation model trained on a large-scale dataset, SA-1B, which contains 1.1 billion labeled masks. SAM demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization across diverse vision tasks but often struggles in specialized domains such as medical imaging.

Given an input image _I_ and a prompt _b_ (e.g., points or bounding boxes), SAM predicts the corresponding segmentation mask _y_ as 
y=f S A M(I,b),(1)

f S A M(I,b)=F D(F E(I),F P(b)).(2)

View Source![Image 3: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

Here, ℱ _E_, ℱ _P_, and ℱ _D_ denote the image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder, respectively.

Most existing approaches adapt SAM to specialized domains by fine-tuning it on large-scale domain-specific datasets. However, this process requires substantial annotation effort and computational resources. In contrast, our approach leverages a pretrained source-domain model to guide SAM adaptation. In this setting, the source model facilitates knowledge transfer to the target domain, while SAM simultaneously learns domain-specific representations and provides guidance during self-training.

### 2.3 Uncertainty-Aware Adaptation

Directly using pseudo labels or bounding-box prompts generated from noisy predictions can lead to severe error accumulation under domain shift. This issue is particularly critical for the Segment Anything Model (SAM), whose segmentation quality is highly sensitive to prompt reliability [9]. To mitigate this problem, we propose an Uncertainty-Aware Adaptation (UAA) strategy that explicitly filters unreliable predictions before they are used for prompt construction and supervision.

**Uncertainty Estimation.** Given an unlabeled target image _x t_, we apply a weak augmentation A w to obtain pixel-wise predictions from the teacher model: 
p v=f T v(A w(x t)),(3)

Prediction uncertainty is directly estimated from the predictive distribution using entropy: 
U v=−p v log p v−(1−p v)log(1−p v).(4)

Given a confidence threshold _γ_, pseudo labels are generated as 
y^v=1 l[p v≥γ].(5)

To prevent unreliable regions from contaminating prompt construction, we explicitly exclude high-uncertainty pixels when generating bounding boxes: 
B t=BBox({v∣y^v=1,U v<H}),(6)

### 2.4 Cross-Model Supervision

Conventional teacher-student self-training methods [8] rely on a single model to generate pseudo labels for itself, which often leads to confirmation bias under domain shift. In contrast, directly applying symmetric cross-pseudo supervision between multiple models can cause rapid model drift when predictions from both models are unreliable during the early adaptation stage.

To address these issues, we introduce a second-stage joint training strategy based on Cross-Model Supervision (CMS). The teacher model provides stable uncertainty estimation and guidance for SAM through temporally smoothed parameters. Guided by this supervision, SAM progressively aligns with the target-domain distribution. The student model acts as a buffer by learning from SAM under strong data augmentations, which improves generalization and stabilizes the EMA-based update of the teacher. This design enables controlled knowledge transfer, mitigates confirmation bias and early drift, and ensures stable target-domain adaptation.

Pseudo labels for the student are generated from the predictions of SAM obtained in the uncertainty-aware adaptation stage. To prevent unstable gradient propagation into SAM, we apply a stop-gradient operation, denoted as sg(•): 
y^SAM v=1 l[sg(p SAM v)≥γ].(12)

View Source![Image 13: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

**Weak-Strong and Strong-Strong Consistency.** The student model receives a different strong augmentation A s 2 of the same target image: 
p S v=f S v(A s 2(x t)).(13)

View Source![Image 14: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

The student is supervised using Dice and BCE losses: 
L S=L Dice(p S v,y^SAM v)+L BCE(p S v,y^SAM v).(14)

View Source![Image 15: Right-click on figure for MathML and additional features.](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/assets/img/icon.support.gif)

This design enforces (i) weak-to-strong consistency between the teacher and SAM, and (ii) strong-to-strong consistency between SAM and the student under distinct augmentations, enabling robust knowledge transfer.

**Teacher Update.** To gradually incorporate target-domain knowledge while maintaining training stability, the teacher model is updated using an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student parameters: 
θ T←α θ T+(1−α)θ S,(15)

### 2.5 Overall Objective and Methodology

The training process consists of two stages. In the first stage, the foundation model _f_ SAM is adapted to the target domain using uncertainty-aware pseudo labels and refined prompts, optimized with ℒ SAM in [Eq. (11)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/#deqn11). (14)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/#deqn14). The teacher _f T_ is updated via EMA and continues to guide SAM same as the first stage.

The overall objective is

SECTION 3

## Experiments

### 3.1 Dataset and Metrics

Following prior work, we evaluate our method on three public fundus segmentation datasets from different clinical centers. REFUGE serves as the source domain, while RIM-ONEr3 [15] and Drishti-GS [16] are used as target domains. The training/testing splits are 320/80 (REFUGE), 99/60 (RIMONE-r3), and 50/51 (Drishti-GS). Images are ROI-cropped to 512 × 512, and performance is evaluated using Dice and Average Symmetric Surface Distance (ASSD).

### 3.2 Implementation Details

For fair comparison, we follow the experimental settings of [10]. The pseudo-label confidence threshold _γ_ is set to 0.75. Source-domain training is conducted for 200 epochs using Adam with a learning rate of 1 × 10−3. During adaptation, the uncertainty-aware stage is trained for 10 epochs, followed by 10 epochs of cross-model supervision, both with a learning rate of 5 × 10−4. The exponential moving average coefficient _α_ is fixed at 0.98. An entropy-based threshold ℋ is scheduled via a sigmoid ramp-up from 4 5 U max to _U_ max. Strong augmentations 1 and 2 include brightness adjustment, random erasing, and Gaussian noise, which are randomly sampled and independently applied, while weak augmentations apply mild brightness adjustment. The SAM model is adapted by fine-tuning only the mask decoder.

### 3.3 Comparison with State-of-the-Arts

We evaluate our method against representative UDA and SFDA approaches under identical experimental settings, including BEAL [10], DPL [11], CPR [12], CBMT [8], PLPB [13], SBIF [4], and DKG [14]. We also report _Source only_, where the specialist and foundation model are directly applied to the target domain, and _Target only_, where the specialist and SAM are fully supervised on target data.

As shown in [Table 1](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/), our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on both Drishti-GS and RIM-ONE-r3, outperforming source-access methods such as BEAL and SBIF that rely on zero-shot foundation models. This gain comes from transferring low-uncertainty predictions from the specialist to guide the foundation model, followed by robust target adaptation via cross-model collaborative learning.

Qualitative results in [Figure 3](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/) show that our method closely matches ground-truth boundaries (red and green), while other methods exhibit over-segmentation or miss challenging edges.

### 3.4 Ablation Study

**Different Components.**[Table 2](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/) reports an ablation study of our modules. The baseline student-teacher framework suffers from confirmation bias and lacks guidance from the foundation model. Incorporating Uncertainty-Aware Adaptation (UAA) improves performance by transferring only low-uncertainty predictions to the foundation model. In contrast, applying Cross-Model Supervision (CMS) from the start degrades performance due to learning from noisy pseudolabels. Combining UAA with CMS stabilizes training by progressively updating the teacher and enabling reliable self-adaptation for the foundation model. The full framework achieves state-of-the-art performance through strong-strong consistency, resulting in improved robustness.

SECTION 4

## Conclusions

This paper presents a self-adaptation source-free domain adaptation framework for medical image segmentation that jointly adapts a foundation model and a specialist model without access to any supervision. An uncertainty-aware adaptation strategy prevents noisy pseudo labels and unreliable prompts from degrading the adaptation process, while cross-model supervision enables stable knowledge transfer and mitigates confirmation bias. Extensive experiments on cross-domain fundus segmentation benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms existing approaches with strong robustness and efficiency, highlighting the effectiveness of actively adapting foundation models in source-free medical imaging scenarios.
