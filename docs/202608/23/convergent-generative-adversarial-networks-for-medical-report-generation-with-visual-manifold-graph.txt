# Convergent Generative Adversarial Networks for Medical Report Generation With Visual Manifold Graph 

YUAN WANG  , SHIJIE XU, KUN ZHOU  , AND DONGMEI LI 

> Artificial Intelligence Research Institute, School of Computer Science, China West Normal University, Nanchong 637009, China

Corresponding author: Kun Zhou (zhouk@cwnu.edu.cn) This work was supported by the Sichuan Science and Technology Program (No. 2026YFTX0029), and the Yibin Science and Technology Program (No. 2025JC002). 

ABSTRACT Medical report generation aims to reduce the reporting burden of radiologists by translating medical images into clinically meaningful descriptions. Although generative adversarial networks (GANs) have shown potential for image captioning and report generation, their application to radiology reports remains challenging because radiology text requires standardized negative expressions, adversarial training may be unstable, and lexical metrics alone cannot fully reflect clinical utility. To address these issues, we propose ConCapGAN, a GAN-based medical report generation framework with a visual manifold graph (VMG), a zero-centered Wasserstein regularizer, and a language-style constraint. The VMG explicitly models object–predicate associations between detected image regions and report semantics, thereby helping represent clinically important positive and negative findings. The zero-centered regularizer improves the local stability of adversarial optimization under clearly stated assumptions, and the language-style constraint encourages reports to follow clinician-like phrasing without introducing an additional large language module. Experiments on IU X-Ray, MIMIC-CXR, and LGK show that ConCapGAN achieves competitive natural language generation performance and improved clinical efficacy (as measured by several diagnostic metrics) compared with recent report generation methods. 

INDEX TERMS Medical report generation, visual manifold graph, local adversarial stability, clinical language style. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Medical report generation is an important computer-aided diagnosis task that converts medical images into coherent, clinically meaningful reports. A reliable system can help reduce the repetitive reporting workload, improve reporting consistency, and provide preliminary diagnostic support for clinicians, especially in high-throughput imaging scenar-ios [1]. Compared with retrieval- or template-based systems, deep learning methods can learn more flexible image–text mappings and have achieved promising progress in report generation [2].

Despite this progress, three issues remain insufficiently addressed. First, radiology reports follow domain-specific writing conventions, particularly standardized negative descriptions such as ‘‘no pleural effusion’’ or ‘‘no focal consolidation.’’ These expressions require the model to associate visual regions with report predicates rather than rely solely on conventional < subject-predicate-object > triples to describe object-like findings. Second, adversarial report generation is difficult to optimize because a finite number of discriminator updates may lead to oscillation or prevent the model from approaching a local equilibrium. Third, lexical natural language generation (NLG) metrics do not fully reflect clinical usefulness; therefore, clinical efficacy, physician assessment, efficiency, and statistical reliability should also be considered.

> •

We construct a visual manifold graph (VMG) that connects detected visual objects with textual predicates, enabling explicit modeling of positive and negative clinical findings.  

> •

We introduce a zero-centered Wasserstein regularizer and provide a corrected convergence analysis show-ing local stability under standard smoothness, non-degeneracy, and small-step-size assumptions.

> II. RELATED WORK

Medical report generation has progressed from retrieval- and template-based systems to deep generative frameworks that jointly model visual perception and language generation. This section reviews representative studies from four aspects: encoder–decoder models, attention-based models, LLM-based methods, and diffusion- or GAN-based methods. Table 1 summarizes representative methods that are closely related to our work, and Table 2 provides a conceptual map that positions ConCapGAN according to visual-structure modeling, adversarial optimization, clinical language style, and computational cost. 

> A. ENCODER–DECODER MEDICAL REPORT GENERATION

Encoder–decoder frameworks typically extract visual rep-resentations with an image encoder and generate reports with a language decoder. PGT [10] incorporates disease-label priors to guide the model toward lesion-related regions, while TriNet [11] introduces a three-branch architecture to jointly encode visual and semantic information. Graph-enhanced methods improve disease representation by modeling abnor-mal attributes and semantic relations, such as ATAG [12]. To strengthen cross-modal consistency, M2KT [3] integrates learnable medical knowledge with multimodal alignment, while CoPlan [13] models the descriptive order of disease terms to improve report organization. AMLMA [14] further combines residual attention CNNs and Bi-LSTMs to improve semantic extraction and cross-modal alignment. Since clinical reports require domain-specific knowledge, several methods introduce medical prior knowledge into report generation. KGAE [15] reduces dependence on paired data by projecting images and reports into a shared latent space. GSK [16] combines generic and specific medical knowledge to enhance image features, while SSVE [17] inte-grates a memory-guided Transformer with a spatial-semantic visual extractor to improve visual understanding and text generation. 

> B. ATTENTION-BASED MEDICAL REPORT GENERATION

Attention mechanisms enable report generation models to focus on clinically relevant regions and semantic units. MedVAG [18] combines self-attention, GPT-2, graph feature fusion, and multiple attention mechanisms for report gener-ation. ASGK [19] incorporates external medical knowledge and internal visual signals to enhance abnormal-region perception and visual-text alignment. WGAM-HI [20] intro-duces pathology event-guided weakly supervised attention and hierarchical interaction to improve region-level semantic focusing. DACG [21] strengthens image-text correlation through dual attention and context guidance, while ARG [22] focuses on critical image regions to generate semantically expressive reports. Transformer-based models have also been widely adopted due to their strong global modeling ability and compatibility with bottom-up visual features. S3-Net [23] combines ResNet and Swin Transformer with self-supervised learning for enhanced visual representation. DMVF [4] fuses region-and grid-level features through a bimodal feature flow mechanism, while KiUT [24] introduces a U-Transformer with medical knowledge graphs. RRGMambaFormer [25] further combines Transformer and Mamba to improve long-and short-range dependency modeling. 

> C. LLM-BASED MEDICAL REPORT GENERATION

Recently, large language models (LLMs) have shown strong potential in medical report generation due to their powerful linguistic and reasoning capabilities. To bridge the gap between visual features and frozen LLMs, R2GenGPT [6] aligns visual representations with the word embedding space through a lightweight visual alignment module. CoD [26] first generates question-answer pairs to extract key findings and then prompts LLMs for explainable report generation. CXRAgent [27] formulates report generation as a multi-stage agent process with tool invocation and diagnostic plan-ning. STREAM [28] further mimics clinical workflow by

D. DIFFUSION- AND GAN-BASED REPORT GENERATION 

GAN-based methods have been explored for medical report generation due to their ability to model latent data dis-tributions. GAN-MLC [30] introduces adversarial learning to jointly improve multi-label disease classification and report generation. NSL [31] combines neural learning and symbolic reasoning for spine report generation, improving the modeling of complex pathological relations. DiagDiff [9] uses diagnosis-aware prompts from disease labelers to guide denoising toward clinically con-sistent findings. Despite these advances, several limitations remain. First, existing methods do not adequately model negative findings, which are essential in radiology reports. Second, adversarial training may become unstable or oscillatory when only a finite number of discriminator updates is used. Third, reports have distinctive linguistic and clinical styles, making metrics developed for natural-image captioning insufficient for evaluating clinical report quality. To address these issues, we propose ConCapGAN for clinically faithful medical report generation. The central novelty of ConCapGAN is not a simple stacking of existing modules, but a task-specific coupling between visual–textual structure modeling and regularized adversarial learning. Compared with DMVF, which mainly improves report generation through grid- and region-level visual feature flow, the proposed VMG explicitly represents object–predicate associations and is therefore better suited to negative and relational clinical statements. Compared with CGFTrans, which focuses on global feature extraction and mid-fusion Transformer decoding, ConCapGAN further constrains the adversarial dynamics and clinical language style through a discriminator-level objective. Compared with R2GenGPT, R2-LLM, and recent multimodal LLM methods, ConCap-GAN avoids a large frozen or fine-tuned decoder and instead emphasizes graph-guided clinical semantics under a compact adversarial generation framework. Compared with diffusion-based methods, which rely on iterative denoising, ConCapGAN generates reports autoregressively and uses the zero-centered regularizer to damp local rotational dynamics in adversarial optimization. Thus, the proposed framework is positioned as a lightweight adversarial alternative that jointly addresses negative finding modeling, local stability, and clinician-like style control.

> B. FEATURE ENCODER

To address the limitation that pre-trained visual detectors may ignore key relations required for report generation, we con-struct a visual manifold graph (VMG) to capture underlying visual–semantic dependencies. A graph is denoted by G =

(N , E), where N and E represent the sets of nodes and edges, respectively. Given the distinctive linguistic style of medical reports, the VMG uses object and predicate nodes rather than the object, attribute, and relationship nodes commonly used in conventional graphs. Unlike scene graphs that describe spatial and semantic relations among image objects and knowledge graphs that capture broad semantic relations with standard object– attribute–relation structures, the VMG is tailored to medical report generation. By modeling object and predicate nodes, the VMG better captures clinical semantics and enables the explicit expression of negative findings, such as ‘‘no findings,’’ which are difficult to represent with conventional 

⟨subject–predicate–object ⟩ triples. By integrating visual objects and clinical predicates, the VMG models the visual–textual alignments required for professional report generation. This graph construction is preferred over a conventional scene graph for three reasons: (1) radiology reports often describe the absence of findings, which cannot be represented naturally by object-only nodes; (2) predicate nodes provide a compact way to encode relations such as location, suggestion, and negation without requiring pixel-level lesion annotations; and (3) the fusion strategy in Eq. (3) combines semantic label embeddings and RoI appearance features before graph propagation, allowing the subsequent GCN to exchange both visual and textual evidence.

> E. CONVERGENCE ANALYSIS OF THE ZERO-CENTERED REGULARIZER

We formulate the effect of the proposed adversarial regu-larizer as a local stability result. We do not claim that the regularized objective is a strict mathematical distance or that it guarantees global convergence for arbitrary deep networks. Instead, the analysis shows that the zero-centered term damps the transverse discriminator dynamics around an equilibrium manifold under explicit local assumptions. Let s(·) be the scalar adversarial measurement function used by the discriminator objective. Around a centered equilibrium, s is assumed to be twice differentiable with 

s′(0) ̸ = 0. The regularized discriminator objective in Eq. (22) can be viewed as the usual image–text adversarial objective plus the zero-centered penalty 

R(φ) = k E(x,z)∼Pu

∥∥∇(x,z)Dφ (x, z)∥∥22 , (23) where Pu denotes the mixture distribution used for regulariza-tion and k > 0 controls the damping strength. This penalty is minimized when the discriminator output is locally flat on the data manifold. Therefore, it discourages rapidly changing discriminator gradients near real or generated image–text pairs, which are a source of local instability.

> F. OBJECTIVE FUNCTION

Integrating the generator and discriminator objectives yields the final objective function of ConCapGAN :

L = αLGen + βLAtt + λLDis , (31) where α, β, and λ balance the generator loss, attribute/fitness loss, and discriminator loss, respectively. These terms jointly form the adversarial objective, encouraging the generator to produce descriptions that lie on the clinical report manifold while fooling the discriminator. The discriminator enforces report naturalness and language-style consistency, whereas the fitness loss improves the alignment between generated reports and target attributes.

> IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS

To evaluate the effectiveness of ConCapGAN, we conduct experiments on three benchmark datasets and compare it with representative report generation methods. We evaluate generation quality, clinical efficacy, scalability, statistical reliability, and component effectiveness. In addition, physi-cian assessment and statistical significance analysis are conducted to further verify the clinical utility and robustness of the proposed method. 

> A. DATASETS

IU X-Ray [35] is a widely used chest X-ray report generation dataset, containing over 7,400 images paired with 3,955 radiology reports. Following [36], reports without findings are excluded, resulting in 6,471 images and 3,336 reports. The reports are tokenized, and the top 1,200 words covering over 99.0% of the corpus are retained as the vocabulary. MIMIC-CXR [37] contains 227,835 imaging studies from 65,379 patients, with 377,110 images and 227,835 reports. We adopt the official split, including 368,960 images and 222,758 reports for training, 2,991 images and 1,808 reports for validation, and 5,159 images and 3,269 reports for testing. LGK [4] contains over 6,500 ultrasound images with corresponding reports collected from a Grade-A tertiary hospital in Chongqing, China. It covers liver, gallbladder, and kidney examinations with 11 disease categories, providing richer organ-level pathological information and a more challenging generation scenario than chest X-ray datasets. For all datasets, images are resized to 224 × 224 pixels, and reports are parsed from XML or CSV files into structured text. Non-medical terms, stop words, punctuation, extremely short reports, and irrelevant content are removed. The remaining reports are tokenized using the NLTK tokenizer. A Faster R-CNN pre-trained on DeepLesion 1 is used to localize object instances and extract visual features for subsequent modules. 

> B. IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS AND EVALUATION METRICS

All experiments are conducted on an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6148 CPU with 20 cores and eight Tesla V100-SXM2 GPUs. For visual encoding, the pre-trained Faster R-CNN extracts RoI features, where each region is represented by a 2,048-dimensional vector. A feed-forward network with a 1,024-dimensional output layer is used for predicate classification. For text decoding, the word embedding dimension is set to 1,000, and the hidden dimensions of the top- and bottom-layer LSTMs are set to 1,024 and 512, respectively. The graph parser, implemented as a feed-forward neural network, is trained on medical reports to classify semantic relations between object nodes in the visual manifold graph. During training, pre-trained modules are frozen to ensure stable visual feature extraction.

I. DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

The two-layer LSTM decoder is adopted as a deliberate archi-tectural choice rather than a simple implementation prefer-ence. Compared with Transformer- or LLM-based decoders, a compact recurrent decoder introduces fewer adversarially updated sequence-generation parameters, which helps reduce optimization difficulty when the generator is trained jointly with a discriminator. This design is also consistent with the linguistic characteristics of radiology reports, where the generated text is usually formulaic and the primary objective is faithful expression of diseases, attributes, negations, and clinical relations rather than open-ended linguistic diversity. Nevertheless, this choice does not imply that LSTM decoders are universally superior to Transformer decoders. With sufficient training data, computational resources, and carefully regularized optimization, stronger decoders may further improve lexical diversity and long-range textual modeling. Despite the competitive results, several limitations remain. First, although the proposed VMG improves object–predicate association, the current framework does not use direct lesion-level grounding annotations, such as bounding boxes or pixel-level masks. As a result, the model may still generate less precise location descriptions when fine-grained spatial evidence is required. Future work may incorporate weakly supervised localization, anatomy-aware region constraints, or explicit grounding evaluation to further improve spatial faithfulness. Second, VMG construction relies on automatic predicate parsing and detector-based region extraction. Errors from these upstream components may introduce noisy graph structures and affect relation modeling. A more robust parser with uncertainty estimation, or an end-to-end graph learning strategy, would help reduce this pipeline dependency. Third, the theoretical analysis establishes local stability under smoothness, non-degeneracy, and sufficiently small learning-rate assumptions. Therefore, the proposed zero-centered regularizer should be interpreted as improving local adversarial stability rather than providing a global con-vergence guarantee for arbitrary non-convex GAN dynamics. Fourth, while the language-style discriminator encourages clinician-like report phrasing, it may occasionally promote conservative or repetitive expressions when visual evidence is ambiguous. Incorporating diversity-aware decoding and clinically constrained repetition control may alleviate this issue. Finally, the current framework still depends on a pre-trained detector and an explicit graph construction stage, which may limit its scalability to modalities or datasets where reliable detectors are unavailable. Future extensions will explore lightweight end-to-end graph learning, stronger cross-modal semantic alignment, and direct clinical grounding evaluation. KAN-based models have recently combined functional representations with attention for histopathology classification and cardiac seg-mentation [45], [46]; these ideas may inform future mul-tiscale medical feature extraction. Work on lightweight real-time vision systems also underscores the importance of deployment efficiency [47]. Integrating these directions into cross-modal report generation, however, will require dedicated modeling and clinical validation. 

V. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we proposed ConCapGAN, a GAN-based medical report generation framework with a visual manifold graph, a zero-centered adversarial regularizer, and a clin-ical language-style constraint. The VMG models relations between visual objects and textual predicates, helping the model represent positive and negative clinical findings. The zero-centered regularizer improves local adversarial stability under explicit smoothness and non-degeneracy assumptions, and the language-style constraint encourages clinician-like report phrasing. Experiments on IU X-Ray, MIMIC-CXR, and LGK demonstrate that ConCapGAN achieves com-petitive NLG performance and favorable clinical efficacy across several diagnostic metrics. Future work will focus on direct lesion grounding, stronger cross-modal semantic alignment, and more robust graph construction to further improve clinical reliability.
