# Abstract 

Deep learning–based automated segmentation has the potential to improve radiotherapy planning work fl ows for glioblastoma; however, safe clinical integration requires not only high accuracy but also reliable performance across heterogeneous postoperative tumor regions. This study presents a clinically oriented reliability and failure-risk evaluation of nnU-Net v2 for automated multi-class segmentation of postoperative glioblastoma using multi-modal MRI. A total of 240 cases from the BraTS 2024 postoperative dataset were analyzed using four MRI modalities (T1, T1-Gd, T2, and FLAIR). Segmentation performance was assessed using a comprehensive multi-metric framework including Dice similarity coe ffi cient, 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), mean surface distance (MSD), precision, and sensitivity. In addition to global accuracy, a clinically motivated failure-threshold analysis was performed to identify subregion-speci fi c reliability patterns relevant to radiotherapy planning. The model demonstrated high and consistent performance for non-enhancing tumor regions, achieving Dice scores of 0.91 ± 0.05 for the non-enhancing tumor core and 0.90 ± 0.06 for surrounding FLAIR hyperintensity. In contrast, enhancing tumor and resection cavity segmentation showed reduced accuracy and greater variability, with Dice scores of 0.66 ± 0.12 and 0.57 ± 0.15, respectively. Failure-threshold analysis revealed substantial reliability limitations in enhancing tumor detection and cavity delineation, highlighting radiotherapy-relevant segmentation risks. These  fi ndings indicate that automated segmentation with nnU-Net can reliably delineate non-enhancing postoperative tumor regions, whereas delineating enhancing tumor and resection cavity contours requires careful expert veri fi cation. This study emphasizes the importance of failure-aware, multi-metric evaluation frameworks for the safe clinical integration of deep learning–based segmentation tools into postoperative glioblastoma radiotherapy work fl ows. 

# Introduction 

Accurate delineation of postoperative glioblastoma (GBM) subregions is a fundamental prerequisite for effective radiotherapy planning. ( 1). In contemporary neuro-oncological practice, radiotherapy target de fi nition relies heavily on precise identi fi cation of residual tumor components, treatment-related changes, and postoperative anatomical structures (1). Inaccurate segmentation may directly in fl uence target volume de fi nition, dose distribution, and sparing of surrounding healthy brain tissue, thereby affecting both tumor control and treatment-related toxicity ( 2). 

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) remains the primary imaging modality for postoperative assessment and radiotherapy planning in GBM ( 3). Clinically relevant postoperative subregions include enhancing tissue (ET), representing areas of blood–brain barrier disruption; non-enhancing tumor core (NETC), corresponding to necrotic or residual tumor components; surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH), re fl ecting edema and in fi ltrative tumor; and postoperative resection cavities (RCs) (4). Accurate delineation of these subregions is essential for de fi ning gross tumor volume (GTV), clinical target volume (CTV), and boost regions in modern radiotherapy protocols ( 1). 

> Page 2/25

Despite its clinical importance, manual segmentation of postoperative GBM remains labor-intensive, time-consuming, and subject to substantial inter- and intra-observer variability ( 5). Surgically altered anatomy, postoperative hemorrhage, air pockets, and evolving cavity morphology introduce signi fi cant uncertainty into image interpretation. These factors complicate differentiation between residual tumor, postoperative enhancement, and treatment-related changes, thereby limiting the reproducibility of radiotherapy target delineation across institutions. Recent studies have demonstrated that postoperative glioblastoma segmentation remains substantially more challenging than preoperative tumor segmentation due to surgically altered anatomy and heterogeneous residual disease patterns ( 2). 

Recent advances in deep learning have signi fi cantly improved automated brain tumor segmentation( 6). In particular, the nnU-Net framework has demonstrated robust and adaptable performance across a wide range of biomedical image segmentation tasks by automatically con fi guring preprocessing, architecture, and training parameters to dataset characteristics ( 5). Numerous studies have reported high segmentation accuracy using nnU-Net and related convolutional neural network architectures in glioma imaging ( 5). 

However, most existing investigations have focused predominantly on preoperative imaging, where anatomical structures remain relatively intact, and segmentation tasks are less complex. Postoperative glioblastoma segmentation presents substantially greater challenges due to surgically induced cavities, heterogeneous enhancement patterns, susceptibility artifacts, and dynamic postoperative tissue changes( 4). These factors reduce segmentation reliability even among expert clinicians and are insu ffi ciently addressed in current evaluation frameworks. 

Moreover, the majority of prior studies rely primarily on overlap-based metrics such as the Dice similarity coe ffi cient to assess segmentation performance. Although useful, such metrics may obscure clinically signi fi cant boundary deviations, detection failures, or subregion-speci fi c limitations that directly affect radiotherapy planning. For safe clinical integration of automated segmentation tools, evaluation must extend beyond global accuracy metrics and incorporate boundary reliability, detection sensitivity, and failure-risk analysis of radiotherapy-relevant structures. 

To address these gaps, the present study investigates the clinical reliability and failure-risk pro fi le of deep learning–based automated segmentation of postoperative GBM subregions using nnU-Net v2. Using the BraTS 2024 postoperative dataset, we perform a comprehensive multi-metric and subregion-speci fi c evaluation of segmentation performance across enhancing tissue, non-enhancing tumor core, FLAIR abnormalities, and resection cavities. Particular emphasis is placed on identifying clinically signi fi cant failure modes that may in fl uence radiotherapy target delineation.

# Materials and Methods 

# Study Design 

This retrospective methodological study was conducted to develop and evaluate a deep learning–based framework for automated multi-class segmentation of postoperative glioblastoma (GBM) subregions using multi-modal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The primary objective was to assess segmentation accuracy, boundary reliability, and subregion-speci fi c performance of a state-of-the-art deep learning model in the postoperative setting, with particular relevance to radiotherapy planning. This study does not aim to propose a new segmentation architecture but rather to establish a clinically oriented failure-aware evaluation framework for postoperative radiotherapy segmentation. 

All analyses were performed using publicly available anonymized imaging data. No patient re-identi fi cation was possible, and no additional data collection was conducted.

# Dataset 

# Data Source 

Imaging data were obtained from the Brain Tumor Segmentation (BraTS) 2024 postoperative glioblastoma dataset ( 4). This dataset is a curated, multi-institutional collection designed for postoperative brain tumor segmentation tasks and includes expert-annotated postoperative tumor subregions. 

A total of 240 multi-modal postoperative MRI cases were included in this study. All data were fully anonymized and released for research purposes by the BraTS consortium. 

# MRI Modalities 

Each case consisted of four co-registered MRI sequences routinely used in neuro-oncology and radiotherapy planning: 

T1-weighted (T1) 

Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted (T1-Gd) 

T2-weighted (T2) 

Fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) 

These sequences provide complementary anatomical and pathological information necessary for accurate delineation of tumor subregions and postoperative cavities. 

# Ground-Truth Annotations 

Ground-truth annotations were obtained directly from the publicly available BraTS 2024 postoperative dataset. The annotations were created according to the standardized BraTS postoperative labeling protocol by the BraTS consortium and its expert neuroradiologist annotators, as described in the original BraTS 2024 publication. No additional manual annotations were generated by the authors of the present study. 

Four clinically relevant postoperative subregions were de fi ned: 

Enhancing tissue (ET) : contrast-enhancing tumor components on post-contrast T1-weighted images 

Non-enhancing tumor core (NETC) : necrotic or residual non-enhancing tumor components 

Surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH) : edema, in fi ltrative tumor, and treatment-related changes visible on FLAIR/T2 

Resection cavity (RC) : postoperative surgical cavities with variable signal characteristics

# Data Partitioning and Validation Strategy 

To ensure unbiased evaluation and methodological rigor, the dataset was divided into development and independent test cohorts: 

Development set: 200 cases 

Independent test set: 40 cases 

The independent test cohort was completely excluded from all training and model selection procedures. 

Within the development cohort,  fi ve-fold cross-validation was applied. In each fold: 

160 cases were used for training 

40 cases were used for validation 

Each case served once as validation data across folds. Model selection was based on average validation performance. The  fi nal model was evaluated on the 40 cases of the independent test set. 

# Image Preprocessing 

All preprocessing steps were performed using the standardized nnU-Net v2 automated pipeline to ensure reproducibility and eliminate operator-dependent variability ( 7). 

Preprocessing included: 

Automated co-registration of MRI modalities 

Resampling to target voxel spacing determined from dataset statistics 

Z-score intensity normalization applied independently to each modality 

Automated foreground cropping to remove non-informative background regions 

No additional manual preprocessing, skull stripping, or bias  fi eld correction beyond the default nnU-Net v2 pipeline was applied. 

# Deep Learning Framework 

# Network Architecture 

Automated segmentation was performed using nnU-Net version 2 with the three-dimensional full-resolution (3D fullres) con fi guration. nnU-Net is a self-con fi guring deep learning framework that automatically adapts preprocessing, network topology, and training parameters to dataset characteristics( 8, 9).

The architecture is based on a 3D U-Net encoder–decoder structure with skip connections that preserve spatial information across resolution levels. Multi-class segmentation of ET, NETC, SNFH, and RC was performed simultaneously( 8, 9). 

# Training Con fi guration 

Model training followed the default nnU-Net v2 con fi guration: 

Loss function: combined Dice loss and cross-entropy loss 

Data augmentation: random rotations, scaling, mirroring, Gaussian noise, and intensity perturbations 

Optimization: Adam 

Early stopping based on validation performance 

Fixed random seeds where applicable to improve reproducibility 

Training and inference were performed on a workstation equipped with an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU (32 GB memory). Implementation was conducted using Python, PyTorch, and nnU-Net v2.

Performance was analyzed separately for ET, NETC, SNFH, and RC to evaluate subregion-dependent variability. Boundary accuracy and sensitivity were examined to identify systematic segmentation limitations, particularly in enhancing tissue and resection cavity regions. 

A subgroup comparison between recent and chronic resection cavities was also performed to assess the in fl uence of postoperative cavity characteristics on segmentation performance. 

# Statistical Analysis 

Statistical analyses were conducted using Python scienti fi c libraries. Normality of metric distributions was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Depending on distribution, either independent-samples t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were used for subgroup comparisons. 

The Morphological irregularity index was calculated as the ratio between the lesion surface area and the equivalent spherical surface area derived from lesion volume. 

A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically signi fi cant. Effect sizes were calculated where appropriate to quantify the magnitude of observed differences. 

# De fi nition of Segmentation Failure Threshold 

> Page 8/25

In the present study, segmentation failure was de fi ned as a Dice similarity coe ffi cient (DSC) < 0.70 for a given subregion. This threshold was selected based on methodological, statistical, and clinically interpretable considerations supported by prior literature. 

First, in medical image segmentation research, a DSC of 0.70 has frequently been considered the lower boundary of acceptable spatial agreement for clinically meaningful structures, particularly in radiotherapy planning contexts where contour accuracy directly affects target de fi nition. In glioma segmentation benchmarks such as the Brain Tumor Segmentation Challenge, high-performing models typically achieve Dice scores above 0.80–0.90 for whole tumor regions, while values below 0.70 are generally associated with visibly incomplete or structurally inconsistent segmentations. 

Second, from a geometric perspective, a DSC below 0.70 implies substantial spatial mismatch. Given the mathematical formulation of Dice: 

Dice similarity coe ffi cient remains one of the most widely used segmentation evaluation metrics, although metric selection should be interpreted in the context of target structure characteristics and clinical objectives ( 11 ). Recent recommendations for image-analysis validation emphasize that performance metrics should be selected according to the underlying clinical problem rather than used in isolation ( 12 ). 

A value below 0.70 typically corresponds to either substantial boundary deviation or partial detection failure. Previous methodological studies have shown that overlap-based metrics such as the Dice similarity coe ffi cient become increasingly sensitive to clinically meaningful contour discrepancies as overlap decreases, particularly in small or morphologically complex structures. Consequently, DSC values below 0.70 are often associated with observable under-segmentation or over-segmentation rather than minor contour variability ( 11 , 12 ). 

Third, in radiotherapy contouring studies, inter-observer variability for gross tumor volume (GTV) delineation in glioblastoma generally corresponds to Dice values ranging from approximately 0.75 to 0.85, depending on region complexity. Therefore, a DSC < 0.70 falls below typical human inter-observer agreement levels and can reasonably be interpreted as performance inferior to acceptable clinical variability. 

Finally, the chosen threshold was supported empirically in the present dataset. Visual inspection of cases with DSC < 0.70 consistently demonstrated one or more of the following failure patterns: 

Missed small enhancing regions 

Incomplete resection cavity delineation 

Substantial boundary displacement (> 10 mm HD95)

Accordingly, a DSC threshold of 0.70 was selected as a conservative and clinically meaningful cutoff for de fi ning segmentation failure in postoperative glioblastoma radiotherapy planning. 

# Results 

Overall Segmentation Performance 

The nnU-Net v2 (3D full-resolution con fi guration) model was successfully trained and evaluated on the BraTS 2024 postoperative dataset. Quantitative evaluation was performed on the independent test cohort of 40 cases to ensure unbiased performance assessment. Segmentation accuracy was analyzed separately for enhancing tissue (ET), non-enhancing tumor core (NETC), surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH), and resection cavity (RC). 

Overall, the model demonstrated high and consistent performance for non-enhancing tumor regions, particularly NETC and SNFH. In contrast, segmentation of enhancing tissue and resection cavities showed lower accuracy and higher inter-case variability across evaluation metrics.

Implementation Details 

The 3D full-resolution nnU-Net v2 framework was trained on the preprocessed dataset. The nnU-Net pipeline automatically con fi gured preprocessing steps, network architecture, and baseline training parameters based on dataset characteristics, ensuring a standardized and reproducible training setup. Additional training settings were de fi ned to ensure controlled and comparable experimentation. 

A fi ve-fold cross-validation scheme was applied to the training cohort. For each fold, the model was trained for a maximum of 100 epochs using a composite loss function combining Dice loss and cross-entropy loss. Training was performed with a batch size of 2 and a patch size of 128 × 128 × 128 voxels. Optimization was conducted using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a cosine learning rate decay schedule. 

Data augmentation strategies included random rotations, scaling, elastic deformations, intensity shifts, and Gaussian noise to improve model robustness and generalization. Early stopping was applied if no improvement in validation performance was observed for 20 consecutive epochs, reducing the risk of over fi tting. 

Inference was performed using a sliding-window approach to generate full-volume predictions for each MRI case. 

Hardware and Software Environment 

Training and inference were conducted on a high-performance computing node equipped with an NVIDIA Tesla V100 GPU (32 GB VRAM), an AMD EPYC 7502 CPU, and 64 GB system RAM. The model was implemented in Python 3.10.8 using PyTorch 2.7.1 with CUDA 12.6 support. The average training time per epoch was approximately 60 seconds. 

Quantitative Performance by Subregion 

Non-Enhancing Tumor Core (NETC) 

Segmentation of the non-enhancing tumor core achieved the highest accuracy among all evaluated subregions. The mean Dice similarity coe ffi cient (DSC) reached 0.913 ± 0.054, indicating strong spatial agreement with expert reference annotations. Boundary accuracy was also high, with a mean 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) of 3.85 ± 1.27 mm and a mean surface distance of 1.97 ± 0.45 mm. 

Precision and sensitivity were well balanced (0.922 ± 0.076 and 0.909 ± 0.061, respectively), demonstrating reliable detection and delineation of tumor core structures. Volumetric agreement between predicted and reference segmentations remained stable across cases, with low relative volume differences and limited variability.

Segmentation of SNFH also demonstrated strong performance. The mean Dice score was 0.899 ± 0.056, re fl ecting high overlap with reference annotations. This region exhibited the best boundary accuracy among all subregions, with a mean HD95 of 2.47 ± 1.22 mm and a mean surface distance of 1.20 ± 0.56 mm. 

Precision was high (0.940 ± 0.058), while sensitivity was slightly lower (0.866 ± 0.080), suggesting a tendency toward conservative delineation in regions with diffuse FLAIR hyperintensity. Despite the heterogeneous nature of SNFH, volumetric differences remained low and consistent across the independent test cohort. 

Enhancing Tissue (ET) 

Enhancing tissue segmentation demonstrated substantially lower accuracy and greater variability compared with non-enhancing regions. The mean Dice coe ffi cient for ET was 0.655 ± 0.12, with marked inter-subject dispersion. This variability re fl ected the heterogeneous and often fragmented appearance of postoperative contrast enhancement. 

Boundary-based evaluation revealed higher errors relative to other subregions, with a mean HD95 of 5.91 ± 3.86 mm and a mean surface distance of 4.54 ± 6.69 mm. Precision (0.72 ± 0.382) and sensitivity (0.626 ± 0.297) were both reduced compared with NETC and SNFH, indicating challenges in detecting small or irregular enhancing regions. Volumetric differences were also more variable, particularly in cases with minimal residual enhancement. 

Resection Cavity (RC) 

Resection cavity segmentation presented the greatest challenge for the automated framework. The mean Dice score was 0.570 ± 0.15, accompanied by the highest boundary variability among all subregions. The mean HD95 reached 13.44 ± 14.73 mm, and the mean surface distance was 5.49 ± 7.63 mm. 

While precision remained relatively high (0.70 ± 0.320), sensitivity was considerably lower (0.548 ± 0.348), indicating under-segmentation in a subset of cases. Large volumetric discrepancies were observed in cases with irregular or heterogeneous postoperative cavities. Performance variability re fl ected substantial differences in cavity morphology and signal characteristics across patients.

# Discussion 

The present study investigated the clinical reliability and failure-risk pro fi le of deep learning–based automated segmentation of postoperative glioblastoma subregions using the nnU-Net v2 framework. By focusing speci fi cally on postoperative anatomy and radiotherapy-relevant structures, this work provides a clinically contextualized assessment of segmentation performance beyond conventional accuracy reporting. 

From a radiotherapy planning perspective, segmentation reliability is not solely determined by global overlap metrics. Even small boundary deviations in enhancing tumor or resection cavity regions can in fl uence the de fi nition and planning of clinical target volumes. Therefore, failure-risk awareness is essential before integrating automated segmentation into clinical work fl ows. The present  fi ndings suggest that deep learning–based segmentation can serve as a decision-support tool, but expert supervision remains necessary, particularly in anatomically complex postoperative regions. 

Smaller cavity volume independently predicted failure (OR = 0.89 per 1000 mm³ increase; 95% CI: 0.81–0.97; p = 0.008). 

Higher morphological irregularity index signi fi cantly increased failure risk (OR = 1.42; 95% CI: 1.12– 1.80; p = 0.003). 

The  fi nal model achieved an AUC of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.71–0.94), indicating good predictive performance. 

Variance in fl ation factor (VIF) analysis con fi rmed the absence of multicollinearity (all VIF < 2.0). 

Summary of Findings 

In summary, the nnU-Net v2 framework achieved high and reproducible segmentation performance for non-enhancing postoperative tumor regions, particularly NETC and SNFH. Enhancing tissue and resection cavity segmentation remained more challenging, with increased variability and reduced sensitivity. These  fi ndings highlight subregion-speci fi c performance differences and emphasize the importance of multi-metric evaluation when assessing automated postoperative glioblastoma segmentation.

Interpretation of Overall Performance 

The  fi ndings demonstrate that automated segmentation using nnU-Net v2 can achieve high and consistent accuracy for non-enhancing tumor regions, particularly the non-enhancing tumor core (NETC) and surrounding non-enhancing FLAIR hyperintensity (SNFH). These regions exhibited strong spatial overlap, stable boundary accuracy, and consistent volumetric agreement across the independent test cohort. The relative homogeneity and larger spatial extent of non-enhancing regions likely facilitate feature learning and contribute to robust model performance. 

From a radiotherapy planning perspective, reliable delineation of non-enhancing tumor components is of particular importance in the postoperative setting, where residual disease may not demonstrate prominent contrast enhancement. Accurate segmentation of these regions supports more consistent target volume de fi nition and may help reduce inter-observer variability in contouring clinical target volumes. The balanced precision and sensitivity observed for NETC and SNFH suggest that automated segmentation could be integrated as a supportive tool for these structures with appropriate clinical veri fi cation. 

Challenges in Enhancing Tissue Segmentation 

In contrast, segmentation of enhancing tissue demonstrated substantially lower accuracy and greater variability. Fragmented postoperative enhancement, small residual tumor volumes, and confounding treatment-related changes contribute to increased segmentation di ffi culty. The observed sensitivity de fi cits for enhancing tissue are particularly relevant for radiotherapy planning, as under-segmentation of viable tumor regions may result in underestimation of boost volumes or biologically active disease. 

These  fi ndings highlight that enhancing tissue segmentation represents the most clinically critical failure mode identi fi ed in this study. While moderate over-segmentation may be acceptable from a dosimetric perspective, missed enhancing regions could have direct implications for local control. Consequently, automated segmentation outputs for enhancing tissue should be interpreted cautiously and require expert review before clinical use. 

Resection Cavity Delineation and Boundary Uncertainty 

Resection cavity segmentation exhibited the greatest variability across all evaluated metrics. Postoperative cavities present substantial heterogeneity in size, morphology, and signal characteristics depending on surgical technique, presence of blood products, and time since surgery. This variability complicates feature extraction and leads to increased boundary uncertainty, particularly in recently formed cavities. 

The low Dice performance in RC segmentation should be interpreted in the context of highly irregular postoperative geometries and the known sensitivity of overlap-based metrics to boundary perturbations in cavity-like structures. 

> Page 16/25

Accurate delineation of resection cavities is clinically relevant for radiotherapy planning, as cavity margins often in fl uence boost volume de fi nition and dose gradients near critical brain structures. Boundary deviations in cavity segmentation may therefore affect local dose distribution, especially in highly conformal treatment techniques such as VMAT or stereotactic radiotherapy. The observed performance variability underscores the need for manual veri fi cation of automated cavity contours before clinical implementation. 

Importance of Failure-Aware Multi-Metric Evaluation 

A central contribution of this study is the implementation of a failure-aware and multi-metric evaluation framework tailored to radiotherapy-relevant segmentation reliability. Many previous investigations have relied primarily on overlap-based metrics such as the Dice similarity coe ffi cient, which may mask clinically signi fi cant boundary deviations or detection failures. By incorporating boundary-based metrics, sensitivity analysis, and subregion-speci fi c evaluation, the present study provides a more comprehensive understanding of segmentation reliability. 

This approach enables identi fi cation of clinically meaningful failure modes that are not apparent from aggregate accuracy metrics alone. In particular, sensitivity de fi cits in enhancing tissue detection and boundary uncertainty in resection cavity delineation emerged as the most relevant limitations for radiotherapy applications. Such insights are essential for safe clinical translation of automated segmentation tools, where understanding model limitations is as important as achieving high average accuracy. 

Comparison with Existing Literature 

Previous deep learning–based glioblastoma segmentation studies have predominantly focused on preoperative imaging, where tumor morphology remains relatively intact and segmentation tasks are less complex. Under these conditions, high Dice similarity coe ffi cients have been consistently reported for whole tumor and edema regions. However, postoperative segmentation presents substantially greater challenges due to surgically altered anatomy and heterogeneous imaging characteristics. Similar reductions in segmentation performance for postoperative enhancing tumor regions have been reported in recent postoperative glioblastoma segmentation studies(13). 

The present  fi ndings align with emerging evidence that segmentation performance decreases in postoperative scenarios, particularly for small enhancing lesions and dynamically evolving cavities. By explicitly including resection cavity delineation and conducting subregion-speci fi c evaluation, this study extends prior work. It provides a more clinically realistic assessment of automated segmentation performance in postoperative radiotherapy planning contexts.

Clinical Integration Considerations 

Automated segmentation tools have the potential to improve e ffi ciency and reproducibility in radiotherapy work fl ows. However, safe integration into clinical practice requires a clear understanding of reliability across different anatomical structures. Based on the present  fi ndings, automated segmentation may be su ffi ciently reliable for non-enhancing tumor regions when used as a decision-support tool under expert supervision. In contrast, enhancing tissue and resection cavity segmentation requires careful manual veri fi cation before treatment planning. 

Rather than replacing clinician-driven contouring, deep learning–based segmentation should be viewed as an assistive technology that can reduce contouring time, improve consistency, and highlight regions requiring focused expert review. Failure-aware evaluation frameworks such as the one presented here may support risk-informed deployment of automated tools in clinical environments. 

Limitations 

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, model development and evaluation were conducted using a single publicly available dataset. Although the BraTS dataset includes multi-institutional data and heterogeneous imaging protocols, external validation on independent clinical datasets is necessary to fully assess generalizability. Second, this study focused on segmentation accuracy and reliability rather 

> Page 18/25

than direct dosimetric impact. Future investigations should evaluate how segmentation variability in fl uences dose distribution, target coverage, and organ-at-risk sparing. Third, inter-observer variability among expert annotations was not analyzed and may provide additional context for interpreting automated segmentation performance. 

The relatively high observed failure rates should be interpreted in the context of conservative failure thresholds, highly heterogeneous postoperative anatomy, and the known sensitivity of Dice metrics to small lesion volumes. 

Future Directions 

Future work should focus on improving segmentation reliability for enhancing tissue and resection cavities through advanced modeling strategies such as uncertainty-aware learning, multi-stage architectures, and incorporation of temporal postoperative imaging. Prospective clinical validation and evaluation of dosimetric consequences will be essential for establishing the practical utility of automated segmentation in radiotherapy planning. Integration of clinician-in-the-loop work fl ows and human–AI interaction studies may further facilitate safe adoption of deep learning–based contouring tools in routine practice. 

Future research should focus on integrating segmentation reliability maps, uncertainty estimation, and dosimetric impact analysis to enable safe clinical deployment of deep learning–based contouring tools in radiotherapy. External multi-center validation and prospective clinical studies will be essential to con fi rm generalizability and work fl ow integration. 

# Conclusion 

This study demonstrates that deep learning–based segmentation using nnU-Net v2 provides clinically relevant reliability for non-enhancing postoperative tumor regions and can support radiotherapy planning work fl ows under expert supervision. However, segmentation of enhancing tissue and resection cavities remains associated with substantial variability and a clinically meaningful risk of failure, necessitating careful veri fi cation before treatment planning. Failure-aware and clinically interpretable evaluation frameworks, such as the one presented in this study, are essential to ensure the safe and effective clinical translation of deep learning–based segmentation systems into radiotherapy practice.
