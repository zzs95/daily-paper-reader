## Ab stract 

Although modern machine -learning models attain strong predictive performance, their clinical adoption remains 

limited by a lack of transparency.  This  concern is particularly relevant in multimodal diagnostic pipelines, where 

the contribution of each modality to the final prediction is more difficult to discern . Relatively few studies 

integrate heterogeneous clinical and imaging data,  where they  typical ly employ decision - or feature -level fusion, 

and provide limited insight into the cross -modal interactions that  underline  the diagnostic decision.  We propose 

a Fuzzy Cognitive Map Encoder (FCM -Encoder) framework for multimodal explainable classification. I maging 

features extracted by a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) are mapped into low -dimensional concepts and 

combined with normalised clinical concept activations inside a unified FCM. The connections to the diagnostic 

output are initialised from the Spe arman rank correlation of each concept with the label, and all interconnections 

are then refined by a particle swarm optimisation strategy enhanced with Lévy -flight exploration.  Under 10 -fold 

cross -validation, the framework reached 81.82% ± 4.51% accuracy  for Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) from 

clinical variables and polar maps, and 93.39% ± 3.09% for Solitary Pulmonary Nodule (SPN) classification in 

Non -Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) from CT, PET, and clinical data.  The ablation analysis confirmed that 

each  component contributes to the overall performance, the image -encoder comparison showed that the selected 

encoder matches or exceeds substantially larger pretrained backbones, and the graph -baseline comparison 

demonstrated that the FCM -based reasoning surpa sses graph neural networks.  FCM -Encoder  further links 

imaging concepts to clinical labels and generates clinically aligned narratives through a Vision –Language 

module. 

Keywords: Fuzzy Cognitive Maps, Deep Learning, Classification,  FCM -Encoder, Qwen,  NLG 

## 1.  Introduction 

1.1. Prologue 

Cardiovascular and oncological diseases remain the top causes of death worldwide, challenging early , accurate 

diagnostic solutions that are also interpretable  for guiding treatment decisions.  Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) 

represents o ne of the most significant challenges in contemporary cardiovascular medicine and  has a substantial 

impact on patient health and quality of life worldwide . Its progression is therefore a critical public health 

priority  [1] . The  primary characteristic is the gradu al development of atherosclerotic plaques in coronary 

arteries that reduce myocardial perfusion and are known to cause ischemic events  [1, 2] . Timely and appropriate 

detection of CAD is essential to  enhance  patients'  outcomes  and guide clinical decision -making.  [2, 3] .

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer -related deaths worldwide . Based on  the size of cancer cells , lung 

cancer is  divide d into small -cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non -small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). NSCLC makes 

up about 85% of lung cancer cases, including adenocarcinoma (ADC) at around 50%, squamous cell carc inoma 

(SCC) at about 40%, and large cell lung cancer  [5] . The chances of survival and the selection of the appropriate 

treatment  improve  significantly with the early detection of lung cancer .

For CAD diagnosis, several reports have shown the growing utilization of artificial intelligence  [3, 6 –8] .

Similarly, for SPN diagnosis, several studies have been reported  [5, 9 –13] , focusing on the detection and 

chara cterization of SPNs. These methods aim at lowering the number of consecutive examinations and at

reducing the medical workload by ruling out obvious cases, as seen in  [14, 15]  for CAD studies and in  [16, 17] 

for SPN characterization. Additional indep endent clinical factors, such as age and histology, may have a 

substantial role in making personalized survival predictions, with reference to CAD studies  [8, 18]  and for SPN 

[19, 20] . It has been proven beneficial to employ diagnostic assistance tools in such analyses, as it minimizes the 

need for extensive interventions, as dem onstrated in  [21, 22]  for CAD and in  [23, 24]  for SPN  classification .

These studies have achieved high performance metrics, but they lack explainability. Consequently, nuclear 

experts are hesitant  to fully incorporate these systems into their decision -making workflows due to the lack of 

transparency and interpretability. 

On the other hand,  Fuzzy Cognitive Maps (FCMs), which aim to support clinical decision making by a well -

structured representation of the relationship among patient -specific factors, allow  for  a comprehensive  and 

transparent  view  of  the state of the patient  [25] . FCMs, introduced by Kosko  [26]  as an enhanced version of 

cognitive maps , represent variables and system states with nodes, where relationships in the form of weighted 

values illustrate the influence among them. These values lie within the range of [ -1, 1]. The interconnection 

values within FCMs signify the strength of relation ships between nodes. More information about FCMs can be 

found in  [27]  [28]  [26]  [29]  [30] . FCMs have exhibited noteworthy outcomes in medical diagnosis  [28 –32]  and 

are proficient at establishing relationships among clinical variables , which are transformed to  concepts of the 

FCM system , and enhancing the applicability and reliability of their outcomes. 

There has been  an  increasing research interest in recent years in hybrid and multimodal approaches to medical 

diagnosis that combine clinical and imaging data, aiming to improve  prediction accuracy and interpretability. 

Among them, concept -level  me thods have drawn attention  because they explicitly model the reasoning process, 

offering  a transparent alternative to  fully data -driven deep learning models . These methods are presented in the 

following section. 

1.2. Literature studies 

This section  demonstrates  the related literature in  two  subsections:  firstly , it reviews external literature on 

multimodal fusion approaches for CAD and SP N, and  second ly  it summarizes our earlier work on Deep Fuzzy 

Cognitive Maps (DeepFCM) and  its  learning techniques  for CAD and  SPN classification  diagnosis . For a more 

analytical comparison, representative literature studies are presented in the Appendix, organised by input 

modalities, model architecture, evaluated instances, performance metrics, interpretability mechanisms, and 

reported limitations. 

1.2.1.  Multimodal studies for CAD diagnosis 

A multimodal CAD classification framework was introduced in  [33] , by Apostolopoulos et al. , to overcome the 

limitations of single -modality diagnostic approaches. The method combined myocardial perfusion imaging 

(MPI) SPECT polar maps with structured clinical variables, demonstrating that CNN -based imaging models 

alone inadequately capture patien t-specific risk heterogeneity, while clinical -only models lack spatial ischemic 

representation. Deep CNN –derived polar -map features were fused with machine -learning classifiers trained on 

clinical data. Performance was reported in terms of accuracy, sensit ivity, specificity, and AUC, achieving 

approximately 82% accuracy and an AUC of 0.90. However, fusion was performed at the decision level using 

independent learners, limiting cross -modal interaction.  The technique was also still data -driven, lacking any 

me chanistic or interpretability aspect , and validation was limited to a single retrospective cohort. 

A support vector machine –based multimodal CAD classification framework was introduced in  [34] , by 

Arsanjani et al. , to integrate heterogeneous  clinical information with quantitative myocardial perfusion analysis. 

Rather than using raw images, the study relied on expert -engineered polar -map –derived perfusion and 

functional scores combined with clinical risk factors. Diagnostic performance was eval uated using accuracy and 

agreement with expert interpretation, achieving an accuracy of approximately 86% and an AUC of 0.92. While 

the framework demonstrated that structured multimodal fusion could outperform isolated quantitative imaging 

and expert asses sment, reliance on handcrafted imaging descriptors  limited the ability to capture complex 

imaging patterns , and the nonlinear SVM  model offered limited interpretability of feature interactions. In 

addition, the absence of deep feature learning limited robu stness across scanners and clinical populations. 

Using a large multicenter cohort, Otaki et al.  [35]  evaluated an explainable deep learning framework (CAD -DL) 

for obstructive CAD  detection that integrates SPECT myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI) polar maps with 

selected clinical parameters through early feature -level fusion. Raw stress perfusion, wall motion, and wall 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 3 of 36 

thickening polar maps were processed through convolutional layer s to extract imaging representations, while 

non -imaging clinical attributes (age, sex, and left ventricular end -diastolic and end -systolic volumes) were 

incorporated directly into the final fully connected layer of the network, jointly contributing to prob ability 

estimation rather than being combined at the decision level. The model was evaluated  on  3,578 patients (10,734 

vessels) using invasive coronary angiography  (ICA)  as reference. In repeated 10 -fold validation, CAD -DL 

achieved a per -patient AUC of  0.84 (95% CI: 0.81 –0.87), with sensitivity of 85.6% and specificity of 60.0%, 

outperforming stress total perfusion deficit (TPD) and expert visual interpretation . P er -vessel analysis yielded an 

AUC of 0.78 (95% CI: 0.76 –0.80). External validation on 555 pa tients confirmed robustness (AUC = 0.80, 95% 

CI: 0.76 –0.84).  Interpretability was achieved through gradient -based attention  maps showing important regions 

in the myocardium ; however, interpretability was image -driven. 

A deep learning –based framework for st ress -only MPI CAD classification was proposed in  [36] , by  Liu et al. , to 

address inefficiencies  in conventional stress –rest imaging workflows. Deep convolutional networks were used to 

extract features from stress SPECT polar maps, which were combined with selected clinical variables.  The 

proposed model attained a  reported accuracy of approximately 79 % and AUC values around 0.80. The results 

showed that stress -only functional imaging augmented with clinical context can preserve diagnostic reliability. 

Nevertheless, the framework prioritised workflow efficiency over interpretability, remained largely da ta -driven, 

and was evaluated under a constrained acquisition protocol, limiting generalisability to more complex CAD 

presentations. 

1.2.2.  Multimodal studies for  SPN  diagnosis 

In the following section, w e present key studies that implement multimodal approaches for SPN malignancy 

characterization.  Studies relying solely on single -modality models based on either imaging or clinical data are 

not considered, as these usually have limited diagnostic ability a nd inherently biased outcomes  [9, 37 –40] .

In paper  [41] , Wang et al. tackled the problem of reducing false positives in CT -based lung cancer de tection 

using  explicit nodule morphology modeling in a deep learning framework. Their method combined a 3D 

convolutional neural network and a recurrent neural network for nodule detection, followed by CNN -based 

morphological feature extraction and XGBoost -based logistic regression for malignancy prediction. The 

experiments were carried out with the Lung Image  Database Consortium and Image Database Resource 

Initiative (LIDC -IDRI) dataset on  radiologist -annotated  nodule characteristics such as diameter, spicu lation, 

lobulation , and internal composition. The proposed system achieved an accuracy of 82.39%, with diameter, 

spiculation, and lobulation identified as the most influential predictors. Despite improved false -positive control, 

this framework  was  still co nfined to CT imaging analysis and heavily relying on hand -crafted morphology 

features without explicit modeling over clinical/imaging concepts. 

In study  [42] , He et al. addressed the need for highly  sensitive, non -invasive early lung cancer diagnosis by 

integrating imaging, clinical variables, and cell -free DNA methylation biomarkers. Leveraging a large 

multicenter cohort of 1,380 patients across 24  hospitals to develop and extend the initial model wi th the 

addition of molecular biomarker information (PulmoSeek Plus), CIBM was developed. The combined 

framework achieved sensitivities up to 0.98, particularly for early -stage and small nodules. However, although it 

performed with high diagnostic accuracy  and was externally validated, the approach require d specialized 

molecular assays,  increased  clinical cost and complexity, and  provided  limited interpretability regarding the 

interaction between biological, imaging, and clinical risk factors 

1.2.3.  DeepFCM studies 

Several studies have examined the incorporation of DL architectures with FCMs, leading to hybrid frameworks 

[32] –[35]. 

In this context, DeepFCM is an FCM -based multimodal methodology developed by our research team within 

the funded EMERALD project  [43] , integrating deep fea ture extraction with fuzzy cognitive reasoning to 

support interpretable medical diagnosis . The DeepFCM architecture has since been applied to multimodal 

settings that incorporate both clinical and imaging data for diseases such as CAD and SPN. By fusing cl inical 

variables  with CNN -derived imaging  predictions , it  achieved high diagnostic accuracy while maintaining strong 

interpretability through  its  underlying reasoning mechanisms.  For all  experiments , the initialization of the 

interconnections, experts'  knowledge was applied in the form of linguistic values , which were later transformed 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 4 of 36 

into a spectrum of numerical values . In these studies,  the graph is built using both clinical and imaging 

characteristics. Then, meta -heuristic methods , including Particle  Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic  Algorithm 

(GA), and Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) -based strategies, are used to determine the optimal  edge weights. 

DeepFCM  was first introduced in  [31]  to classify malignant SPNs using clinical data an d PET scans. The 

clinical features included SUVmax uptake derived from the PET scan, SPN diameter, and the decision output of 

an RGB -CNN  (originally introduced in  [44] ), which was  responsible for analyzing the PET image 

independently. The  DeepFCM  model  attained 9 4.71 % accuracy with 2.99% standard  deviation , assessed by 10 -

fold cross -validation , and displayed the relationships between input and output concepts through a concept 

graph .

DeepFCM  achieved 77.95% accuracy in diagnosing CAD in  [45] , utilizing  twenty -two clinical characteristics 

and  RGB -CNN predictions as an additional concept attained from the training on  MPI  polar maps . The model’s 

explainability was demonstrated by comparing the connections among input -output concepts with relationships 

initialized by experts and weights initialized randomly.  In  [30] , DeepFCM’s training functionalities were 

improved, achieving an accuracy of 83.07% for the same task.  Using a Random Forest –based feature selection 

process, we identified a subset of eight informative clinical features. These eight features,  along with the CNN 

predictions obtained from the  RGB -CNN trained on  Polar Maps, were used as the final input concept s. As an 

imaging XAI technique,  Gradient -weighted Class Activation Mapping ( Grad -CAM ) highlighted the pixel areas 

that significantly influenced the  RGB -CNNs’  decisions. Additionally,  Generative Pre -trained Transformer -

Application Programming Interface ( GP T-API ) transformed  DeepFCM outputs into clear, understandable 

language. 

Deep -FCM -PSO was employed  in  [46]  using an expanded dataset  comprising nine clinical characteristics and 

PET images to effectively identify malignant SPNs, achieving  88.14% accuracy. Grad -CAM was used to 

highlight malignant areas in the PET images based on RGB -CNN feature maps. GPT -API provided natural 

language ex planations for the DeepFCM -PSO outputs. 

In study  [47]  Extreme Learning Machine (ELMs)  was  incorporated into the DeepFCM  learning framework as 

the core training mechanism for estimating inter -concept relationships. Within this formulation, ELM was 

employed to analytically compute the output weight matrix of the FCM using the Moore –Penrose 

pseudoinverse, enabling fast  learni ng  of the interconnection weights that minimise the prediction error. The 

resulting model, termed DeepFCM -ELM, achieved an accuracy of 80.4% ± 4.97% for CAD diagnosis and 

90.57% for SPN identification. Model interpretability was supported through explicit  visualisation of the 

weighted concept graph, allowing direct inspection of the influence of all clinical and imaging features. In 

parallel, Grad -CAM was applied to interpret the RGB -CNN component, highlighting image regions most 

influential for the CNN pre dictions. The DeepFCM -ELM outputs were further translated into structured natural -

language explanations using the GPT -API. All components were integrated into a unified medical decision 

support system (MDSS) with a clinician -oriented interface and evaluate d by medical experts. 

1.2.4.  Limitations of the literature studies 

Existing multimodal approaches for CAD and SPN diagnosis have shown promising performance ; however, 

important limitations remain. In many DeepFCM -based systems, multimodal fusion is performed by c ombining 

clinical variables with the final predictions of CNN models, rather than with structured or intermediate image 

representations. In such cases, imaging information is compressed into a single decision signal, limiting the 

richness of image -derived  features and reducing the contribution of imaging evidence to the overall reasoning 

process.  Conversely, in other multimodal frameworks, learning is primarily driven by imaging -derived features, 

with clinical variables incorporated only as secondary modifi ers. This modality imbalance results in inconsistent 

integration strategies, where either clinical or imaging information dominates the decision process,  thus limiting 

transparency and interpretability. Moreover, the FCM connections are usually initialized  randomly or using 

domain knowledge, which may not accurately reflect data -driven relationships among features and can 

negatively affect convergence. 

Interpretability is also commonly addressed in a limited or post hoc manner. Many approaches rely on a single 

explainability technique, such as saliency maps, which does not influence how clinical and imaging information 

are integrated during learning. Mor eover, natural -language explanations are often generated using proprietary, 

token -based large language models, introducing cost, scalability, and reproducibility constraints. These 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 5 of 36 

limitations motivate the development of a unified multimodal framework that  integrates clinical variables with 

richer image representations, adopts data -driven initialization strategies, and supports transparent, interpretable, 

and reproducible clinical reasoning, which is the aim of the present study. 

1.3.  Aim of this study 

This pap er fills these gaps with several new contributions. 

 Firstly,  the proposed FCM -Encoder is constructed by employing modality -specific RGB -CNN 

encoders that transform imaging inputs into semantically meaningful concept representations, which 

are subsequently  combined with structured clinical variables to form a unified multimodal concept 

space. 

 Second,  the proposed FCM -Encoder employs a PSO -based learning strategy with Lévy -flight 

perturbations to enhance global exploration during weight optimisation, improvi ng convergence , while 

preserving sparsity and interpretability of the learned FCM structure. 

 Furthermore , it replaces traditional expert -driven FCM weight initialization with data -driven Spearman 

correlation so that the initial adjacency really represents true dependency between features rather than 

subjective expert assumptions. 

 Lastly,  the framework replace s proprietary token -based NLG APIs with the open -source Qwen3 -VL 

model, allowing the generation of transparent, clinical narratives derived from FCM reasoning and 

patient -specific activations. 

## 2.  Materials and Methods 

2.1.  CAD data 

A total of 2036 patients underwe nt gated SPECT MPI between February 2018 and February 2022 at the Nuclear 

Medicine Department at the University Hospital of Patras.  The ethical committee of the University General 

Hospital of Patras approved the data collection process.  Attenuation  correction was applied to all the gathered 

images. After ICA within 60 days and applying exclusion criteria, 594 patients were selected, with 43.82% 

CAD -positive. The twenty -two clinical characteristics considered are: 1. Sex, 2. Age, 3. body mass index (B MI), 

4. known CAD, 5. previous acute myocardial infarction (AMI), 6. previous percutaneous coronary intervention 

(PCI), 7. previous coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), 8. previous STROKE, 9. Diabetes, 10. Smoking, 11. 

Hypertension, 12. Dyslipidemia, 13 . Angiopathy, 14. Chronic Kidney Disease, 15. Family History of CAD, 16. 

Asymptomatic, 17. Atypical Symptoms, 18. ANGINA LIKE, 19. Dyspnea on Exertion, 20. Incident of 

Precordial Pain, and 21. ECG, along with the 22. Expert Diagnosis with one output node,  the CAD diagnosis, 

which is normal and pathological. 

2.2.  PET/CT data 

The PET/CT image data  have  been recorded in the Clinical Sector of the Department of Nuclear Medicine at the 

University Hospital of Patras using a hybrid PET/CT scanner (Discovery iQ3 sl16, General Electric Healthcare). 

The data collection process was approved by the Ethics Committ ee of the University General Hospital of Patras. 

This system uses three detector rings with a 15cm field of view to reconstruct 35 axial images at 4.25mm 

intervals. 3D volumes are acquired to represent the whole body using various bed positions. At the sam e time, 

the patient was in a supine position. Two experienced human readers (N.P., 10 years of experience, D.J.A., 30 

years of experience) characterized the SPN malignancy  with  patient follow -up. The study's nature waives the 

requirement for obtaining pati ents' informed consent. From 2020 to 2023, more than 800 PET/CT scans were 

reviewed to identify  potential  participants. Patients without detected SPNs or with SPNs with a diameter greater 

than 30mm were excluded. 456 patients with a single SPN were qualifi ed. The total benign cases were 222 , and 

the total malignant cases were 234. Experts annotated CT scan slices, noting the finding's type, location, 

margins, diameter, and SUVmax and SPN diameter , along with demographic information about each patient 

(gender, age, BMI).  Also, the GLU variable is included . The SUVmax and diameter parameters were extracted 

from the PET scan. Each SPN finding is represented by a single 2D slice in which the full exten t of the nodule is 

visible. Ultimately, the dataset’s features were age, gender, BMI, SUVmax,  diameter, and three categorical 

variables : location ( left  upper,  right  upper,  left  lower,  right  lower,  middle,  lingula),  type ( solid,  ground -class, 

consolidated,  speckled,  calcified,  cavitary), and  margins ( spiculated,  lobulated,  well -defined,  ill -defined). Each 

categorical feature is one -hot encoded into  binary  variables.

2.3.  Proposed methodology 

In this study, we propose the FCM -Encoder framework for two diagnostic tasks: CAD diagnosis and SPN 

characterization. The proposed methodology represents clinical variables and image -derived features as the 

nodes (concepts) of an FCM. Clinical nodes corres pond to explicit medical attributes, whereas image nodes are 

latent features extracted by the CNN encoders, whose clinical associations are characterised separately through 

correlation analysis. 

For CAD diagnosis, structured clinical variables are  initiall y normalised and transformed into clinical concept 

activations, forming the clinical input stream of the FCM. In parallel, myocardial perfusion polar -map images 

are  trained  by an RGB -CNN encoder  to learn task -relevant imaging representations. The trained e ncoder 

compresses the polar maps into a set of latent imaging concepts suitable for integration within the FCM. 

For SPN diagnosis, clinical and demographic variables are similarly normalised and represented as clinical 

concept activations, while CT and PET  images are processed by modality -specific RGB -CNN encoders  trained 

in the same manner .

For each task, the resulting imaging and clinical concepts are jointly integrated within the FCM -based 

architecture, enabling unified multimodal reasoning over heteroge neous data sources. 

In both applications, the connection from each input concept to the diagnostic output is initialised from the 

Spearman rank correlation between that concept and the label, providing a data -driven prior, while all concept -

to -concept inte rconnections are initialised at zero and refined during optimisation. During training, the FCM -

Encoder adopts an enhanced particle swarm optimisation (PSO) strategy to iteratively refine the interconnection 

weight matrix, allowing clinical and imaging conc epts to jointly influence the final prediction. Compared with 

conventional PSO, the proposed optimiser incorporates four key extensions: (i) a linearly decreasing inertia 

weight to regulate the exploration –exploitation balance; (ii) numerical gradient appr oximation for local solution 

refinement; (iii) Lévy flight –based perturbations to facilitate escape from local optima; and (iv) sparsity -aware 

regularisation to encourage clinically interpretable cognitive graphs. 

The final system integrates multiple expla inability -enhancing components. The learned interconnections among 

concepts are visualised, highlighting the most influential clinical characteristics guiding the prediction. A 

complementary Spearman correlation analysis is additionally performed between e ach learned imaging concept 

and the named clinical variables, assigning a derived clinical label to every informative image feature. Finally, 

the Qwen3 -VL model, an open vision –language model (VLM), is employed to translate the numerical outputs of 

the FCM -Encoder into human -understandable clinical explanations. A schematic representation of the complete 

methodology is shown in Figure 1.

In the following subsections, we provide  a detailed description of the proposed methodology. 

2.3.1.  Clinical  data preprocessing 

For CAD diagnosis, the continuous clinical variables were scaled to comparable ranges: age  and BMI  were 

normalised  to [0, 1] using min –max scaling, preventing variables with larger numeric ranges from dominating 

the learning process  [48] . The categorical variable gender was binarised, and all remaining clinical variables 

were already encoded in binary form, indicating absence (0) or presence (1). 

For SPN diagnosis, age was likewise min –max normalised, while body mass index (BMI), glucose (GLU), 

standardised uptake value (SUV), and nodule diameter were each scaled by dividing by a fixed maximum value 

(70, 192, 30, and 3, respectively) to bring all numerical columns onto a comparable scale.  Categorical clinical 

features, such as nodule location, type, and margins, were one -hot encoded, converting each category into a 

separate binary column. This representation allows each clinical attribute to be treated as an independent 

concept within the  FCM  [48] .

2.3.2.  Imaging encoder 

A lightweight RGB convolutional neural  network (RGB -CNN), adapted from the architecture introduced in  [44] 

for bone -metastasis diagnosis,  where it is employed in our study  as an imaging feature encoder. Its role is not to 

perform classification but to transform raw imaging inputs into numerical representations that summarise the 

most rel evant visual information. The configuration used for each modality is described below. 

For the CAD task, the RGB -CNN was trained on polar -map images. It comprises three convolutional blocks 

with 32, 64, and 128 filters (3×3 kernels), each followed by batch  normalisation, ReLU activation, 2×2 max -

pooling, and dropout (rate = 0.15); L2 weight regularisation (1×10⁻ ⁴)  is applied throughout. The feature maps 

are reduced by global average pooling and passed through a 128 -unit ReLU dense layer, followed by a  8-uni t

concept layer whose activations form the image concepts, and finally a sigmoid output used for normal –

pathological classification during  RGB -CNN  training . Images were resized to 300×300 and trained with a batch 

size of 16 using the Adam optimiser and bin ary cross -entropy loss (label smoothing 0.05). Light augmentation 

(random horizontal flips and random rotations) and early stopping on the validation loss (patience = 6, best 

weights restored) were used to limit overfitting. 

For the SPN task, two separate  RGB -CNN encoders were trained, one for CT and one for PET, sharing the same 

architecture but with modality -specific weights. Each comprises four convolutional blocks with 32, 64, 128, and 

256 filters, each with batch normalisation, ReLU, 2×2 max -pooling, a nd dropout (rate = 0.25), followed by 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 8 of 36 

global average pooling, a 128 -unit ReLU dense layer, an 8 -unit concept layer, and a sigmoid output for benign –

malignant classification. Training used 100×100 inputs, a batch size of 16, the Adam optimiser, and binary 

cross -entropy loss (label smoothing 0.05), with random flip/rotation augmentation and early stopping (patience 

= 8). The CT and PET concept layers yield eight concepts each, giving sixteen imaging concepts in total. 

During the  training of the RGB -CNN  encoder , each RGB -CNN is trained in a supervised manner on the 

diagnostic labels so that its concept layer learns task -discriminative features. After  this phase,  the encoder 

weights are frozen , and the sigmoid classification head is discarded; only the concept -layer activations are 

forwarded to the FCM. The standalone RGB -CNN accuracy reported in Table 2 , therefore reflects the encoder 

used in isolation as a baseline classifier and does not repr esent the decision produced by the FCM -Encoder. 

Keeping the encoder fixed thereafter preserves the learned visual semantics and represents the imaging data in a 

reusable form for integration with the clinical concepts in the FCM reasoning layer. 

2.3.3.  FCM -Encode r implementation: concept -level fusion of clinical and imaging 

streams 

Following  the  preprocessing of the clinical variables and extraction of image -derived concepts by the frozen 

CNN encoder, the  imaging  stream  is  integrated at the concept level. For each  subject, the normalised clinical 

feature vector is directly concatenated with the corresponding image -concept vector, and the combined vector is 

standardised to form the multimodal input representation that serves as the input layer of the FCM.  This fused 

representation is augmented with a dedicated output concept initialized to 0.5, resulting in an initial concept 

state  A0 ∈ ℝ 𝑁+1 , which serves as the starting point for the FCM -Encoder. Through this formulation, both 

clinical and image -derived concepts are treated uniformly as input nodes within the same FCM structure, 

allowing the model to analyse associations between patient char acteristics and  imaging patterns in a shared 

concept space. 

For the CAD classification task, eight image -derived concepts extracted from polar map images , and  were 

concatenated with twenty -two clinical variables, forming a thirty -concept multimodal FCM, pl us one diagnostic 

output node.  For the SPN classification task, eight CT -derived concepts and eight PET -derived concepts were 

combined with nine clinical attributes, expanded to twenty -five concept nodes by one -hot encoding of the 

categorical variables, yi elding a forty -one -concept multimodal input layer, again augmented with a single 

diagnostic output node .

At the fusion stage, standard feature normalization (StandardScaler) is applied to the combined concept vector 

to ensure that all clinical and imaging  concepts lie on a comparable  numerical scale. This step prevents 

dominance due to differences in feature magnitude and ensures that the relative influence of each concept is 

determined by the learned FCM interconnections rather than by scale effects. 

Wit hin the proposed framework, the fused multimodal representation is governed by a shared FCM concept 

layer, where each dimension corresponds either to a clinical variable or to an image -derived concept. 

To clarify this distinction, two categories of input concepts are  defined  within the FCM. The first category 

contains named clinical concepts, that is, pre -defined and clinically interpretable variables such as age, diabetes, 

and smoking history, whose sem antic meaning is fixed  before  training. The second category contains learned 

imaging features, that is, the continuous activations produced by the final concept layer of the RGB -CNN 

encoder (eight per branch). Unlike the clinical variables, the learned ima ging features are not pre -specified 

medical attributes; they emerge from  the RGB -CNN  training  (imaging encoder)  and acquire their semantic 

content through their statistical association with the diagnostic label. The remainder of this paper retains the 

term  "concepts" for both categories to preserve consistency with the FCM literature, but the distinction between 

named and learned concepts is maintained throughout. The interpretability of the learned imaging features is 

examined in  Sections  3.1.6 and 3.1.12  for CAD and SPN diagnosis, respectively. 

Finally, the full dataset was partitioned into an 80% training set and a 20% external hold -out test set. The 

training portion was further evaluated using 10 -fold cross -validation (random seed = 42) to ensure 

reprodu cibility of the reported results .

2.3.4.  Weight initialization 

The model integrates a data -driven initialization strategy that aims  to  align each concept's initial influence on the 

diagnostic outcome . Rather than  relying  on expert -defined interconnections,  the  influence of each input concept 

on the outcome is computed . Spearman’s rank correlation has been frequently applied as a global and non -

parametric measure, ensuring robustness to non -linear and non -Gaussian feature distributions commonly 

encountered in cli nical data  [49, 50] . For every input concept  j (clinical and imaging), the Spearman correlation 

coefficient  𝑝 𝑗  with respect to the outcome is estimated and used directly as the initial weight from that concept 

to the output node ( 𝑤 {𝑗 →𝑜𝑢𝑡 } = 𝑝 𝑗  ). Its sign encodes the direction of influence , posi tive and negative values 

indicating an increasing or decreasing monotonic relationship, respectively , and its magnitude the initial 

strength. Undefined correlations (e.g., for constant features) are set to zero, so that no directional bias is imposed 

and t he model determines their influence during learning  [50, 51] .

These correlation -based priors are applied to all input concepts, both clinical and imaging,  affecting only the 

edges that link each input to the diagnostic  output ; all concept -to -concept edges are initialized at zero and 

learned during optimisation. In this way, the initialization provides an empirically grounded starting point for 

each concept's influence on the outcome without prescribing a fixed structure. 

2.3.5.  Parti cle Swarm Optimization 

Following the proposed correlation -based initialization, the interconnection weights of the FCM must be further 

refined through a training procedure that can effectively search the high -dimensional and non -convex parameter 

space. In  this work, Particle Swarm Optimisation (PSO) is adopted as a population -based learning strategy for 

adjusting the cognitive weight matrix in a fully data -driven manner. The main principles of PSO are briefly 

outlined next .

PSO  [52]  is a learning procedure that is inspired by  the cooperative behavior of a flock of birds. The particles 

that represent candidate solutions are deployed across the search space.  Each  particle  behaves  as  a potential 

solution has its position and velocity  vector. These particles  are distributed within the search  space,  and they 

change their trajectories over time after learning from  their  own experience and information shared with others. 

Each pa rticle track s its best -known position, denoted as  𝑝 𝑖  , which influences its future  actions . Similarly , the 

swarm  keeps a  record  of  the global best position  𝑔  found by any particle.  This allows the particles to collaborate 

from their continuous  interaction, toward a single best solution . A detailed  description of the PSO approach is 

available in the literature  [29, 30, 53] .

The weight matrix  that constitutes the total of interconnections  in an FCM is highly influential, as it directly 

determines both the predictive performance of the model and the plausibility of the learned inter -concept 

relationships. Therefore, the  training  process must minimise classification error while ensuring that  the resulting 

cognitive structure remains consistent with empirical clinical tendencies and historical evidence, when available 

[31] . In the present study, we propose the pioneering FCM -Encoder method for estimating the weight matrix in 

order to effectively capture the interconnections among concepts . In the  following, we present the equations 

that constitute the proposed model.

2.4.  Methods for Interpreting Model Outputs 

In this section, we  describe the explainable methods implemented in the proposed diagnostic framework. These 

methods are designed to improve the transparency and interpretability of model by associating multimodal 

inputs with concept interactions. 

2.4.1.  Generat ed Concept Interconnections (Weight Matrix) 

Within the proposed framework, interpretability of the clinical reasoning pathway is grounded in the weighted 

interconnections between clinical concepts and the model’s diagnostic output.  The output edge of every  input 

concept is initialised from its Spearman rank correlation with the label, computed on the training split.  For the 

named clinical concepts, these correlation -based priors are directly interpretable: the sign indicates whether the 

variable has a posit ive or negative monotonic association with malignancy, and the magnitude its initial strength. 

They initialise the corresponding outgoing edges to the diagnostic output, providing a clinically structured 

starting point aligned with the associations observe d in the training data. 

Each weighted edge connecting a clinical concept to the output node is characterized by both a magnitude and a 

sign. The absolute value indicates relative importance with respect to prediction, while the sign indicates 

whether incr easing the variable is expected to increase or decrease the estimated malignancy probability . During 

FCM -Encoder optimization, these correlation -informed initial  weights  may be  altered  when supported by the 

data, allowing the final structure to reconcile prior statistical evidence with learned multimodal interactions. 

After training, the learned clinical weight matrix provides an explicit representation of the direction and relative 

str ength of influence between individual clinical concepts and the diagnostic output.  Each weight represents 

both the direction and strength of influence, allowing the FCM structure to be inspected directly without post -

hoc explanation methods. 

2.4.2.  Clinical  Inter pretation of the Learned Imaging Features 

Unlike the named clinical variables, the CNN -derived imaging features enter the FCM as data -driven activations 

without  a priori medical  attributes . A complementary analysis is performed at the end of training to associate 

each informative imaging feature with a named clinical concept. The imaging features are first ranked by the 

absolute magnitude of their learned FCM weight to the diagnostic output , identifying those on which the model 

most strongly relies. Each feature is then correlated with every named clinical variable using Spearman's rank 

coefficient, and the  clinical variable with the strongest absolute Spearman correlation is adopted as the  derived 

label, with its associated p -value reported . Visual confirmation is finally obtained by inspecting, for each 

informative feature, the input images (polar maps for CAD, CT/PET  images  for SPN) of the patients with the 

highest activation.

2.4.3.  Natural Lang uage Generation 

FCMs provide an explicit structure through concept nodes and weighted interconnections , however, expressing 

these numerical influences in clinically meaningful language requires an additional interpretive layer.  For this 

purpose, a n open  multimodal large language model (Qwen3 -VL), as introduced by Bai et al. in  [59] , was 

employed as a narrative generation component and deployed via the Ollama inference framework. The model 

operates strictly in inference mode and  is not involved in prediction, classification, or diagnostic decision -

making. In the proposed study, it is used exclusively to convert model outputs into structured, clinician -oriented 

explanations. 

At inference time, the prompt to Qwen3 -VL includes  four  elements: (i) the  FCM -Encoder  output label and its 

full set of concept weights, (ii) the patient -specific clinical concept activations, (iii) the polar map for CAD or 

CT/PET scans for SPN diagnosis , (iv) the RGB -CNN prediction for the image instances acco rding to the 

diagnostic task  and (v) guidance about the structure of the generated response . These inputs are provided as 

contextual evidence for explanation, not for learning.  The prompt includes the following rules . For the clinical 

section, only activated concepts with  a positive influence are eligible for explanation. Demographic variables, 

numerical encodings, and shortcut diagnostic inputs (e.g., sex, age, BMI, expert diagnosis) are explicitly 

excluded. Each sel ected feature must be described using disease -specific medical terminology without reference 

to binary or numerical values.  For the imaging section, Qwen3 -VL analyses the provided image together with 

domain -specific instructions. In CAD cases, explanations  focus on perfusion defects, ischemic patterns, stress –

rest reversibility, and correspondence to coronary territories (LAD, RCA, LCx). In SPN cases, explanations 

focus on lesion morphology, margins, uptake behaviour, and spatial context. Visual layout arte facts and 

embedded text are ignored.  This design separates prediction and reasoning (performed by CNN and  FCM -

Encoder ) from language generation, ensuring that explanations are derived from model outputs and images, 

while remaining clinically structured, re producible, and constrained by fixed prompting rules.  The prompt that 

was inserted  into  Qwen3 -VL for inference of the CAD instance is available in the Appendix.

3.1.4.  Comparison with Graph Neural Network Baselines  for CAD diagnosis 

Because the FCM -Encoder and graph neural networks (GNNs) both learn weighted relations between feature 

nodes, three representative GNN baselines were compared against the proposed model while the rest of the 

multimodal pipeline was preserved. The three spa n the main families of graph learning. The GCN of Kipf and 

Welling  [66]  represents the spectral family: each layer updates a node through a symmetrically normalised , self -

loop -augmented averaging of its neighbours' features followed by a learnable linear transformation, and in its 

original form operates transductively over a fixed graph. The Graph Attention Network (GAT) of Veličković et 

al.  [67]  represents the attention -based family, replacing these fixed aggregation weights with learned per -ed ge 

attention coefficients (four heads in the first layer) so that informative neighbours contribute more strongly, 

while additionally yielding edge -level importance scores. GraphSAGE, of Hamilton et al.  [68] , represents the 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 19 of 36 

inductive family, learning a mean aggregator over a sampled neighbourhood that generalises naturally to nodes 

unseen during training. 

All baselines op erate on the same feature stream as the proposed model: the RGB -CNN concept vector 

concatenated with the clinical variables. A population graph is built inductively per fold, with each patient 

connected to its five nearest training neighbours by cosine sim ilarity and no edges between validation nodes, so 

that label information cannot propagate among validation patients .

The three GNNs share a two -layer architecture with 24 hidden units and a dropout rate of 0.6, and are trained 

with the Adam optimiser (lear ning rate 1 × 10⁻ ²,  weight decay 2 × 10⁻ ³)  under cross -entropy loss for up to 150

3.2.  Natural Language Generation 

In this section, we report the results of the  LLM -assisted clinical narrative generation framework  to interpret the 

proposed FCM -Encoder  model  and RGB -CNN  decision -making process . The FCM -Encoder provides 

diagnostic predictions  for both CAD (using clinical variables and polar maps) and SPN diagnosis (using clinical 

variables together with CT and PET scans), while the R GB -CNN is used exclusively for imaging -based 

inference (polar maps for CAD and CT/PET scans for SPN). In addition to classification outputs, the FCM -

Encoder produces  interconnections  among concepts  for each diagnostic setting. 

Qwen -3VL is employed as a vis ion –language model to transform these quantitative outputs into structured, 

clinically readable narratives. Specifically, the generated FCM interconnections and activated concepts are used 

to identify the most influential clinical contributors for each pat ient, based on their relative activation strength 

and relational importance within the cognitive graph. These contributors are then described textually, without 

altering the underlying model predictions. In parallel, Qwen -3VL interprets imaging inputs , pol ar maps in the 

CAD task , and CT/PET scans in the SPN task  by translating salient visual patterns identified by the RGB -CNN 

into qualitative radiological or metabolic descriptors.

## 4.  Discussion 

This study int roduced the FCM -Encoder framework as a multimodal, interpretable model for CAD and SPN 

diagnosis.  Imaging  data are  processed through modality -specific RGB -CNN encoders and combined with 

normalised  clinical concept activations, representing both data streams in a unified conceptual space . Spearman 

rank correlations between each input concept and the diagnostic label were used to derive data -driven priors on 

the concept -to -output connections, while t he concept -to -concept connections were initialised at zero so that their 

influence could emerge through optimisation. The FCM weights were then  updated  by a hybrid PSO scheme 

enriched with Lévy -flight exploration  to escape local minima , gradient -based loca l adjustments, and sparsity -

aware regularisation. 

Relative to existing multimodal CAD and SPN studies, the proposed FCM -Encoder overcomes key limitations 

of conventional classical machine -learning approaches. Prior frameworks typically rely on decision - or  feature -

level concatenation,  which yields restricted interpretability. DeepFCM -based methods introduced reasoning and 

improved transparency but often depended on  expert -assigned weights, and the fusion relied on CNN 

predictions rather than imaging represe ntations.  In contrast, the FCM -Encoder integrates deep imaging 

representations and clinical variables within a unified concept -level space, using data -driven, sign -coherent 

clinical initialisation and neutral imaging concepts whose influence emerges throug h optimisation. This design 

delivers competitive diagnostic performance and clinically interpretable multimodal reasoning, addressing the 

transparency and robustness gaps observed in prior literature. Across the two diagnostic tasks, the FCM -Encoder 

reache d 81.82% ± 4.51% accuracy for CAD with clinical data and polar maps, and 93.39% ± 3.09% accuracy 

for SPN characterisation with clinical data and CT/PET scans, assessed by 10 -fold cross -validation. 

The ablation studies confirmed that the performance of the  FCM -Encoder is the product of the  combination  of 

its components rather than the dominance of any single one. Restricting the model to the clinical branch or to 

the imaging branch in isolation produced the largest reductions in accuracy  and loss  on both  dataset s, which 

indicates that the two modalities encode complementary rather than redundant diagnostic information and that 

the FCM reasoning layer is essential to integrate them. The removal of the optimisation components produced 

the following pattern:  disabling the PSO stage and relying on a randomly initialised FCM yielded the highest 

cross -entropy loss of any configuration despite a comparable accuracy, which demonstrates that PSO contributes 

less to classification correctness than to the calibration  of the resulting probability estimates. Disabling only the 

Lévy -flight escape step within PSO produced a smaller  loss , indicating that the global -search component of the 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 31 of 36 

optimiser contributes meaningfully when the loss surface contains local minima that gr adient -style refinement 

alone would not escape. Replacing the  Spearman correlation  prior with a zero -weight initialisation lowered 

accuracy  and increased loss , which suggests that the prior shapes the region of the weight space. Taken together, 

the configu rations show that the full model attains its performance because these contributions are 

complementary. 

The image -encoder comparison further supported the choice of the RGB -CNN. The FCM -Encoder paired with 

the RGB -CNN outperformed both end -to -end lightweig ht ImageNet -pretrained CNNs and frozen feature 

extractors, while using substantially fewer parameters per modality branch, which indicates that the advantage 

does not derive from encoder capacity but from the alignment between the visual representation and  the 

reasoning module that consumes it. Lightweight architectures such as MobileNetV3 -Small and EfficientNetB0 

are optimised for general -purpose natural -image classification and produce embeddings whose internal structure 

reflects the statistics of ImageNe t rather than those of nuclear medicine imagery, with the result that any 

subsequent classifier must compensate for this mismatch through additional fine -tuning. Frozen feature 

extractors are subject to the same limitation in a more pronounced form, since  their representations are fixed and 

cannot adapt to the target modality at all. The RGB -CNN, by contrast, is trained directly on the target imagery 

and produces concept -level features , attain ing  the lowest loss of all encoders examined. 

The FCM -Encoder wa s benchmarked against representative graph neural network baselines (GCN, GAT, and 

GraphSAGE) operating on the same concept -level feature stream . Across both datasets, the FCM -Encoder 

matched or exceeded the best graph baseline in accuracy and AUC while re maining competitive across 

sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F1 -score, which indicates that the advantage  arises  from the manner in 

which inter -concept influence is modelled. Graph neural networks propagate information along edges that are 

fixed pri or to training, and their reasoning is therefore constrained by the quality of the pre -specified similarity 

graph and by the assumption that patients proximate in concept space should also influence one another's 

predictions. The FCM -Encoder does not rely  on this assumption: it learns directed, signed influences between 

the concepts themselves, which constitutes a more faithful representation of clinical reasoning. The most 

pronounced difference was in loss, where the FCM -Encoder achieved a substantially lo wer cross -entropy than 

every graph baseline , which  is consistent with the PSO stage optimi zation . A further practical benefit is that the 

FCM learns its own connectivity during optimisation, removing the need to pre -specify a population graph and 

the asso ciated hyperparameter tuning required by graph -based models. 

The interpretability of the FCM -Encoder is supported by several complementary mechanisms. The Spearman -

based initialisation generated clinically coherent positive and negative pathways consistent  with established risk 

patterns across both disease domains, whereas random initialisation within [−1, 1] produced trainable but 

clinically incoherent interconnections . Moreover, a  complementary interpretability mechanism is provided by 

the analysis of the learned imaging features, which in both tasks could be linked to recognised clinical  findings .

For CAD, the Spearman labelling procedure assigned each informative polar -map concep t a named clinical 

attribute, and visual inspection of the most strongly activated images confirmed that the signed FCM weights are 

consistent with the underlying clinical content: the positive -weight concept responds to perfusion -defect patterns 

indicativ e of disease, whereas the negative -weight concept responds to normal -perfusion patterns.  For SPN, the 

two imaging branches captured separate diagnostic features established in clinical practice for malignancy 

assessment : the CT concepts encoded lesion size  (nodule diameter), with positive -weight concepts 

corresponding to larger, malignancy -prone nodules and negative -weight concepts to smaller, benign nodules, 

while the PET concepts encoded metabolic activity (FDG uptake), with positive -weight concepts corre sponding 

to high -uptake, malignancy -associated lesions and negative -weight concepts to low -uptake, benign lesions. The 

learned image concepts could therefore be tied to clinically meaningful markers in both applications, reinforcing 

the transparency of the  framework. 

Finally, the Qwen3 -VL model translate d the FCM interconnections, clinical attributes, and imaging evidence 

into structured, clinician -readable narratives without altering or inferring diagnostic decisions, contextualising 

rather than replacing  the FCM -Encoder and RGB -CNN predictions. The combined use of  interpreted 

interconnections among concepts  and vision –language explanation establishes a multi -perspective 

interpretability framework that enhances transparency and supports clinical validation. 

While the framework demonstrates strong interpretability and competitive diagnostic performance, several 

methodological considerations should be noted. First, the proposed multimodal optimisation procedure requires 

training of two modality -specific CNN en coders and performing population -based PSO weight search within 

## ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Page 32 of 36 

each cross -validation fold. Although this design enables fully data -driven multimodal concept integration, it 

increases runtime compared with single -stage gradient -trained classifiers and may  restrict applicability in 

settings where computational resources are limited. Second, the experimental evaluation is confined to a defined 

set of datasets and diagnostic tasks, selected to enable a focused and systematic analysis of the proposed 

methodolog y. Extending the evaluation to additional datasets and clinical scenarios represents a natural 

progression of this work and would better support the generalisation claims of the framework. As future work, 

all developed components, including the FCM -Encoder , optimisation strategies, explainability and narrative -

generation modules, will be consolidated into a unified software package, enabling systematic reuse, 

extensibility, and consistent evaluation across a broader range of multimodal diagnostic tasks. 

## 5.  Conclusions 

The proposed FCM -Encoder framework introduces a novel and interpretable approach to multimodal medical 

decision support for CAD diagnosis and SPN characterisation. By unifying modality -specific imaging encoders 

with structured clinical variable s inside an FCM -based cognitive reasoning model, the method achieves 

competitive diagnostic performance while maintaining  explicit structural transparency throughout the decision 

process.  The  FCM  weight matrix is initialised through a Spearman correlation –guided strategy, aligning each 

concept's initial influence on the diagnostic output with the associations found in the training data, and is then 

refined through a hybrid PSO scheme enriched with Lévy -flight exploration.  Systematic ablation, image -

encoder,  and graph neural network comparisons confirmed three findings. First, each architectural element of 

the framework contributes positively to performance. Second,  the RGB -CNN  encoder matches or exceeds 

substantially larger pretrained alternatives. Third, th e FCM -based reasoning module outperforms representative 

graph -based architectures while preserving signed, concept -level interpretability . A complementary correlation 

analysis further assigned a derived clinical label to each informative learned image conc ept, ensuring that the 

imaging -derived nodes of the FCM carry a meaningful interpretation on the same footing as the named clinical 

variables. The integration of concept -level interaction reasoning, weight -based saliency interpretation, and 

vision –language  narrative generation via Qwen3 -VL provides clinicians with clinically meaningful insight into 

both imaging evidence and multimodal concept dependencies. Given the critical need for reliability, 

transparency, and interpretability in medical AI, the FCM -Enc oder constitutes a reliable and practically relevant 

infrastructure for multimodal diagnostic reasoning in healthcare.
