Abstract Purpose: This study aimed to develop and validate a classification model for preoperative prediction of WHO grading of meningioma based on spatial habitat radiomics that can be explained by Ki-67 and p53 biomarkers. 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Methods: A retrospective cohort of 400 patients (261 WHO grade I; 139 grade II/III) from a single institution was analyzed. Contrast-enhancing tumors were partitioned into three biologically distinct subregions (hypo-, intermediate-, and hyper-enhancement) using a 3D Simple Linear Iterative Clustering algorithm. Radiomics and clinical-radiological features were selected based on dual associations with Ki-67≥5% and p53≥5%. 

Subsequently, three classification models for preoperative prediction of WHO grading were constructed: clinical-radiological model (C-model), whole-tumor radiomics model (CRW-model), and subregion-based radiomics model (CRS-model). Performance was evaluated using AUC in training (n=280) and test (n=120) cohorts. 

Results: Spatial habitat radiomics demonstrated biomarker-specific predictive power: hyper-enhancement subregions achieved the highest Ki-67 prediction AUC (training: 0.81; test: 0.70), while hypo-enhancement subregions excelled in p53 prediction (training: 0.93; test: 0.57). The CRS-model, achieved the highest AUC in both the training cohort (0.89 vs. 0.80 for CRW-model and 0.76 for C-model) and the test cohort (0.76 vs. 0.68 and 0.67, respectively). In the training cohort, the CRS-model significantly outperformed both the CRW-model and C-model after Bonferroni correction (adjusted p = 0.000450 and 7.06 × 10⁻⁷, respectively). In the test cohort, the CRS-model showed numerically higher AUCs, but the differences were not statistically significant after correction (adjusted p = 0.0891 and 0.0918, respectively). 

Conclusions: Spatial habitat radiomics may help characterize intratumoral microenvironmental heterogeneity associated with Ki-67 and p53 expression, while providing a biologically interpretable framework for preoperative WHO grading of meningiomas. 

Keywords: Ki-67; p53; spatial habitat analysis; radiomics; meningioma grading; tumor heterogeneity. 

1.Introduction 

Meningiomas are the most common primary intracranial tumors, accounting for more than one-third of all primary central nervous system tumors [1] . Their biological behavior is critically influenced by the World Health Organization (WHO) grade, which is closely associated with recurrence risk, progression-free survival, and postoperative management [2] . Despite rapid progress in molecular characterization, clinical management remains largely based on surgery and radiotherapy, and systemic therapies are mainly considered for treatment-refractory cases or clinical trials [3] . Recently, somatostatin receptor-targeted imaging with DOTA-conjugated peptides has 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS enabled visualization of receptor expression and identification of candidates for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT) [4, 5] . Emerging evidence suggests that PRRT is a promising option for selected patients with relapsed/refractory meningiomas, and artificial intelligence-based strategies may further assist dose optimization and side-effect monitoring [6] . In this context, non-invasive imaging biomarkers that can identify aggressive biological behavior before surgery may be helpful for individualized risk stratification and treatment planning. In neuropathological practice, Ki-67 and p53 serve as important biomarkers for meningioma prognostication. Ki-67, a proliferation index, marks hyperproliferative subregions associated with accelerated tumor growth [7, 8] while p53 nuclear positivity may indicate aberrant TP53-related biology and has been associated with aggressive tumor phenotypes [9-11] . Although Ki-67 and p53 are not formally incorporated into WHO grading criteria, previous studies have demonstrated their association higher histological grade and recurrence risk, suggesting that these biomarkers may provide complementary information for tumor characterization [8, 10] . Therefore, identifying imaging phenotypes associated with Ki-67 and p53 may help improve the biological interpretability of MRI-based meningioma grading [12] .Contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (CE-T1WI) provides important information regarding tumor vascularity and blood–tumor barrier disruption. Hyper-enhancing regions may reflect increased vascular permeability or angiogenic activity, whereas hypo-enhancing regions may correspond to necrotic, hypovascular, or hypoxia-related microenvironmental components [13-15] . Tumor oxygenation is an important determinant of cancer biology and treatment response, and hypoxia-related heterogeneity [16] has been extensively discussed in glioblastoma [17] . Although these mechanisms have not been fully validated in meningiomas, they provide a biological rationale for investigating enhancement-defined habitats as potential surrogates of heterogeneous tumor microenvironments. MRI-based radiomics has been increasingly used for preoperative prediction of meningioma grade, biological markers, and aggressive behavior [18] . For example, Zhao et al. developed a multiparametric MRI radiomics model incorporating CE-T1WI, T2WI, and tumor-to-brain interface features for WHO grading prediction [19] . Karabacak et al. further reported a large-scale multi-institutional radiomics-driven machine learning study using the BraTS-MEN dataset, highlighting both the potential and the generalizability challenges of radiomics-based meningioma grading [20] . Other recent studies have explored MRI radiomics for predicting 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS mitotic activity, Ki-67, p53, NF2 alteration, TERT promoter mutation, progesterone receptor status, and brain invasion [21, 22] . Nevertheless, most existing radiomics models focus on whole-tumor or peritumoral features, and the biological meaning of selected features often remains unclear. Spatial habitat imaging has recently emerged as a strategy to characterize intratumoral heterogeneity by partitioning tumors into phenotypically distinct subregions [23-25] . Compared with whole-tumor radiomics, habitat imaging better preserve spatial information and may help identify subregions associated with hypoxia, angiogenesis, cellularity, or treatment resistance [24] .However, its application in meningioma remains limited, and the relationship between habitat-specific radiomic features and biological markers such as Ki-67 and p53 has not been sufficiently investigated. To address this gap, we hypothesized that CE-T1WI-based spatial habitat radiomics could capture Ki-67/p53-associated angiogenic heterogeneity in meningiomas. In this study, contrast-enhancing tumors were partitioned into hypo-, intermediate-, and hyper-angiogenic subregions using a three-dimensional Simple Linear Iterative Clustering (SLIC) algorithm. Radiomic and clinical-radiological features jointly associated with Ki-67 and p53 were selected and integrated to construct a biologically interpretable model for preoperative WHO grading. Unlike conventional radiomics models that are primarily optimized for statistical discrimination, our approach aims to link habitat-specific imaging phenotypes with biologically relevant markers of proliferation and tumor aggressiveness. 

2.Materials and methods 2.1 Study Design and Patient Selection 

This retrospective study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine (approval number: 2021-0566), with waived informed consent due to the retrospective nature. All the methods were carried out in accordance with the requirements of China Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and Declaration of Helsinki(2013 revision). Initially, 2,878 meningioma patients with pathological confirmation from January 2011 to August 2020 were screened. A total of 460 patients were included according to the following inclusion criteria: (1) Preoperative MRI with T1-weighted (T1WI), T2-weighted (T2WI), and contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (CE-T1WI) within 1 week before surgery; (2) Available immunohistochemical (IHC) data for Ki-67 and p53.

A final cohort of 400 patients (261 low-grade [WHO I], 139 high-grade [124 grade II; 15 grade III]) was randomly split into training (70%, n=280, [ 190 grade I, 80 grade II, 10 grade III] ) and test cohorts (30%, n=120, [ 71 grade I, 44 grade II, 5 grade III]). The flow chart of the patient selection process is demonstrated in Figure 1. 

2.2 MRI Acquisition 

All MRI examinations were performed using routine clinical scanning protocols on eight MRI scanners, including 3.0-T systems (GE Discovery MR750, GE Discovery MR750W, GE Signa HDxt, and United Imaging uMR 790) and 1.5-T systems (Siemens MAGNETOM Aera, Siemens MAGNETOM Avanto, Siemens MAGNETOM Sonata, and GE Signa HDxt). The CE-T1WI scans were acquired after a bolus injection of 0.2 mL/kg of contrast agent. Detailed scanner information and acquisition parameters are provided in Supplementary Table S1. 

2.3 Immunohistochemical (IHC) Analysis 

Histopathological evaluation was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tumor specimens obtained during surgical resection. IHC for Ki-67 (clone MIB-1, Dako) and p53 (clone DO-7, Dako) was conducted following standardized protocols. All slides were independently reviewed by a board-certified neuropathologist blinded to clinical and imaging data. Ki-67 labeling index: Defined as the percentage of tumor nuclei showing positive staining across five high-power fields (HPF, ×400). Following established grading criteria [26, 27] , specimens were dichotomized as: (1) Low proliferation: Ki-67 <5%; (2) High proliferation: Ki-67 ≥5%. 

p53 status: Interpreted using a combined staining intensity/distribution approach [22] :(1) Negative: Wild-type pattern (heterogeneous weak staining) or nuclear positivity <5%; (2) Positive: Null phenotype (complete absence of staining) or nuclear positivity ≥5% (indicative of TP53 mutation).

2.5 Tumor Segmentation and Spatial Habitat Analysis 

The workflow of this study, including image preprocessing, tumor segmentation, feature extraction and selection, model building, and model comparative evaluation (Figure 2). Manual whole-tumor segmentation was performed slice by slice on the original contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images before intensity normalization and spatial resampling. This approach allowed the radiologists to delineate the tumor boundaries using the original clinical images without potential alterations introduced by preprocessing. Two radiologists independently delineated the whole-tumor volume of interest (VOI) slice by slice. Disagreements between the two raters were resolved by discussion, and unresolved discrepancies were adjudicated by a senior neuroradiologist with 30 years of experience. After the whole-tumor regions of interest had been delineated, the CE-T1WI images and their corresponding segmentation masks were mapped to the same standardized image space for subsequent spatial habitat analysis and radiomic feature extraction. Specifically, the images underwent Z-score intensity normalization and were resampled to isotropic 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ voxels using third-order B-spline interpolation. Inter-observer reproducibility of whole-tumor segmentation was assessed in a randomly selected 40-patient validation cohort. 2.6 Feature Extraction and Selection 

Firstly, before feature extraction, all images underwent Z-score normalization and were resampled to isotropic 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ voxels using third-order B-spline interpolation. Radiomic features were subsequently extracted using PyRadiomics (version 3.0.1). The main extraction parameters include a fixed bin width of 25, a voxel array shift of 1,000, isotropic resampling to 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ using B-spline interpolation. The enabled image types included Original, Wavelet, Exponential, Gradient, LBP2D, LBP3D, Logarithm, Square, SquareRoot, and LoG filters, with LoG sigma values ranging from 1.0 to 5.0. All first-order, shape, GLCM, GLDM, GLRLM, GLSZM, and NGTDM features were enabled. A total of 2,153 radiomic features were initially extracted from each ROI. Importantly, the same procedure (image preprocessing, gray-level discretization, filtering, and feature-extraction) was applied to the three habitat ROIs and the whole-tumor ROI. All feature-reduction procedures were performed using the training cohort only, and the test cohort was not involved in feature selection, feature scaling, or model fitting. Secondly, interobserver reproducibility was evaluated in a randomly selected subset of 40 patients independently segmented on CE-T1WI by two radiologists. For each set of whole-tumor masks, SLIC-based habitat partitioning and radiomic feature extraction were repeated independently. Feature-level ICCs were 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS calculated using a two-way random-effects, absolute-agreement, single-measure model. Features with ICC < 0.90 were excluded. Among the 2,153 radiomic features initially extracted from each ROI, 899, 701, 935, and 946 features were excluded from the whole-tumor ROI, hypo-enhancing habitat, intermediate-enhancing habitat, and hyper-enhancing habitat, respectively. Accordingly, 1,254, 1,452, 1,218, and 1,207 reproducible features were retained for subsequent analysis. The per-feature ICC distributions for each ROI are summarized in Supplementary Figure S1. Thirdly, pairwise Pearson correlation analysis was performed among the remaining features within the training cohort. When two features showed an absolute correlation coefficient greater than 0.90, one feature was removed to reduce redundancy. The retained features were then standardized using parameters estimated from the training cohort and entered into least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression for final feature selection. This procedure was conducted separately for the Ki-67 and p53 prediction tasks within each ROI. Radiomic features associated with both Ki-67 and p53 were subsequently regarded as biologically anchored candidate features for WHO-grade prediction. For clinical semantic features, univariate logistic regression (LR) was used to screen out the features with statistical significance, retaining those with a p-value of < 0.05 for use in the final model. 

2.7 Prediction Model Development and Evaluation 

2.7.1 The construction of the Ki-67 and the p53 status prediction models: Firstly, the significant clinical-radiological features related to the Ki-67 and p53 were screened out respectively using univariate analysis. Subsequently, four prediction models for each biological anchor (Ki-67 and p53) were developed using radiomic features selected from three distinct subregions and the whole-tumor region, respectively. Based on the hypothesis that imaging biomarkers demonstrating dual associations with both Ki-67 proliferation index and p53 mutation status would exhibit biological relevance for tumor grading, we strategically integrated the overlapped radiomic features with clinical-radiological parameters associated with both Ki-67 and p53 for WHO grading prediction of meningiomas. This integrative analytical framework resulted in robust predictive models, with emphasis on biomarker validity and enhanced clinical interpretability via integration of radiomic-pathological correlations. 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS 2.7.2 The construction of the WHO grading prediction models: Three distinct predictive models were systematically developed and evaluated: (1) Clinical-radiological model (C-model): Significant clinical-radiological features both associated with Ki-67 and p53 status were subsequently integrated using LR to establish a baseline predictive framework. (2) Subregion-based combined radiomics model (CRS-model): The s patial habitat radiomics 

framework was developed through multivariable LR analysis, combining the overlapped biomarker related radiomic features from three distinct subregions with C-model predictors to construct a classification model with enhanced biological plausibility. (3) Whole-tumor combined radiomics model (CRW-model): Using identical feature selection criteria and modeling methodology as the CRS-model, this comparator model was developed using radiomic features extracted from whole-tumor region rather than subregional partitions. All model performance was quantified using area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. The Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) framework [33] was employed for post hoc interpretation of feature contributions in the final CRS-model. Specifically, SHAP analysis was applied to the final CRS-model using SHAP LinearExplainer in the Python shap package. SHAP values were calculated for the 120 patients in the independent test cohort, with 280 training samples used as the background dataset. Using the independent test cohort for SHAP analysis allowed us to characterize the model’s decision patterns in previously unseen cases rather than merely explaining its behavior in the training data. The CRS-model maintained acceptable discriminative performance in the test cohort, supporting the relevance of the reported explanations to unseen data. Because the study used a predefined training–test split rather than k-fold cross-validation, fold-wise SHAP stability analysis was not applicable. For visualization, the SHAP summary plot was limited to the top 20 features ranked by mean absolute SHAP value to improve readability. 

2.8 Statistical Analysis 

Data analysis was performed using SPSS (v27, IBM) and R (v4.2.2). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, with normality assessed via Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests. Group comparisons utilized independent t-tests (normal distribution) or Mann-Whitney U-tests (non-normal distribution). 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Categorical variables were analyzed using χ2 or Fisher's exact tests. Model discrimination was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, and the AUC was calculated for each model in the training and test cohorts. To visualize the uncertainty of ROC curves, pointwise 95% confidence bands for sensitivity were generated at fixed specificity values using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. Pairwise comparisons of AUCs among the C-model, CRW-model, and CRS-model were performed using DeLong’s test for correlated ROC curves. Bonferroni correction was applied for multiple comparisons, with the adjusted p values calculated as the raw p values multiplied by 3 within each cohort. Considering the exploratory nature of this radiomics study, Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR) correction was additionally performed as a sensitivity analysis. To provide a more complete evaluation of model performance, precision-recall (PR) curves were additionally generated in the test cohort, and the area under the PR curve (AUPRC) was calculated using average precision. Model calibration was assessed using calibration plots based on deciles of predicted probabilities with the Brier score reported. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was performed using the “rmda” package in R under a cohort design to evaluate the clinical utility of each model. Net benefit was calculated across threshold probabilities ranging from 0.00 to 1.00 with an increment of 0.01. Uncertainty in DCA was estimated using 1000 bootstrap resamples. For clarity, bootstrap confidence intervals were not displayed in the final plots. All tests were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The CRS-model achieved the highest AUPRC among the three models (0.669), compared with the C-model (0.566) and CRW-model (0.552). Calibration analysis showed that the CRS-model had the lowest Brier score in the test cohort (0.206), compared with the C-model (0.235) and CRW-model (0.238), suggesting relatively better probabilistic performance. SHAP analysis of the final CRS-model in the test cohort identified the top 20 features contributing to WHO grading prediction (Figure 5). The most influential features were predominantly habitat-derived radiomic

These findings suggest that texture heterogeneity within the intermediate-enhancing habitat contributed substantially to model prediction. Clinically interpretable variables, including the largest tumor diameter, peritumoral edema, and tumor location, were also among the important contributors. 

Discussion 

In this study, we developed a Ki-67/p53-associated spatial habitat radiomics framework for preoperative WHO grading of meningiomas. The CRS-model achieved the highest AUCs among the three models in both the training and test cohorts. Pairwise DeLong tests confirmed its significant improvement over the CRW-model and C-model in the training cohort after Bonferroni correction. In the test cohort, the CRS-model also showed higher AUCs than the comparator models. These differences did not remain statistically significant after the conservative Bonferroni correction, but remained significant after BH-FDR correction as an exploratory sensitivity analysis. Therefore, the test-cohort advantage of the CRS-model are supportive rather than conclusive, and underscore the critical need for prospective external validation to confirm the clinical utility of this framework. Our findings are consistent with recent studies supporting the value of MRI-based radiomics in meningioma grading and risk stratification [19-22] . Compared with these studies, the principal novelty of our work lies not merely in constructing a grading classifier, but in integrating spatial habitat partitioning with dual biological anchors (Ki-67 and p53). Rather than relying solely on grade-driven feature selection, we intentionally selected radiomic features based on their associations with both proliferation and tumor-suppressor dysregulation, aiming to enhance biological interpretability —a dimension often lacking in purely data ￿driven radiomics models. The incorporation of Ki-67 and p53 as biological anchors represents a strategic advancement over conventional radiomics. This aligns with emerging evidence that angiogenesis-heterogeneity drives meningioma progression [34, 35] , where Ki-67 reflects proliferative activity while p53 abnormality is associated with aggressive tumor biology in a subset of meningiomas [10, 11] . In our analysis, the hyper-enhancing subregion was strongly associated with Ki-67 status, likely reflecting regions of active angiogenesis and vascular permeability . 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Conversely, the hypo-enhancing subregion showed a stronger association with p53 status in the training cohort, potentially corresponding to hypoxic or necrotic niches where TP53 mutations confer a survival advantage under stress. These findings support the hypothesis that different enhancement-defined habitats may capture different biological aspects of tumor aggressiveness. Nonetheless, these associations remain statistical hypotheses awaiting prospective validation via direct MR-immunohistochemistry maps co-registration. In addition, although cultured high-grade meningioma cells are useful for mechanistic studies [36] , current in vitro models may not fully reproduce the spatial vascular and hypoxic heterogeneity observed in vivo [17] .Notably, intermediate angiogenic subregion 2 emerged as the most influential predictor, contributing three of the top five discriminative features. These high-ranking texture-based features may reflect spatial irregularity in enhancement and microvascular organization, potentially because subregion 2 serves as an interface zone between hypo-and hyper-enhancing habitats, where enhancement heterogeneity and vascular architecture are more pronounced. This " interface zone" phenomenon mirrors Bailo's observation in glioma ecosystems [30] ,where interface regions between habitats exhibit amplified genomic information, although research on meningiomas is still lacking. Collectively, these findings suggest that future studies should prioritize the clinical significance of such interface zones. The integration of clinical-radiological features further improved biological interpretability. Tumor diameter, peritumoral edema, tumor location, and cystic or necrotic components are established imaging features associated with aggressive meningioma behavior. Their interaction with habitat-specific radiomics suggests that macroscopic radiological signs and microscopic texture patterns may reflect different but complementary levels of tumor biology. For example, peritumoral edema may be related to vascular permeability, pial blood supply, or secreted angiogenic factors, while habitat radiomics may further characterize intratumoral spatial heterogeneity. This combination may partly explain the improved discrimination of the CRS-model in the training cohort and its numerically better performance in the test cohort compared with models based on either clinical-radiological features or whole-tumor radiomics alone. Several limitations warrant discussion. Firstly, this retrospective single-center study lacked external validation. MRI scans were acquired on eight different scanners, and although normalization and ICC-based filtering were applied, residual scanner-related variability may affect feature stability. Therefore, prospective 

ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS multi-center validation with harmonized MRI protocols is needed before clinical translation. Secondly, the biological interpretation of habitat-specific radiomic features remains inferential. The proposed links between enhancement-defined habitats and Ki-67/p53-related tumor biology [37] were supported by prior evidence and our modeling results. Future studies should combine MRI habitat mapping with spatially resolved immunohistochemistry or radiologic-pathologic co-registration. Thirdly, WHO grade II and III tumors were combined as “high-grade ” due to the rarity of grade III (only 15 cases), precluding separate evaluation of this most aggressive subgroup. Larger multi-center series will be required to enable separate assessment of grade II versus grade III tumors. Finally, the present habitat analysis was based on CE-T1WI alone. Future studies incorporating diffusion- and perfusion-weighted imaging may better characterize the continuum of cellularity, vascularity, hypoxia, and proliferative activity in meningiomas [38-40] .

Conclusion 

Spatial habitat radiomics may help characterize Ki-67/p53-associated vascular heterogeneity and provide a biologically interpretable framework for preoperative WHO grading of meningiomas. By linking habitat-specific imaging phenotypes with biological markers of proliferation and tumor aggressiveness, this approach may represent a step toward pathophysiology-informed radiomics. Further prospective multi-center validation and spatially matched histopathological confirmation are required before clinical translation.
