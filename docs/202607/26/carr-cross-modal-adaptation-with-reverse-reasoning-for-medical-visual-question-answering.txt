Title: CARR: Cross-Modal Adaptation with Reverse Reasoning for Medical Visual Question Answering

URL Source: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-92-3378-6_15

Published Time: 2027

Markdown Content:
## Abstract

Medical Visual Question Answering (MedVQA) aims to answer clinically relevant questions from medical images, yet existing methods still face two major challenges: early cross-modal interaction may obscure fine-grained pathological cues, and conventional one-way prediction lacks explicit reasoning consistency. To address these issues, we propose Cross-modal Adaptation with Reverse Reasoning (CARR), a MedVQA framework that improves multimodal fusion through delayed alignment and reverse reasoning. First, the Dynamic Alignment Adapter (DAA) decouples early unimodal feature extraction from later high-level semantic interaction by restricting cross-modal adaptation to upper transformer layers, thereby reducing shallow over-alignment while preserving subtle diagnostic textures. Second, the Attribute-Aware Reverse Reasoning (A2R2) module introduces weak clinical prior anchoring together with a backward question-intent reconstruction objective. By encouraging the model to recover question intent from visual evidence and predicted answers during training, A2R2 strengthens bidirectional semantic consistency and mitigates reasoning deviation in complex diagnostic scenarios. Extensive experiments on three public benchmarks, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and VQA-2019, show that CARR consistently outperforms baselines and achieves state-of-the-art overall accuracy.
